The Anchor · BTC $75,496 — When the Stock Market Recovers and Crypto Doesn’t

The Nasdaq rose 1.9% on Friday. Bitcoin fell 3.1%. That divergence is the most important data point in Friday’s crypto session — not the price, not the liquidations, but the fact that a strong jobs report and dip-buyer recovery in equities produced zero floor in crypto. The reason is structural: Thursday’s $140 billion selloff was partly hedging and partly position unwinding, and the position unwinding is the component that the jobs report cannot stop. The crypto positions being unwound were built on the assumption that the Democratic sweep would be unambiguously positive for crypto regulation — an assumption that was revised on Thursday and is still being revised on Friday. Stocks have a macroeconomic floor; crypto’s floor requires a regulatory signal from the new Democratic congressional leadership about crypto policy, a signal that will not arrive until the new Congress convenes in January.

Bitcoin Quantum Landmines Proposal Cory Fields Core Developer Increase Financial Cost Quantum Attack Steal Bitcoin Private Keys P2PK Addresses BIP Review Community Consensus November 6 2026 Quantum Landmines · Cory Fields · BTC Security Security (Fri Nov 6 2026): Cory Fields → Core developer → Quantum Landmines → on-chain → economic penalty → quantum attack → P2PK → exposed public keys → legacy addresses → BIP → review → community → consensus → required

Bitcoin Core developer Cory Fields formally proposed a “Quantum Landmines” mechanism to significantly increase the financial cost of stealing Bitcoin should future quantum computing advances eventually break the elliptic-curve cryptography securing Bitcoin private keys — a defensive mechanism designed to make the economic cost of a quantum attack prohibitive rather than merely technically difficult; the proposal targets approximately 4–6 million BTC held in legacy pay-to-public-key (P2PK) addresses where the public key is permanently exposed on-chain and directly vulnerable to a sufficiently capable quantum computer; Quantum Landmines would create on-chain economic penalties for anyone attempting to sweep vulnerable old-format addresses, raising the attack cost by orders of magnitude above the hardware and energy cost of the quantum computation alone; the proposal is in early-discussion phase and requires Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) review and community consensus before any implementation.

Quantum computing timelines remain contested — most estimates put cryptographically relevant systems at 2030–2040 — but Bitcoin’s governance process moves slowly enough that community discussion needs to begin years before the threat becomes operational. Fields’ proposal is a defensive measure that only costs if implemented incorrectly; not implementing it costs everything if the quantum timeline is shorter than expected.
