August 10 marks the start of the Senate state work period — the final deadline for a CLARITY Act floor vote before the legislation waits until mid-September — with the ethics language dispute still unresolved three days after Senator Warren issued a statement opposing the "for compensation" qualifier in the new draft; Bitcoin held at $64,903.30 (▼1.90%) and Ethereum at $1,882.42 (▼2.50%) as of Saturday; Friday's Brent crude retreat to $96.78 (▼3.89%) partially eased the oil-rates risk-off pressure that has governed crypto since the Iran conflict began — with the UST 10-year yield declining 4 basis points to 4.68% — though both rates and risk remain elevated relative to the pre-conflict baseline; and the GENIUS Act advancing on a parallel stablecoin legislative track preserves the ETH settlement-layer thesis regardless of CLARITY's August timeline.

The August 10 deadline is the most concrete constraint the CLARITY Act has faced since the Senate Banking Committee's 15-9 vote in May. Prior deadlines — the June floor schedule, the July window, the August 7 recess date — all slipped as language disputes continued. August 10 is different because it is not a scheduled floor date that can be moved; it is the start of the state work period, after which senators return to their states and the Senate schedule cannot hold CLARITY without extraordinary scheduling. A miss at August 10 means mid-September at earliest, which extends the legislative uncertainty into a period when the September FOMC decision, the Iran nuclear deadline, and the midterm campaign season will all compete for Senate attention. The language gap — whether "for compensation" adequately covers the economic interests that Democrats demanded ethics rules address — is narrow enough to resolve with redrafted text. The question is whether the political will to resolve it exists before the clock runs out. Institutional Bitcoin ETF inflows continuing through the price decline suggest the market's answer is yes — or at least that the downside of being wrong is manageable.

The August 10 deadline and the August 17 nuclear deadline are the two clocks running simultaneously. Both are hard endpoints. CLARITY at August 10 or mid-September; Iran nuclear compliance at August 17 or accelerating proliferation risk. For BTC, both clocks matter: CLARITY passage drives the positive catalyst; nuclear resolution drives the risk-off pressure reduction. The scenario where both resolve favorably before August 17 is the scenario where BTC's thin supply becomes a dramatic price story.

The ethics language gap is narrower than Thursday's Warren statement made it appear. "For compensation" covers active issuance and promotion of digital assets — preventing a President from launching another TRUMP coin for profit. What it does not cover is maintaining a beneficial interest in a crypto fund or advisory stake in a crypto company that profits from favorable legislation. Democrats want coverage of those indirect interests; Republicans who accepted the ethics principle in general want to preserve passive investment rights. The gap between them is: can an official hold a crypto ETF, or only hold coins directly? That is a drafting question, not a policy disagreement — and it is resolvable. The question is whether Senate staff resolve it before the clock runs out.

Institutional ETF inflows continuing through $64,903 BTC is the behavioral signal that the market prices CLARITY's language dispute as temporary, not structural. The same investors who drove BTC to $66K+ when the CLARITY ethics agreement was first announced have not reversed their accumulation — they have continued buying into the language fight. That behavioral pattern implies: the institutional thesis is intact, the language dispute is viewed as a drafting delay, and the August 10 deadline is viewed as pressuring resolution rather than guaranteeing failure.

August 10 marks the start of the Senate state work period — the final deadline for a CLARITY Act floor vote before the legislation waits until mid-September — as the ethics language dispute remains unresolved three days after Senator Warren opposed the "for compensation" qualifier in the new draft, and three separate disputes continue to block the 60-vote cloture threshold. The Bitcoin Foundation has called August 10 "crypto's biggest deadline in 2026." The Motley Fool pegged full-year passage odds at 50/50 as of July 6. Remaining floor windows are the week of July 28, August 3–7, and the August 10 cutoff.

The "for compensation" qualifier is the residual language dispute after the first-order ethics acceptance. It covers officials who actively launch, promote, or issue digital assets for payment — think a Presidential memecoin sold to supporters. It does not straightforwardly cover a President holding a beneficial interest in a crypto hedge fund, an advisory stake in a blockchain company, or a portfolio of digital assets acquired before taking office. Democrats who demanded broad economic-interest coverage argue that gap is large enough to render the ethics provision symbolic. Republicans who accepted ethics coverage in principle argue that "for compensation" is the correct scope — officials should be able to hold investments, not launch products. That gap is drafting, not politics — and it is what Senate staff need to resolve before August 10.

The GENIUS Act — covering stablecoin issuance, reserve requirements, and banking oversight — is advancing on a parallel legislative track to CLARITY, preserving the stablecoin regulation story for Ethereum regardless of whether CLARITY achieves a floor vote before August 10. The stablecoin yield clause, allowing ETH holders to earn protocol yields under the regulatory framework, remains intact in both drafts. The CLARITY Act, if passed, would sort digital assets into three regulatory buckets: digital commodities like Bitcoin and Ethereum under CFTC oversight, fundraising tokens under SEC regulation, and payment stablecoins under banking regulators. XRP and Dogecoin have held through the current uncertainty alongside BTC and ETH, as the broader CLARITY framework is expected to clarify the regulatory status of all major tokens.

The GENIUS-CLARITY parallel track is the ETH investor's legislative hedge. CLARITY is the market structure bill that defines the full regulatory framework; GENIUS is the stablecoin-specific bill that advances the single largest ETH use case — settlement and yield generation for stablecoin products. USDC and Tether alone represent over $180 billion in stablecoin market cap, almost all of which settles on Ethereum. A GENIUS Act that defines reserve requirements, yield rights, and banking oversight for those products advances the ETH settlement thesis more directly than CLARITY's broader token classification. If CLARITY misses August 10, ETH investors are not without a positive catalyst — they are waiting on GENIUS alone. CLARITY passage on top of GENIUS would be the compound positive that drives the $200K BTC thesis.
