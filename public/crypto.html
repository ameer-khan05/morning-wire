The CLARITY Act's failure — if August 7 passes without a floor vote — would not be a policy rejection but a scheduling casualty: the bill has bipartisan support on its core provisions (SEC/CFTC jurisdiction, DeFi framework, developer protections) and was defeated not by opposition but by the inability of Senate leadership to prioritize its floor time over competing legislation in the final days before recess. For crypto markets, the distinction matters less than the outcome: the regulatory certainty that institutional allocators have been awaiting before deploying at scale remains absent, and the next window for CLARITY-equivalent legislation is 2027 at the earliest.

CLARITY Act status: Polymarket probability for passage in 2026 has fallen to 28% — down 38 percentage points from earlier levels — as Thursday arrives with the Senate's schedule showing no floor vote for the crypto bill. August 7 is the last working day before the state work period begins; if no vote occurs on Thursday or Friday, the bill dies with this congressional session and the entire legislative process must restart in the next Congress. The collapse is not ideological — the bill retains bipartisan support on SEC/CFTC jurisdiction, DeFi framework, and developer protections — but scheduling: Senate leadership's competing priorities (appropriations, the Iran-adjacent AUMF debate, and confirmation backlog) have crowded the floor calendar. The yield-rights provision and the ethics rules (specifically targeting public officials holding crypto assets, tied to Trump's WLFI position) remain unresolved and have given senators opposing the bill a principled procedural justification for delay. What CLARITY failure means: (1) SEC/CFTC jurisdiction ambiguity continues — the regulatory uncertainty that has depressed institutional crypto adoption persists; (2) DeFi framework does not exist — decentralized finance platforms continue operating in legal gray zones; (3) Stablecoin yield rights not codified — $317B stablecoin market remains yield-free; (4) ETH settlement layer thesis: partially preserved through GENIUS, not fully realized; (5) Legislative restart: earliest 2027, with no guarantee the 2027 Congress is more crypto-friendly than the current one. BTC resilience: institutional spot ETF inflows have been consistently positive throughout the week — suggesting that the institutional buyers who entered via ETF are treating CLARITY as a bonus catalyst rather than a prerequisite, because their regulatory framework for ETF ownership already exists. ETH's relative preservation: if GENIUS passes without CLARITY, ETH benefits from regulated stablecoin settlement activity without the yield-rights provision that would have made the economic benefit most concentrated. The CLARITY Act's effective death — if August 7 passes without action — is the most significant crypto legislative event since the spot Bitcoin ETF approval in January 2024, but in the negative direction. The ETF approval created regulatory clarity for BTC ownership; CLARITY's failure perpetuates regulatory ambiguity for DeFi, stablecoin yield rights, and the SEC/CFTC jurisdictional question that has produced years of inconsistent enforcement. The market's measured response — BTC holding above $64,000, not crashing — reflects two things: institutional ETF inflows as a demand floor, and the recognition that CLARITY's failure, while significant, is a scheduling failure that can be reversed in 2027, not a policy defeat that closes the legislative pathway permanently. The September rate-cut probability thesis, sustained by WTI at $74.79 and Friday's Jobs Report ahead, is the macro tailwind that is preventing the CLARITY news from producing a larger negative price reaction. If both catalysts disappoint — Jobs Report strong, CLARITY dead — the macro and regulatory configuration for BTC would be simultaneously unfavorable for the first time this week.

The GENIUS stablecoin bill's independent advancement provides ETH's most important legislative floor: even without CLARITY, GENIUS would establish reserve requirements and issuance standards for the $317 billion stablecoin market — activity that runs predominantly through Ethereum's settlement layer. GENIUS's passage without CLARITY means ETH retains a regulatory pathway for stablecoin settlement without the yield-rights provision that would have generated the strongest settlement-layer economics; the floor is preserved, but the ceiling is lower than the CLARITY-plus-GENIUS scenario.

The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act's effective death entered its final confirmation Thursday: Polymarket probability for 2026 passage fell to 28% — down 38 percentage points — as August 7 arrived without a Senate floor vote scheduled, leaving the bill's survival dependent on Senate leadership scheduling and completing a vote within the final 24 hours of the legislative week; the bill's collapse is not a policy rejection — the provisions with bipartisan agreement (SEC/CFTC jurisdiction, DeFi trading framework, developer liability protections) enjoy majority support — but a scheduling failure in which the unresolved yield-rights and ethics-rules disputes have given senators procedural justification for delay that was always available but only became lethal when the legislative clock expired. The yield-rights provision, which would allow stablecoin holders to earn interest on their holdings, faces opposition from the banking lobby because a regulated yield-bearing stablecoin would compete directly with bank deposits — a concern that is economically legitimate but represents a policy choice about whether to prioritize financial system stability or crypto market development.

For crypto markets, the practical scenario analysis that was previously a binary (CLARITY passes or fails) has now effectively resolved to the failure scenario: if Polymarket is pricing 28% odds and August 7 is the last working day with no vote scheduled, the market is treating the legislative outcome as determined. The remaining 28% reflects tail probability of emergency Senate floor action on Thursday or Friday — possible in theory if the Iran deal's finalization creates an unexpected window of political goodwill toward the crypto-industry political coalition, but not something markets are pricing as likely. The 2027 restart scenario resets the clock to the beginning: new Congress, new committee assignments, new sponsor negotiations, and the uncertain political configuration that follows the 2026 midterms.
