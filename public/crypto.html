The Anchor · CLARITY at 24–26% — What the Single-Session Odds Jump Means for Tomorrow’s Vote

The jump in CLARITY Act passage odds from Galaxy’s 10% and Polymarket’s 16% on Sunday to DeFiRate’s 24–26% on Monday is the bill’s largest single-session shift in recent weeks and the most significant change in the legislative picture entering the cloture vote. The three unresolved disputes that blocked Democratic crossovers through the weekend — ethics provisions targeting Trump’s crypto income, DeFi developer liability under Section 604, and Coinbase’s $1.35 billion annual USDC yield threatened by the stablecoin provision — produced no announced resolution Sunday. The odds jump therefore reflects either a changed whip count based on undisclosed dealmaking, a softer read on undecided senators, or a reassessment of the bill’s procedural path that the public record does not yet explain. Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong’s public declaration that the bill will pass is an unusual intervention at this stage: exchange CEOs do not typically comment on active Senate procedural votes, and Armstrong’s statement — either a genuine signal of insider whip-count confidence or a public pressure campaign designed to move undecided senators — has the practical effect of raising the cost of a no vote for senators on the fence. If Armstrong knows something the prediction markets don’t, the 24–26% reading is still understating the probability. If he doesn’t, his statement is the last piece of public lobbying before the vote closes. The 60-vote threshold for cloture requires seven Democratic crossovers from a caucus that has been split by the three unresolved disputes. The ethics provisions targeting Trump’s specific crypto income stream are the most politically charged blocker — Democratic senators who support crypto regulation in principle have used Trump’s personal conflict of interest as cover to withhold support, and that cover only comes off if the ethics provisions are strengthened, not if they are removed. Whether the jump to 24–26% reflects progress on that specific dispute is the question the vote will answer. BTC at $76,838 — stable through a session that took Brent to $108.42 and the 10Y to 5% — says the market is not pricing a CLARITY surprise in either direction; it is holding its position and letting the vote happen. The Strive ₿25,000 milestone represents the same posture at the institutional level: accumulation through the uncertainty window, not through the resolution. Both say the same thing: the medium-term thesis on BTC does not depend on CLARITY passing tomorrow, but passing would accelerate it.
