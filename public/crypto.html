Senate Majority Leader Thune signaled Tuesday that CLARITY Act passage before the August 10 hard cutoff is not guaranteed, with no cloture motion filed despite the White House ethics agreement Sunday that resolved the final drafting impasse — the first public acknowledgment from Republican Senate leadership that the bill may miss its 2026 legislative window and be pushed to a mid-September timeline; Bitcoin held at $65,062 (▲0.33%) and Ethereum at $1,944 (▲1.62%), with both holding their Sunday night gains from the White House ethics agreement but losing the further momentum that a floor vote announcement would have generated, as the market reprices the August 10 scenario from near-certain to contested. The FOMC opened Day 1 of its July 28–29 meeting Tuesday, with a hawkish hold broadly expected Wednesday — Brent at $85.87 gives Powell the most defensible "supply shock assessed" framing of the conflict period, but the Wednesday rate language on September probability is the variable that determines BTC's non-yielding-asset attractiveness entering a potential CLARITY delay.

Thune's Tuesday signal introduces the scenario that Bessent's "1-yard line" framing on July 21 and Sunday's White House ethics agreement had led markets to discount: that the legislative machinery may not produce a floor vote in the August 7–10 window even with the drafting question resolved. The precondition (ethics language) is met. The cloture coalition (60 votes) was achievable with the ethics agreement. What Thune's signal suggests is that scheduling floor time in the remaining days — between Tuesday July 28 and Friday August 7 — faces competition from other Senate priorities, and that leadership is not committed to clearing that calendar. For BTC and ETH, a mid-September CLARITY vote changes the institutional custody and position-building timeline by six weeks, deferring the supply-squeeze dynamics that the Standard Chartered $100K scenario depends on.

Thune's signal is the most significant CLARITY development since the White House ethics agreement, because it introduces the scenario the bill's optimists had fully discounted: that the calendar, not the language, kills the August window. The drafting question is resolved. The 60-vote question is answered in principle. The scheduling question — does Senate leadership commit to floor time before August 7 — is not answered, and Thune's Tuesday signal suggests the answer may be no. Mid-September is not a catastrophic outcome for CLARITY: the bill's 2026 passage probability remains above 70% across most analyst forecasts regardless of August vs September timing. But the timing matters for the supply dynamics above $70K that the $100K scenario requires — six weeks of delay is six weeks of reduced institutional position-building, which is the mechanism through which CLARITY passage produces price appreciation above the current $65K level.

The CLARITY Act's legislative arithmetic has not changed with Thune's signal — the votes are there if leadership schedules a floor vote. What has changed is the probability assigned to leadership making that scheduling decision in the next ten days. Bessent's "1-yard line" framing implied White House pressure was mobilizing Senate action. The White House ethics agreement delivered the drafting resolution. Thune's signal says the White House's resolution of the drafting question has not (yet) produced a Senate leadership commitment to floor time. That gap — between a resolved precondition and a committed scheduling decision — is where August 10 passage can fail even with 60 votes nominally available.

The FOMC dimension is not secondary. A Wednesday rate decision that signals a September hold — made more probable by Brent at $85.87 and the supply-shock-assessed framing — compresses the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding BTC in a way that is independent of CLARITY timing. Treasury yield compression from oil-normalization has been a background support for BTC since Sunday's Brent decline began. If Powell's Wednesday press conference language reinforces that the September hold is the base case, BTC gets a rate-channel tailwind that partially offsets the CLARITY-delay headwind. ETH benefits additionally from the GENIUS stablecoin track's advancing independently — the settlement-layer thesis does not require CLARITY to progress.

Quick hits

  • The distinction between CLARITY "not guaranteed" and CLARITY "failed" is the market's interpretive challenge this week. Thune's signal is probabilistic uncertainty, not a scheduling decision that forecloses August. Senate leadership can file cloture between now and August 7 even after signaling uncertainty Tuesday — the uncertainty signal reflects where scheduling discussions stand today, not an irrevocable decision about the calendar. Institutional participants who track Senate schedules closely will know before retail markets whether cloture was filed; the ETF inflow data from Wednesday and Thursday will be the first available proxy for whether institutional positioning is accelerating (passage confidence rising) or plateauing (delay being priced). The 5-day $727M streak from last week is the baseline to watch against.
  • The FOMC-CLARITY interaction on Wednesday is unusual for crypto markets. Normally the FOMC is a macro input to BTC through the rates channel and CLARITY is a regulatory input through the adoption channel — the two are independent variables. This week they are correlated: if CLARITY misses August 10, the mid-September window coincides with the September 16 FOMC meeting, meaning the next dual crypto catalyst (CLARITY passage + September FOMC hold) would arrive at the same time in mid-September that this week's dual catalyst (CLARITY passage + July hold) was hoped for in August. A six-week delay doesn't change the catalyst structure — it delays it by one Fed cycle.
