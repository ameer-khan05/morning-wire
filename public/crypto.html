The Anchor · The Thanksgiving Test — What Bitcoin Does When the Nasdaq Is Closed Reveals Whether the Equity Correlation Is the Price or the Price Is the Price

Wednesday night’s question for Bitcoin was simple: what does the asset do when equities close for a day? Thursday’s answer is a 4.7% rebound. All week, Bitcoin’s daily direction has been set by equity markets. On Tuesday, the Nasdaq fell on Nvidia’s governance-premium retracement and BTC fell 5.52%. On Wednesday, the Nasdaq fell on PCE inflation data and BTC fell 2.21%. The correlation ratio was consistent: approximately 2:1 on a macro-driven day, 3.5:1 on a chip-specific day. Bitcoin was not trading on any Bitcoin-specific signal in either session. It was amplifying whatever moved equities. With equity markets closed Thursday for Thanksgiving, that amplifier was switched off. Bitcoin rose 4.7%. There are two readings of this result. The optimistic reading: genuine demand for Bitcoin at $58,564 — near a support level — with buyers who had been waiting for equities to stabilize stepping in when the equity-driven selling pressure was removed. The cautious reading: a short-covering rally in a low-liquidity session — with US equity traders largely offline, bid-ask spreads widen, and a relatively small volume of buy orders can produce outsized price moves. Both readings are consistent with the data. Friday’s abbreviated equity session — markets close at 1pm ET — will provide the first test: does BTC hold the $60,734 recovery when the Nasdaq reopens, or does it re-correlate to whatever equities do in a half-day session?

US spot Bitcoin ETF products recorded a sixth consecutive session of net outflows through Wednesday, bringing November’s cumulative net outflow to approximately $4.94 billion — the largest monthly net outflow from the spot BTC ETF products since they launched in January 2024 — with the institutional exit running directly counter to Thursday’s spot-price rebound and reflecting a divergence between retail and institutional positioning that has characterized the week; the persistent ETF outflows during November’s price volatility — which included both the sharp run-up to $63,988 and the subsequent 8.5% decline to $58,564 — suggest institutional holders have been using price strength to reduce exposure rather than adding to positions, with the November outflow pace accelerating after the Nvidia governance retracement on Tuesday triggered broader risk-asset reassessment; and the structural read is straightforward: retail and momentum buyers are defending the spot price while institutional capital — accessible through ETF products — has been reducing net exposure through six consecutive sessions.

Six consecutive outflow sessions without a price collapse is the week’s most informative data point about Bitcoin’s buyer structure. If spot price held near $58,500 while institutional ETF holders reduced exposure for six sessions, it means spot-market buyers — likely retail, momentum funds, and international buyers operating outside the US ETF wrapper — absorbed that institutional selling without capitulating. That is a measure of bid-side depth, not just price level.
