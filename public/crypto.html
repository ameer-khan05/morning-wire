The Anchor · PCE 3.7% Changes the Jackson Hole Setup — BTC at $79K Is Holding the CLARITY Thesis Without Its Secondary Macro Support

Wednesday's edition described Warsh's Friday Jackson Hole setup as "the most favorable macro environment for a September cut signal" he had faced since taking office: lower oil, clean PCE data, rebounding equities, partial Hormuz de-escalation. Thursday's 3.7% PCE headline has changed three of those four conditions. PCE is not clean — it is 140 basis points above consensus and 170 above target. The Hormuz deal is not a clean de-escalation — Iran's IRGC is saying the US is "standing in the way," and Brent has rebounded from $85.91 to $87.65. The equities are up, but driven by Nvidia rather than broad macro improvement. Warsh now faces a Friday keynote in which the inflation data mandates a more hawkish tone than the base-case September cut scenario, while the legislative calendar (19 days to CLARITY cloture) and the September 16 FOMC decision create maximum institutional attention to every word he says. The setup has shifted from "Warsh can credibly signal a September cut with the data behind him" to "Warsh must explain why 3.7% PCE is not an obstacle to a September cut if he still intends to cut." That is a more difficult communication task, and the bond market's move to 4.77% UST 10-year yield is the market's first pricing of the harder scenario. BTC at $79,027 — holding above $79,000 despite the PCE print — is evidence that the CLARITY structural bid is functioning as the primary driver independent of the macro environment. The structural case for why BTC has moved from ~$64,822 (its pre-CLARITY-rally baseline) to $79,027 is the legislative calendar: 19 days to a cloture vote that, if passed, formally defines BTC as a digital commodity under CFTC jurisdiction rather than leaving it in regulatory ambiguity. That legislative catalyst does not depend on whether Warsh cuts 25 basis points in September. It depends on whether the Senate has 60 votes on September 15. The two drivers — CLARITY structural bid and September rate-cut macro tailwind — are now effectively separated: the CLARITY bid can hold BTC at $79,000 even if the September cut is delayed; the rate-cut tailwind would add additional upside above the CLARITY structural level if it materializes. BTC's Thursday resilience in the face of 3.7% PCE and the Hormuz deal complications is the empirical test of that separation — and so far, the CLARITY structural bid is holding without the macro support it had Monday and Tuesday.

Solana's 5.76% Thursday gain deserves context in the CLARITY narrative. The CLARITY Act's primary legislative clarity benefit is for BTC (commodity classification) and ETH (securities-versus-commodity resolution), but the broader regulatory clarity signal it sends also reduces the legal ambiguity for the broader digital asset ecosystem, including Solana, which has faced SEC classification disputes. A 5.76% gain on a day when BTC gained 0.74% suggests Solana-specific capital rotation that may reflect a combination of technical factors, CLARITY-adjacent optimism about broader regulatory clarity, and the DeFi and NFT ecosystem activity on Solana that tracks independently of BTC's regulatory thesis. Monitoring whether Solana sustains this relative outperformance through the CLARITY countdown will be a signal about the breadth of the regulatory-clarity thesis beyond BTC's commodity classification story.

Bitcoin held above $79,000 Thursday — at approximately $79,027, up 0.74% — despite the July PCE headline landing at 3.7% year-over-year (well above the 2.3% consensus) and the Hormuz deal announced Wednesday facing complications from Iranian hardliners. The dual headwinds — hot inflation reducing rate-cut probability and geopolitical bid partial reversal from Hormuz uncertainty — tested the structural CLARITY thesis: BTC at $79,027 represents roughly $14,200 above its pre-CLARITY-rally baseline of approximately $64,822, and Thursday's resilience in the face of PCE and Hormuz complications confirms the CLARITY structural bid is functioning as the primary driver independently of the secondary macro environment. ETH gained 1.29% to approximately $2,583; Solana surged 5.76% in notable outperformance.

The mechanics of why CLARITY drives BTC independently of PCE: the CLARITY Act's September 15 cloture vote is a binary event on the legislative calendar that, if it passes, formally ends the commodity-versus-security ambiguity for BTC under US law. That legal resolution has a value that does not change based on whether the federal funds rate is 4.75% or 5.00% — the value comes from legal certainty for institutions that cannot hold assets with unresolved regulatory classification, and from the CFTC's lighter oversight framework versus the SEC's. The 19-day countdown is generating pre-positioning from institutional investors who want legal-certainty exposure before the vote rather than after, when the market has already priced the passage scenario. That institutional pre-positioning is structural and persistent in a way that day-to-day macro fluctuations cannot easily override — which is what Thursday's PCE test demonstrated.

Jackson Hole opened Thursday with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh facing the most consequential policy communication setup of any major central banker in years: July PCE at 3.7% year-over-year — the highest reading since the Hormuz crisis began — 18 hours before his first keynote as Fed Chair on a "Financial Innovation" theme, with 19 days until the CLARITY Act cloture vote and 20 days until the FOMC rate decision. Axios reported Thursday that Warsh is "under intense pressure to clarify his message" on the inflation outlook, interest rate path, and the Fed-Treasury relationship. The 10-year Treasury yield's move to 4.77% from Wednesday's 4.66% reflects markets pricing a meaningfully reduced probability of a September cut following the 3.7% PCE print.

The crypto market's specific read on Warsh's Friday keynote has three scenarios. In the first, Warsh defends a September cut despite 3.7% PCE by arguing that the energy-price trajectory is improving (Hormuz de-escalation, even if contested) and that the Hormuz-driven energy inflation spike will show reversal in August's data — a forward-looking cut argument that prioritizes the trend over the current reading. That scenario is BTC-positive: it restores the rate-cut tailwind and may push BTC toward Tuesday's $81,023 high or above. In the second, Warsh signals a September hold and cites 3.7% PCE as the explicit reason, removing the rate-cut tailwind entirely. That scenario tests the CLARITY structural bid's ability to hold BTC in the $76,000-$79,000 range without macro support. In the third, Warsh remains deliberately ambiguous, as central bankers often do when the data contradicts the preferred path — which would maintain the current $79,000 range until the September 16 meeting itself. FOMC blackout begins Saturday, so Friday is the only remaining opportunity for Fed clarification before the vote.

The 10-year Treasury yield jumped from 4.66% Wednesday to 4.77% Thursday as markets priced the reduced probability of a September rate cut — the FOMC blackout begins Saturday, meaning Thursday's yield move is one of the last market-rate signals Warsh will see before delivering Friday's keynote. The Hormuz deal complications — with Brent rebounding from $85.91 to $87.65 as Iran's IRGC claimed the US is "standing in the way" — partially restored the energy inflation premium that Wednesday's deal announcement had temporarily reduced.

For the crypto market, the PCE reading and the Hormuz complication are informative precisely because BTC at $79,027 has absorbed both without breaking below the $79,000 level. The empirical question since Tuesday's $81,023 high has been: how much of BTC's rally from $64,822 (pre-CLARITY baseline) to $81,023 (Tuesday high) was CLARITY structural positioning, and how much was the Iran geopolitical overlay and the rate-cut macro tailwind? Thursday's data gives a partial answer: after the Iran-Oman deal partially unwound the geopolitical overlay (Wednesday), and after the PCE reading partially unwound the rate-cut tailwind (Thursday), BTC is at $79,027 — roughly $14,200 above the pre-CLARITY baseline. The combined unwind of the two secondary demand sources has cost roughly $2,000 from Tuesday's $81,023 high, suggesting each secondary source was contributing approximately $1,000-$1,500 to the premium above the pure CLARITY structural level. The CLARITY structural level appears to be approximately $78,000-$79,000 at current institutional pre-positioning volumes.
