The Anchor · Bitcoin’s 12.1% Week — The Rate Environment, The 200-Day, and a Weekend Without a Reversal Catalyst

The conditions that produced Bitcoin’s 12.1% weekly decline have not changed. The 10-year yield is at 5.14%. The December FOMC hike is priced at near-certain odds. The 200-day moving average is broken on a confirmed daily close. The ETF outflow trend is six sessions old. At $66,687, Bitcoin is down 12.1% from last Friday’s $75,837 — a weekly loss larger than any single session loss in the five-day decline. The composition of the week’s selling: Tuesday’s CPI drove December hike odds to 87%. Wednesday’s bond market closure allowed equities to recover while Bitcoin fell independently. Thursday’s bond market reopening at 4.99% added another 15 basis points of yield pressure. Friday’s PPI YoY at 6.5% pushed the 10-year to 5.14% and broke the 200-day moving average. Saturday’s $66,687 is the second close below the 200-day — confirming the technical regime change as a sustained condition, not a single-session breach. The reversal catalysts that could change the direction: a Fed pivot signal, a PPI or CPI print that shows inflation falling sharply, a risk-on sentiment shift. None of those are on the weekend calendar. The Anthropic IPO drawing $386 billion in institutional demand — six times the shares on offer — on the same week that Bitcoin lost $1.56 billion in ETF capital is not a coincidence. It is the most direct measure yet of a capital rotation: institutional investors who entered crypto through regulated ETFs in 2024 are redirecting marginal dollars toward the AI industry’s public market debut, where the risk-adjusted case for $10 billion commitments from Google and Nvidia is clearer than the case for Bitcoin at $66,687 with 5.14% risk-free competition.

Five consecutive days of ETF outflows are a qualitatively different signal from a single bad session. It means that every business day this week, the net flow from regulated institutional Bitcoin investors was negative — more redemptions than purchases, more institutional holders choosing to exit than to hold. At $66,687, Bitcoin is testing whether long-term holders provide a price floor or sell alongside the institutional de-risking.
