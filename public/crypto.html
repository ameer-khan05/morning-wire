The CLARITY Act entered a modified survival status Thursday: Senate Majority Leader John Thune confirmed there will be no August floor vote before the Senate's summer recess — the August 7 deadline that prediction markets had treated as the bill's last chance has passed without action — but Thune simultaneously committed to scheduling a floor vote no later than September 16, giving the bill a defined legislative window that distinguishes this outcome from a full congressional death and pushes the crypto market-structure debate into September as an active catalyst. BTC at $64,835 (▲1.21%) rose Friday morning as July's weak jobs report dramatically increased September rate-cut probability — the macro tailwind that has been crypto's most consistent support mechanism throughout this week's legislative disappointment — and ETH at $1,911 (▲0.62%) added a structural supply development: a public company's disclosure of accumulating over 1 million ETH since early 2026 compresses the liquid supply in a way that provides demand context independent of regulation.

The shift from "CLARITY dead" to "CLARITY delayed to September" changes the legislative scenario analysis materially: the September vote means crypto market-structure legislation can still pass in 2026 if Thune's commitment holds and the unresolved issues (yield rights, ethics rules) can be negotiated before September 15 cloture. But the delay is not costless — every month the regulatory ambiguity persists is a month that institutional allocators who were waiting for CLARITY before deploying at scale continue to wait. The Fear and Greed Index improving from 25 to 29 reflects the market absorbing the legislative delay alongside the rate-cut tailwind.

Character: both assets rising as weak Jobs Report dramatically increases September rate-cut probability, adding the macro tailwind that has cushioned crypto throughout this week's legislative uncertainty. CLARITY Act status update: Thursday's Senate recess departure happened without an August vote, as Polymarket had been pricing (28% odds). However, the outcome is more nuanced than full legislative death: Senate Majority Leader Thune stated Thursday that there would not be a vote in August but that he would schedule one in September — specifically, if he files for cloture before the Senate returns from recess on Monday September 15, the first procedural vote could be held September 15, with the floor vote possible September 16; a later filing would push the September timeline out by a day or more. The shift from "effectively dead" to "delayed to September" matters for scenario analysis: the bill now has a defined survival path. The unresolved issues — stablecoin yield rights (opposed by the banking lobby because regulated yield-bearing stablecoins compete with bank deposits), ethics rules (specifically targeting public officials holding crypto, tied to Trump's WLFI position), and law-enforcement data access provisions — remain the substantive obstacles that prevented an August vote; whether these can be negotiated before September 15 determines whether Thune's commitment translates into an actual bill. Jobs Report (Fri Aug 7): July nonfarm payrolls came in well below the 85,000 consensus — ADP had shown 44,000 private-sector additions, and the ISM Services Employment Index fell to 47.4 (below 50, indicating contraction) — confirming the labor market softening that dovish FOMC members have been flagging. The weak print pushes September rate-cut probability sharply higher. For BTC and ETH, a September cut matters through two mechanisms: it reduces the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding or lower-yielding assets relative to cash, and it improves the broader risk-appetite configuration across all asset classes. Both mechanisms are positive at current prices. BTC at $64,835 reflects this week's balance: a CLARITY legislative setback absorbed by the institutional ETF demand floor, with the macro tailwind from Jobs and oil ($77.18, well below the conflict peak) now strengthening further on the weak payrolls data. ETH at $1,911: the 1M+ ETH accumulation by a public company disclosed this week removes liquid supply in a way that structural market analysis treats as demand — supply that has been purchased and held by a public company is effectively removed from the liquid market for the medium term, tightening the supply-demand balance. Thune's September commitment changes the CLARITY story from "five years of legislative work dies this week" to "vote is delayed to September, bill is alive." For crypto markets, the distinction matters less than the outcome — the regulatory ambiguity that has limited institutional adoption persists regardless of whether CLARITY fails in August or is delayed to September. But for the legislative scenario analysis, "delayed to September" is meaningfully better than "dead": September passage, if Thune's commitment holds and the issues resolve, would make 2026 the year of US crypto market-structure legislation rather than the year it died. The September FOMC's rate cut, if confirmed by the Jobs and oil configuration, provides the macro tailwind that makes September the most favorable macro context for crypto since the conflict began. Both catalysts — CLARITY September vote and September rate cut — are now live for the same month.

The GENIUS stablecoin bill's continued independent advancement provides ETH's regulatory floor regardless of CLARITY's September fate: even if the September CLARITY vote fails, GENIUS's federal framework for the $317 billion stablecoin market — which runs predominantly through Ethereum's settlement layer — provides ongoing regulated demand for ETH as stablecoin settlement activity. GENIUS without CLARITY preserves the settlement-layer thesis at a lower economic intensity than the CLARITY-plus-yield-rights scenario would have provided.

Senate Majority Leader John Thune confirmed Thursday that the CLARITY Act will not receive an August floor vote before the Senate's summer recess — ending the August window that industry negotiators had prioritized — but simultaneously committed to scheduling a September vote, specifically stating that under Senate procedure, a cloture filing before Monday September 15 enables a first procedural vote that day and a floor vote as early as September 16; this commitment distinguishes Thursday's outcome from the full legislative death that Polymarket's 28% odds had implied, converting a feared legislative extinction into a two-month delay with a defined timeline. CoinDesk reported the Senate had "shelved" the bill to focus on a backlog of nominations and foreign-relations priorities — confirming the scheduling failure rather than a policy rejection, as the bill's bipartisan agreement on core provisions (SEC/CFTC jurisdiction, DeFi framework, developer protections) remains intact.

The September scenario's probability depends on resolving the same three issues that blocked August: the yield-rights provision (whether stablecoin holders can earn interest, which banking industry groups oppose because regulated yield-bearing stablecoins would compete with bank deposits), the ethics rules (specifically targeting public officials holding crypto, tied to Trump's World Liberty Financial position), and law-enforcement data access (provisions that crypto-privacy advocates oppose). If Thune's September commitment is genuine and the issues resolve, the crypto market-structure bill that has been building since 2021 could pass in 2026 — making September the most consequential legislative month for digital assets since the spot ETF approval in January 2024.

Quick hits

  • Thune's September commitment warrants scrutiny against Senate calendar reality. The Senate returns from recess on Monday September 15 — Thune would need to file cloture before or on that day for the September 15-16 vote timeline to work. Under current Senate rules, cloture requires 60 votes (three-fifths of the 100-seat Senate) to end debate; the CLARITY Act would need at least seven Democratic crossovers in addition to the Republican caucus majority. The same dynamics that made August scheduling difficult — the yield-rights compromise requiring banking-lobby negotiation, the ethics-rules compromise requiring bipartisan agreement on crypto disclosure for public officials — will need to be resolved during the August recess for September's timeline to hold. Thune's public commitment creates political accountability that August's implicit deadline did not; breaking a public commitment to a September vote would carry more political cost than simply failing to schedule an August vote. That accountability is what makes "delayed to September" more valuable than it might appear from the July prediction-market perspective.
  • The 1M+ ETH accumulation figure warrants grounding in ETH's total supply context. Total ETH supply is approximately 120 million coins; 1 million ETH represents roughly 0.83% of total supply. The liquid-supply fraction that circulates on exchanges at any given moment is substantially smaller — estimates of liquid ETH circulating on centralized exchanges range from 10-15 million ETH. If that estimate is correct, 1 million ETH removed from circulation represents 6-10% of liquid supply — a substantial supply compression relative to the circulating float that prices are actually traded against. The accumulator's identity as a public company means the ETH is held on a disclosed balance sheet, reducing the risk that the accumulation is leveraged or subject to forced selling in a downward market — a structural stability feature that distinguishes public-company accumulation from leveraged-fund positions.
