The Anchor · CLARITY's Math — Why 17% at Polymarket Is the Most Honest Assessment of Where the Bill Stands

Galaxy Research cutting CLARITY's 2026 passage odds from 50% to 30% and Polymarket pricing the bill at 17% are not analyst pessimism or prediction market noise. They are arithmetic. The CLARITY Act needs 60 votes for the September 15 cloture motion to succeed. Republicans hold 53 Senate seats. Seven Democratic crossovers are required. The ethics provision — requiring disclosure and restrictions on Trump administration officials with more than $1 billion in crypto holdings — is the fulcrum: Democratic senators who might otherwise support CLARITY will not vote for cloture without it, and Republicans will not accept language framed around Trump's holdings. The stablecoin yield language adds a second front from the banking sector. Nine days is not enough time to resolve a stalemate that has persisted through August and reached consensus pessimism by the first week of September. The political economy of the ethics provision explains why the deadlock is structural rather than procedurally solvable on a nine-day timeline. Democrats who support the crypto market structure bill in principle face a constituency cost — voting for a bill that would benefit Trump administration officials who profit from the assets being regulated without any conflict-of-interest mitigation. Republicans face a symmetric cost — accepting ethics language that implies Trump administration officials have conflicts that require statutory restriction. Neither cost goes away as the vote approaches; both harden as positions become public. Senate Majority Leader Thune's window to broker the deal that resolves both costs is measured in days, and the bet that he cannot close it in nine days is the bet that Polymarket's 17% reflects. What happens at 17%? The bill fails cloture, does not reach the floor for a vote, and effectively dies for 2026 as the October recess approaches. The crypto market's response to a confirmed CLARITY failure depends on how much of that failure is already priced. At 17%, the market has priced roughly 83% of the failure scenario. The 17% residual — the chance the bill still passes — is the surprise upside that a successful cloture vote would deliver. If the bill fails as expected, the marginal market reaction is limited: the 83% that was already priced absorbs the confirmation. The structural consequence, however, is that the SEC and CFTC rulemaking path becomes the only regulatory infrastructure the crypto market will have until at least 2027. That rulemaking path — the joint initiative on leveraged crypto transactions, the transfer agent overhaul — is moving, is specific, and is not CLARITY. Bitcoin at $79,622 and Ethereum at $2,471 on Sunday reflect a market that has largely priced the CLARITY failure and is now focused on the rate-hike probability as the primary price driver. That probability — approximately 50% per Waller's Friday framing — is what August CPI will resolve on September 10. Tehran's Hormuz diplomatic push is the weekend's wild card: not because it resolves the rate question, but because it introduces a scenario where the energy CPI risk moderates before September 10, changing the probability inputs without changing the data.
