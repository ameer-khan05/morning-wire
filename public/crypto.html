Public Bitcoin miners cut their aggregate hashrate 13.4% as operators repurpose power capacity and data center infrastructure for AI computing revenue — with Riot Platforms' deal with Anthropic as the most direct and largest-scale single transaction expressing the structural reallocation. Riot Platforms operates one of the US's largest Bitcoin mining campuses (Rockdale, Texas, with hundreds of megawatts of power capacity) and is converting a portion of that infrastructure to AI inference workloads for Anthropic, redirecting the same electrical infrastructure and cooling systems from application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) Bitcoin mining to GPU-based AI inference without the capital cost of greenfield data center development. The structural economics of the trade favor both parties at BTC's current level: AI inference revenue is higher-margin and less price-volatile than Bitcoin mining revenue when BTC is trading at $62,907 with a Fear & Greed Index of 29, and for Anthropic, repurposing existing data center electrical infrastructure is faster and cheaper than new construction for inference capacity.

The template for this transaction was established by Core Scientific's deal with CoreWeave — the first major public miner to convert significant mining capacity to AI GPU compute under a multi-year hosting contract. The Core Scientific/CoreWeave model converted mining data center electrical capacity and cooling into GPU inference capacity for CoreWeave's AI cloud customers, generating stable, contracted revenue that was less sensitive to BTC price fluctuations than mining revenue. The Riot/Anthropic deal follows the same structure applied to a frontier AI lab customer rather than an AI cloud provider — making the inference consumer (Anthropic) rather than an intermediary cloud the contracting party. If Anthropic's training and inference compute needs expand as expected, the Riot facility's capacity may represent a multi-year contracted revenue stream that improves Riot's financial stability significantly at current BTC price levels.

The CFTC will hold its Innovation Advisory Committee meeting on August 20 to explore crypto asset regulation, artificial intelligence, and prediction market regulation simultaneously — the first time the agency has addressed all three as a combined topic in a single advisory session. The meeting's combination of topics reflects the regulatory reality that crypto, AI, and prediction markets have converged in 2026: crypto prediction markets are expanding, AI-driven trading systems are operating in crypto markets, and the CLARITY Act's passage (or failure) will determine how much of the DeFi ecosystem falls under CFTC versus SEC jurisdiction. The meeting's findings will inform the regulatory context in which CLARITY's Senate floor debate occurs on September 14, giving the industry a final opportunity to shape agency guidance before the legislation's most important vote.

The CLARITY Act's September 14 floor action timeline remains the crypto market's primary forward-looking catalyst, alongside the September 16 FOMC decision. Thursday's benign PPI (flat month-over-month, core +0.2% — the second consecutive benign inflation print) reinforces the rate-cut narrative that makes the September 16 FOMC the most favorable macro context for CLARITY passage since the bill entered Senate consideration. The nine cloture votes still needed (51 of 60 confirmed) must come from Republican holdouts whose objections — yield rights, Trump WLFI ethics provisions, law enforcement data access — are legislative-bargaining questions that the Senate recess through September 12 leaves open. The CFTC meeting on August 20 cannot resolve those objections directly, but it can narrow the regulatory uncertainty that some holdouts cite as justification for caution on passage.

Quick hits

  • The Coldcard hardware wallet security vulnerability news that emerged alongside Thursday's ETF inflows deserves a brief technical note: Coldcard wallets are air-gapped hardware devices used primarily by self-custody Bitcoin holders who prioritize security over convenience. They are used by a population that is disproportionately technically sophisticated and security-conscious — the opposite of the ETF buyer population, which uses regulated intermediaries and does not interact with hardware wallets at all. A Coldcard security finding therefore affects the retail self-custody population (who may respond by reducing hardware wallet usage or switching to alternative devices) without meaningfully affecting the ETF inflow population (who hold BTC through Fidelity, BlackRock, or other ETF custodians and never interact with hardware wallets). The fact that ETF inflows hit $850 million on the same day as the Coldcard news is not a statement that institutional buyers are ignoring the security news — it is a statement that the security news is irrelevant to their custody model. This is one of the structural benefits of ETF-format Bitcoin exposure that the institutional market has been pricing since January 2024: the institutional buyer's Bitcoin is not in a hardware wallet that can be subject to a firmware vulnerability. It is held by a custodian (usually Coinbase Custody for most US Bitcoin ETFs) in institutional-grade secure storage with insurance coverage that individual hardware wallet holders cannot access.
  • The 13.4% aggregate hashrate reduction by public miners requires context on what "public miners" means in this calculation. Public Bitcoin miners — companies that are listed on US stock exchanges (NASDAQ, NYSE) and are required to report their operational metrics to the SEC — represent a subset of total Bitcoin mining activity that is concentrated in the United States and primarily powered by North American electrical grid capacity. The total Bitcoin network hashrate is a global metric that includes private miners (non-public companies) in the US, Europe, and increasingly in the Middle East and Southeast Asia. A 13.4% reduction in public US miner hashrate does not necessarily produce a 13.4% reduction in total network hashrate — private miners, particularly those outside the US who do not face the same AI-infrastructure opportunity cost, may maintain or increase their hashrate while US public miners reduce theirs. The practical consequence is that the Bitcoin network's security (measured by total hashrate) is less affected than the US public mining sector's hashrate reduction would imply, while the competitive dynamics of the US public mining sector are significantly affected: public miners who pivot to AI gain revenue diversification but lose hashrate share to private miners who continue mining. The long-term consequence of this shift — whether the US public mining sector re-concentrates in mining as AI infrastructure markets become more crowded — is the strategic question that the next twelve months of Riot/Anthropic and Core Scientific/CoreWeave contract data will answer.
