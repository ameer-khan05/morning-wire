The Anchor · The $100B Post-Election Rally Is Gone — What Erased It and What Comes Next

The crypto market’s $100 billion post-election rally has been fully erased. That is the week’s most important crypto data point. Bitcoin peaked near $80,000 on Wednesday as election results confirmed the Democratic congressional sweep; by Friday’s close it had fallen to $75,496 and the $100 billion in market capitalization that the election added had been given back. The erasure reveals what the rally was built on and what it was not. It was built on the expectation that a Democratic congressional majority would be constructive for digital assets — that the new Congress would advance a Digital Assets Market Structure Act, provide stablecoin regulatory clarity, and remove the enforcement-first posture that has characterized the SEC’s approach to crypto over the past two years. What it was not built on was any actual policy signal — because no such signal has arrived. The rally priced the expectation; the erasure reflects the market’s reassessment that an expectation without confirmation is not a floor. The floor requires the January signal from the new Congress, and that is two months away. Bitcoin at $76,051 on Sunday is the price of that two-month wait.

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin published comments on Sunday endorsing ETH’s role as a store of value, favoring that positioning over the fee-burn deflationary mechanism — EIP-1559’s base-fee burn — as the primary value proposition for holding ETH; the comment represents a shift in Buterin’s public emphasis: he has historically been more cautious about ETH as a pure store-of-value asset, preferring to frame Ethereum’s value in terms of its utility as the programmable settlement layer for DeFi and smart contracts; ETH holders interpreted the comments as a long-term validation of the investment thesis, with Ethereum trading at $2,239 (▲0.1%) on Sunday; in context, Buterin’s store-of-value framing arrives as ETH is trading roughly 25% below its mid-October $3,000 level and the fee-burn mechanism has been less effective than anticipated at reducing supply during the period of reduced on-chain activity that followed the market’s second-half-of-year cooling.

Buterin backing ETH as a store of value is a signal to the long-term holder cohort rather than to traders. It repositions ETH away from “value accrues from activity fees” toward “value accrues from scarcity and settlement primacy” — a thesis that holds in a low-activity environment where fee burn is insufficient to make the deflationary case.

Binance restored HYPE and APT cryptocurrency withdrawals on Sunday, continuing its recovery from the October 29 cyberattack that forced the exchange to suspend withdrawals for multiple digital assets; HYPE and APT join a list of tokens whose withdrawal functionality Binance has progressively restored since the breach, with the exchange entering its second consecutive weekend of post-hack operations; the October 29 attack is among the most significant exchange security incidents of the year, forcing Binance to perform security audits on each affected token’s withdrawal infrastructure before restoration — a process that the exchange has managed without a full trading halt but that has created temporary liquidity constraints for affected assets; full withdrawal restoration across all suspended tokens has not yet been announced, and Binance has not disclosed the technical mechanism of the breach or the total amount compromised.

Binance’s token-by-token withdrawal restoration reflects the genuine complexity of post-hack security review: the exchange cannot restore withdrawals until it has verified that the attack vector for each token has been identified and closed. The fact that HYPE and APT are restored two weeks after the breach suggests those tokens’ infrastructure passed security review; the remaining suspended tokens have not yet cleared that threshold.
