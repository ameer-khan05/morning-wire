The Anchor · BTC $75,799 — A Weekend That Does Not Resolve Friday’s Structural Question

Bitcoin at $75,799 on Saturday is 0.4% above Friday’s $75,496 close. That is not a recovery — it is a weekend with no selling catalyst. The structural question from Friday remains entirely open: crypto’s floor is a regulatory signal from the incoming Democratic congressional majority, and no such signal has arrived. The divergence from equities — stocks up 1.9% on Friday while Bitcoin fell 3.1% on the same day, in response to the same jobs report that rescued stocks — remains unresolved because equities’ floor is macroeconomic and crypto’s floor is regulatory. The jobs data can provide a floor for a Nasdaq long; it cannot provide a floor for a Bitcoin long, whose thesis depends on the new Congress’s interpretation of the Digital Assets Market Structure Act rather than the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ reading of October payrolls. That signal arrives in January at the earliest. Until then, the crypto market is operating in a gap between the last regulatory signal (pre-election ambiguity) and the next one (new Congress policy position), with $75,000 as the technical support level most analysts are watching.

Ethereum recovered to $2,238 on Saturday — up 3.6% from Friday’s $2,160 low — outperforming Bitcoin on the weekend session as the extreme long-squeeze pressure that drove a 7.8% single-day ETH decline on Friday eased without a new catalyst; Ethereum’s deeper Friday decline relative to Bitcoin reflected its higher leverage ratio and greater sensitivity to forced liquidations, and the weekend recovery is partly mechanical — the positions being force-unwound have largely been resolved — rather than a structural reappraisal of Ethereum’s value; at $2,238, ETH remains down approximately 25% from the $3,000 level it held in mid-October and far below the $3,500 level that some analysts targeted for the post-election period on expectations of a constructive regulatory environment for DeFi and staking.

ETH’s deeper loss on Friday and larger relative recovery on Saturday is the classic long-squeeze dynamic: the asset with the highest forced-selling pressure falls furthest on the forced-liquidation day, then bounces hardest the next day when selling pressure mechanically abates. It is not a bullish signal — it is a reversion from an oversold close.

The structural question from Friday’s crypto session — why the same jobs report that produced a 1.9% Nasdaq rebound produced zero floor in Bitcoin — remains unresolved on Saturday with equity markets closed; the answer is not complex: stocks have a macroeconomic floor in October payrolls; crypto’s floor requires a regulatory signal from the incoming Democratic congressional majority on digital assets policy, specifically whether the new majority will advance a constructive Digital Assets Market Structure Act framework or pursue a more restrictive alternative; that signal will not arrive until the new Congress convenes in January 2027; until then, the crypto market is operating in a regulatory signal vacuum, with $75,000 as the technical support and no macro catalyst capable of substituting for the missing policy clarity.

The two-month wait for the January regulatory signal is crypto’s most underappreciated near-term risk: it is not a known unknown with a known date — it is a known unknown with an earliest-possible date of January that could easily extend to February or March as the new Congress sets legislative priorities. The window is wide and the signal could be a long time coming.
