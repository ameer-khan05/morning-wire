The CLARITY Act's bipartisan path has a specific structure that is worth understanding. The bill's advance is not simply a matter of vote counting — it involves a set of substantive policy negotiations over whether stablecoin issuers can offer yield (which banks oppose), what ethics rules apply to public officials holding crypto (triggered by Trump's WLFI position), and what law enforcement access to stablecoin transaction data looks like (which DOJ and Treasury are actively shaping). Each of these issues has a constituency that could delay or alter the bill — the yield debate is the most substantively contested, because it determines whether stablecoins compete with bank deposits and money market funds. A CLARITY Act that passes without yield rights is a weaker ETH catalyst than one that includes them; the Senate's current negotiating posture will determine which version reaches a floor vote.

All four data points are directly relevant to the ETH settlement-layer value thesis that GENIUS supports.

WTI crude's collapse to $79.54 — below $80 for the first time since the Iran conflict began February 28 — improves the September rate-cut probability component of BTC's dual-catalyst scenario before August CPI data even arrives, because the three FOMC hike dissenters who have maintained the hawkish assessment for 34 days have explicitly cited oil's contribution to energy-CPI as their primary reason; at $79.54 WTI and $83.32 Brent (both below the $80 and $85 thresholds respectively that have been associated with the oil-inflation argument for holding rates), the energy-CPI argument weakens materially in a way that is visible to the public record before the FOMC needs to formally reassess, improving the probability-weighted expected value of BTC's September scenario from both the rate-cut component and the CLARITY-passage component simultaneously. The directional improvement is real; the durability depends on the Iran diplomatic channel through Oman.

Iran's denial of direct US talks — and its statement that it is speaking to Oman about Hormuz — means the oil relief is currently priced against a diplomatic probability rather than a confirmed signed framework. The Oman channel is credible and historically effective for US-Iran communication, and a deal brokered through Oman would carry the same oil-market implications as a direct deal: Hormuz traffic restored toward 120+ vessels per day, oil supply increases, Brent retreats further. But if the Oman channel fails and Iran resumes strait restrictions, the oil rally back toward $87+ would compress September rate-cut probability back to near-Friday levels within one to two sessions. For BTC, the most important near-term question is not whether the deal is direct or through Oman but whether the Hormuz arrangement — whatever channel it comes through — is durable enough to sustain WTI below $80 for the two-to-three weeks that would change the FOMC hike dissenters' publicly stated assessment.
