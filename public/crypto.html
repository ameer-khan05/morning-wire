The Anchor · Bitcoin’s 200-Day Break — The Threshold That Changes the Technical Regime

Five days of consecutive losses have a shape. This week’s shape is a staircase: each day added a new source of pressure without removing the previous one, and Friday’s 10-year yield crossing 5.14% was the final step that broke the last technical support. Day one (Monday, Nov 9): Bitcoin at $75,837. Normal volatility range. Day two (Tuesday, Nov 11): October CPI prints hot. December Fed hike odds surge to 87%. Dollar strengthens. BTC falls to $74,826. The macro headwind is installed. Day three (Wednesday, Nov 12, Veterans Day): Bond markets closed. Equities recover on AI earnings. Bitcoin falls anyway, to $73,937 — decoupling from the equity recovery and processing its own independent headwinds. ETF outflows confirm institutional de-risking. Day four (Thursday, Nov 13): Bond markets reopen at 4.99%. Jensen Huang resigns in a spy scandal. BTC falls to $73,208. Day five (Friday, Nov 13): PPI’s year-over-year rate hits 6.5%, the highest since November 2022. The 10-year yield crosses 5.14%. Bitcoin’s 200-day moving average — the last structural technical support — is broken. BTC flash-crashes to $66,900 intraday, triggering $1.47 billion in forced liquidations, before recovering to $68,350 at the close. The five days compose a systematic decomposition of every support structure Bitcoin had: the macro (CPI), the institutional (ETF outflows), the technical (200-day), and the rate-environment (5.14% 10Y). None has reversed. The conditions for a sustained reversal require at least one of those drivers to change direction. As of Friday’s close, none has.
