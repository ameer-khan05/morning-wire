Bitcoin's 2.09% rise to $65,023 is driven primarily by the AI sector sentiment rehabilitation that Amazon's AWS blowout delivered — when the two leading cloud hyperscalers post 37–41% revenue growth with expanding operating margins in the same earnings cycle, the market's assessment of AI infrastructure investment shifts from "Meta-pattern: capex without near-term returns" to "Azure-pattern: infrastructure generating commercial revenue at scale," and that shift removes the AI sector risk-off headwind that had pushed BTC to $63,157 Wednesday; BTC's recovery above $65,000 is the market's expression of restored AI sector sentiment rather than a change in the CLARITY or rate-cut fundamentals, which remain: CLARITY in September (unchanged), September rate cuts uncertain (Brent above $88, three FOMC hike dissenters), Polymarket odds stable at approximately 37%. The AWS-Azure alignment — two of three leading hyperscalers validating the cloud AI returns model in the same quarter — is the most positive AI sector data point for BTC since the sector was unified on the AI-capex-builds-returns thesis before Meta's Wednesday shock.

The indirect channel from AI sector sentiment to BTC pricing is worth being precise about. BTC does not benefit directly from AWS revenue or Azure revenue — the connection is through risk appetite. When AI sector sentiment is strong (multiple hyperscalers generating accelerating cloud revenue from AI compute), institutional and retail investors are more willing to hold speculative assets with AI-adjacent narratives (BTC as digital scarcity asset in an AI-capital-intensive economy, L1 tokens as AI compute demand beneficiaries). When AI sector sentiment deteriorates (Meta's FCF collapse signals AI capex without proportional returns), that risk appetite contracts and crypto positions are reduced. The 2.09% BTC recovery is the sentiment restoration, not a fundamental catalyst change. The structural setup — CLARITY September, uncertain rate cuts — is unchanged.

Iran's Day 31 expansion to Egypt, Kuwait, and Bahrain — drone strikes at the Damietta LNG port and Iranian attacks on US military facilities in both Gulf states — pushed Brent crude to $88.97 (▲1.04%), sustaining the oil-inflation channel that compresses September rate-cut probability and keeps the rate-channel offset for BTC diminished; at Brent above $88, the energy component of CPI remains elevated enough that the three FOMC members who voted for a hike at the July 29 meeting face no data-based reason to reverse their assessment before the September 16 FOMC, making the dual-catalyst scenario (CLARITY passage plus rate cut on September 16) less probable than it appeared before Wednesday's 30-year yield surge to 5.24%. The August CPI release in mid-August remains the key data point: if oil eases below $85 before the print, the case for September cuts strengthens; at $88.97, it does not.

The Damietta port strikes introduce a dimension beyond Brent pricing. The Energos Winter is a floating storage and regasification unit — infrastructure for receiving and processing LNG shipments — and the GasLog Salem is an LNG tanker delivering gas to European buyers transitioning away from Russian pipeline gas. Disrupting LNG infrastructure at an Egyptian hub port adds European gas price risk to the conflict's economic footprint, which feeds into European CPI, which influences ECB decisions, which affects the global rate environment that BTC's macro positioning reflects. The oil price at $88.97 is the direct inflationary input; the LNG infrastructure disruption is the indirect input through European energy supply that will take longer to transmit into visible price data but is additive to the inflationary pressure channel.
