The Anchor · CPI and Crypto — Hot Inflation Adds a New Headwind to an Already Pressured Market

Crypto did not benefit from Monday’s risk-on equity rally. It is not benefiting from Tuesday’s CPI-driven market decline either. Bitcoin drifting 0.4% lower on a day when the same inflation report is dragging equities down 0.8% is the market confirming that Bitcoin is not correlated with equities in a simple way right now — it is processing its own headwinds, and the macro headwinds are adding pressure on top. The CPI-to-crypto transmission mechanism works through the dollar: hotter inflation raises Fed rate-hike expectations, rate-hike expectations strengthen the dollar, and a stronger dollar reduces the attractiveness of dollar-denominated assets like BTC and ETH for non-dollar buyers. That is a moderate-sized headwind, not a catastrophic one — which is why BTC fell only 0.4% rather than the 0.8% that equities gave up. The more important crypto-specific dynamic on Tuesday is Ethereum’s second session below the 200-day moving average. One session below the 200-day is a technical signal; two consecutive sessions below it is a technical confirmation that most systematic trend-following funds treat as a sell trigger. The positions that did not exit Monday exit Tuesday, adding mechanical selling that is independent of any fundamental view on Ethereum’s long-term prospects. The stablecoin market cap decline from $309.7 billion to $288.9 billion is the broader context: capital is leaving the digital asset ecosystem, not rotating within it. When stablecoin balances fall, it typically indicates that investors are converting stablecoins back to fiat rather than redeploying into BTC, ETH, or other tokens. That is what has been happening since May, and Tuesday gives no sign that the direction has changed.

Bitcoin fell to $74,260 on Tuesday — down 0.4% from Monday’s $74,549 — as October’s core CPI coming in at +0.3% month-over-month for a third consecutive above-forecast month pushed December Fed rate-hike odds to 87.3% and strengthened the dollar, adding a macro headwind to the crypto-specific pressures that have been driving Bitcoin’s drift since the post-election rally’s erasure; Bitcoin’s 0.4% decline is notably smaller than the S&P 500’s 0.8% and Nasdaq’s 1.1% falls on the same CPI data, confirming that Bitcoin is not moving in lockstep with risk assets but is processing a separate set of headwinds at a different rate; Bitcoin holds above its own 200-day moving average at approximately $72,500, which remains the technical bull-bear divide for Bitcoin specifically, distinguishing it from Ethereum which is now two sessions below its own 200-day.

Bitcoin’s 0.4% decline on a day when the dollar is strengthening and equities are falling harder is not a sign of resilience — it is a sign that Bitcoin’s current price range reflects accumulated crypto-specific discounts already. The macro headwinds are adding at the margin, but the larger drag on Bitcoin remains the regulatory uncertainty and the absence of a confirmed digital assets policy floor from the new Congress, which does not convene until January.

Ethereum fell 1.3% to $2,090 on Tuesday, extending its violation of the 200-day moving average into a second consecutive session — a technical confirmation threshold that most systematic trend-following funds treat as a sell trigger, as opposed to Monday’s one-session break which some models treat as noise — with the mechanical selling from funds whose models require exit after a confirmed 200-day violation adding to the organic selling pressure from ETH-specific headwinds; Ethereum has now underperformed Bitcoin by approximately 2.5% over the past two sessions, with the ETH/BTC ratio remaining near its July lows and reflecting the market’s continued judgment that ETH carries higher regulatory risk relative to BTC given its utility platform positioning and exposure to DeFi, smart contract regulation, and staking yield frameworks that are all currently undefined under the new Democratic congressional majority’s forthcoming digital assets regulatory agenda.

Two sessions below the 200-day moving average converts a technical signal into a technical confirmation. That confirmation triggers systematic selling from trend-following funds whose models are not designed to hold losing positions through confirmed downtrends. The organic pressure and the systematic pressure are now both pointing in the same direction for ETH, and the fundamental catalyst to change either — a clear regulatory signal from Congress — does not arrive until January at the earliest.

Total stablecoin market capitalization has declined $21 billion from its May 2026 peak of $309.7 billion to $288.9 billion — a 7% drawdown sustained over six months that reflects broad capital exit from the digital asset ecosystem rather than rotation within it; when stablecoin balances fall, it typically indicates that investors are converting stablecoins to fiat rather than redeploying into BTC, ETH, or other tokens, as opposed to periods when stablecoin balances rise alongside falling crypto prices, which indicate investors are holding in the ecosystem and waiting to redeploy; the $21 billion decline coincides with the full erasure of the post-election $100 billion crypto rally, the Binance October 29 hack and its ongoing recovery shadow, and the sustained absence of a confirmed US digital assets regulatory framework; Circle, the USDC issuer, separately noted that global stablecoin payment activity remained at record levels even as market cap fell, suggesting stablecoins’ role as a payment rail is diverging from their role as a speculative holding vehicle.

Stablecoin market cap falling while stablecoin payment activity hits records is the most nuanced signal in Tuesday’s crypto beat. It describes a digital asset ecosystem that is evolving: stablecoins are becoming infrastructure for payments even as speculative capital exits the broader market. The two trends can coexist and do not contradict each other. Payment infrastructure is a durable use case; speculative capital allocation responds to market cycles. Right now, the cycle is pointing down and the infrastructure is growing.

SEC CFTC Moving Forward Crypto Broker-Dealer Rules Existing Authority CLARITY Digital Assets Market Structure Bill Failed September White House Review November 10 2026 SEC CFTC Rules · Existing Authority · CLARITY Bill Failed Regs (Tue Nov 10 2026): SEC → CFTC → moving → forward → crypto → broker-dealer → rules → existing → authority → CLARITY → bill → failed → September → White House → review

The SEC and CFTC are expected to move forward with crypto broker-dealer and market structure rules under their existing statutory authority, following the failure of the CLARITY digital assets market structure bill in September — the legislation that the crypto industry had hoped would create a comprehensive framework distinguishing between crypto securities (SEC jurisdiction) and crypto commodities (CFTC jurisdiction); the SEC submitted proposed crypto broker-dealer rules to the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs for review, the first step in the formal rulemaking process, covering four securities regulations; the CLARITY bill’s failure means that the crypto market structure question will be answered through administrative rulemaking rather than congressional legislation, a process that is slower, more litigable, and more vulnerable to legal challenge than a statutory framework would have been; a central unresolved issue is whether stablecoin companies can offer yield-bearing products, with banking groups warning that such offerings could draw deposits away from traditional banks.

Regulatory rulemaking without a statutory foundation is the worst of both worlds for the crypto industry: it means rules exist and must be followed, but those rules can be challenged in court and reversed by future administrations without the durability of an act of Congress. The CLARITY bill’s failure handed that dynamic to the regulators, and the crypto market is now operating under the uncertainty of rules that exist in regulatory limbo.
