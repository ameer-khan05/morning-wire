The Anchor · Bitcoin’s Three-Month Low — The Rate Environment, MSCI’s Structural Move, and $1.96 Billion in 24-Hour Liquidations

The conditions that produced Bitcoin’s 16.3% weekly decline have not changed, and Sunday added two new structural pressures that were not present last week. At $62,199, Bitcoin has fallen 23% from its November 4 high of approximately $80,900 — a compression driven by the compounding of five macro and institutional events: the October CPI coming in above forecast, the PPI YoY at 6.5%, the 10-year yield crossing 5.14%, the Anthropic IPO drawing $386 billion in institutional demand on the same week, and the Nvidia national security crisis repricing the AI sector broadly. What Sunday added: MSCI’s confirmation that Strategy will be removed from its global standard indexes at the November 30 close. Strategy holds approximately 331,200 Bitcoin on its balance sheet, valued at over $20 billion at current prices. The MSCI removal does not require Strategy to sell Bitcoin — it requires passive index-tracking funds that hold MSTR shares to sell those shares by November 30. The $1.5 billion in forced MSTR selling creates a structural supply overhang on a stock whose price is tightly correlated with Bitcoin’s. As MSTR falls, the correlation dynamic puts additional downward pressure on Bitcoin sentiment — not through direct selling, but through the narrative that Strategy’s balance sheet is marked to a declining asset while its index support is removed. The $60,000 level is the next structural support. CryptoTicker warned Sunday it may not hold if the December FOMC hike at near-certain odds produces another bout of institutional de-risking. The $1.96 billion in 24-hour liquidations are not random — they are the forced position closures of leveraged traders who entered the week long and ran out of margin as Bitcoin fell through $65,000 and then $62,500. Each liquidation adds to selling pressure. At $62,199, the market is testing whether long-term holders provide a price floor or sell alongside the institutional de-risking. The answer arrives with Monday’s open.

The MSCI removal is not an expression of MSCI’s view on Bitcoin. It is a consequence of Strategy’s market cap and liquidity characteristics relative to MSCI’s index methodology. The timing — announced into an existing Bitcoin downtrend — is coincidental. The selling pressure it creates by November 30 is not.

Trezor Confirms Safe 5 Hardware Wallet Supply Chain Backdoor Southeast Asia Reseller Hidden Board Copies Recovery Words Sends Mobile Network Firmware November 11 Blocks Implants Three Wallets Confirmed Sunday November 15 2026 Trezor Safe 5 Supply Chain Backdoor · Hidden Board · Firmware Nov 11 Patches Trezor (Nov 15 2026): Safe 5 → backdoor → hidden → board → copies → recovery → words → mobile → network → Southeast → Asia → reseller → firmware → Nov 11 → blocks

Trezor confirmed Sunday that three Safe 5 hardware wallets purchased from a former reseller in Southeast Asia carried a hidden circuit board that copied seed phrase recovery words off the device screen and transmitted them over a mobile network — a supply chain attack that allowed the attacker to gain access to whatever funds the wallet owner subsequently stored; the firmware update released November 11 detects and blocks the implants in affected devices, but wallets sold before that date that have not been updated remain potentially compromised; Trezor confirmed the attack vector was a third-party reseller, not the Trezor manufacturing chain — a distinction that matters for trust in Trezor’s own supply chain but does not mitigate the risk for buyers who purchased from unauthorized resellers; the discovery arrives in a market where $62,199 Bitcoin and Extreme Fear sentiment already reduce tolerance for additional trust-layer risk in self-custody infrastructure.

Hardware wallet supply chain attacks are not new — but confirmed cases with specific reseller identification, specific device models, and patch-verified firmware are rare enough to warrant attention from every Safe 5 owner. The firmware update is free and blocks the implant. The lesson for buyers is structural: hardware wallets purchased from unauthorized secondary market sellers have no supply chain provenance guarantees regardless of the manufacturer’s reputation.
