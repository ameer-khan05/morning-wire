Polymarket odds on the CLARITY Act passing in 2026 collapsed overnight to 14% — down from a peak of 82% — as the Senate's ethics fight over President Trump's more than $1 billion in 2025 crypto income disclosure emerged as the bill's most serious obstacle. Republicans hold 53 Senate seats but are expected to lose Senators Hawley and Paul on the cloture vote, meaning at least eight Democrats must cross the aisle to reach the 60-vote threshold; the ethics provision targeting Trump's crypto income has made those crossovers politically toxic for the Democratic senators most likely to provide them. Bitcoin fell to approximately $77,648 (▼1.09% from Monday's close of $78,500) as the CLARITY legislative premium began unwinding, compounded by inflation expectations from the Hormuz tanker attack driving oil higher and hardening the Federal Reserve's September rate-hike case. ETH edged up to $2,454 (▲0.33%), reflecting the absence of a CLARITY-specific downward pressure on Ethereum and mild positive momentum independent of the legislative collapse. A single wallet placed an $818,000 bet against CLARITY passage on Polymarket, the largest single position against the bill on record.

The CLARITY Act's collapse from 82% to 14% Polymarket odds in a matter of days is the most consequential single repricing in the crypto legislative narrative of 2026. The 82% peak reflected a market that believed the path to 60 Senate votes was clear; 14% reflects a market that now believes the ethics fight has made those votes unreachable within the September 15 calendar window. BTC at $77,648 has not fully priced a failure scenario — that unwind still lies ahead if the vote falls short.

The Anchor · CLARITY Collapses — The Ethics Fight That Killed the Premium

The CLARITY Act was priced at 82% passage probability a week ago. It is priced at 14% today. The change is not a new vote count, a new amendment, or a new whip intelligence report — it is the emergence of the ethics provision as the central obstacle. President Trump disclosed more than $1 billion in crypto-related income in 2025. The CLARITY Act, as written, includes an ethics provision that would restrict presidential crypto activity of that kind. Trump opposes the provision. The provisions he opposes create a crossover problem for Democrats: voting for the bill without the ethics provision means voting for a crypto law that explicitly protects a presidential crypto income stream that Democrats view as corrupt; voting against the bill means killing the broader crypto regulatory framework that many Democratic senators support in principle. That tension — support the framework, protect the ethics provision — is not resolvable on the September 15 calendar without either the ethics provision coming out (which loses Democratic votes) or staying in (which may lose Democratic votes by making the bill politically untenable for swing-state members). The $818,000 single-wallet bet against passage is the market's most visible signal that sophisticated money now believes 14% Polymarket odds understate the probability of failure — that the ethics fight is not resolvable before September 15, and the vote count will fall short of 60. What happens to BTC if the vote fails depends on where the failure comes from. A hard failure — vote held, falls short, bill dies — prices toward the pre-CLARITY baseline of approximately $64,800, a decline of approximately $12,848 from today's level. A soft failure — Thune acknowledges the count is insufficient and delays the vote — preserves a residual premium for a future legislative attempt while still removing the September binary. The ETH-BTC divergence today (ETH +0.33%, BTC -1.09%) confirms what the market has known all month: CLARITY is a BTC-specific legislative trade. ETH does not carry the same legislative premium and therefore does not reprice the same way on CLARITY news. The two assets are now trading distinct risk profiles inside the same crypto market cap. The inflation and rate-hike macro environment is a secondary headwind: oil at $91.28 and the 10-year at 4.72% ▲ are both consistent with a September rate hike that compresses BTC by raising the discount rate on the legislative upside. If August CPI comes in hot on September 10, the rate headwind adds to the CLARITY legislative collapse on the same 14-day calendar.

Ethereum held at $2,454 Tuesday (▲0.33% from Monday's close) as Bitcoin fell 1.09% on the CLARITY Act collapse — a divergence that confirms what this month's trading has demonstrated consistently: the CLARITY legislative premium is a BTC-specific trade. ETH opened at $2,467 Tuesday morning (up 2% from Monday's opening) before easing to $2,454 through the session, tracking general market sentiment rather than the legislative binary that is repricing BTC. There is no CLARITY-specific legislative premium in ETH to unwind, which is why ETH holds positive on a day when BTC is the primary target of the CLARITY collapse selling pressure.

The macro headwinds — oil-driven inflation expectations, the rising 10-year yield, the hardening September rate-hike case — apply to both BTC and ETH as risk assets. But because ETH carries no CLARITY legislative premium, those macro headwinds are the only force working against it on Tuesday, while BTC faces the macro headwinds plus the CLARITY collapse simultaneously. The result is ETH's relative outperformance. This dynamic will reverse if CLARITY somehow passes: BTC's legislative premium would expand, and ETH would lag. It holds as long as CLARITY remains at sub-20% odds. The global crypto market cap at approximately $2.70 trillion reflects BTC's modest decline offset partially by ETH's slight gain and relatively stable altcoin performance.
