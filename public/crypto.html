The Anchor · Wednesday Fully Reversed — Saudi Arabia Attacks Rebuild the Oil-Inflation Headwind as Brent Exceeds Tuesday’s Five-Month High by $3.58

Thursday’s Bitcoin decline is the structural reversal of Wednesday’s recovery, and the reversal is more severe: where Wednesday added $2,022 to Bitcoin’s price through a combination of oil retreat and AI earnings confirmation, Thursday removes $1,594 through a Saudi Arabia attack surge that exceeds Tuesday’s prior high by $3.58 in Brent terms. The net effect is a worse macro environment on Thursday evening than on Tuesday evening. The driver sequence is the same oil-to-macro-to-crypto transmission mechanism that has governed the past two sessions, operating in the adverse direction: Saudi Arabia energy infrastructure attacks create a supply-disruption risk premium that is qualitatively different from the diplomatic-engagement risk reduction that drove Wednesday’s oil retreat. Witkoff’s St. Petersburg meeting reduced oil’s geopolitical premium through a reversible signal — diplomatic engagement that can deteriorate as easily as it progressed. The Saudi Arabia attacks create a physical infrastructure disruption risk premium that cannot be reversed by a statement: until the specific facilities are assessed for damage, the market prices supply disruption risk at an elevated level. Brent at $112.37 means the Fed’s October 28 decision arrives with energy inflation materially higher than at any point this week, not lower as Wednesday had temporarily suggested. The Fed pause probability that improved from Tuesday to Wednesday on Brent’s retreat has fully reversed — Brent at $112.37 is the highest inflation-relevant energy price the October 28 decision has faced since the current oil run began. Meta’s 6% equity decline adds the AI trade headwind that Wednesday briefly removed: investors who read Wednesday’s $130-145 billion AI capex guidance as demand confirmation are now reading it as debt-issuance magnitude, and the recalculation produces a 6% decline. Both transmission channels — oil inflation and AI trade risk appetite — are operating adversely on Thursday, which is why the session’s decline is proportionally larger than either individual component would produce alone.
