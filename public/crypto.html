The Anchor · The Macro Overlay — What PCE Inflation Data Does to Bitcoin When the Fed’s December Rate Decision Is at Stake and Equities Are Already Selling Off

Bitcoin’s reaction to Wednesday’s PCE data is a sharper test of the equity-crypto correlation thesis than Tuesday’s chip selloff. Tuesday’s Bitcoin decline was driven by Nvidia’s retracement — a governance-premium correction specific to one stock that happened to be the most important driver of Monday’s AI rally. Wednesday’s decline is driven by PCE inflation data — a macroeconomic report that determines whether the Federal Reserve cuts rates in December, which affects every risk asset simultaneously. The question for Bitcoin’s correlation thesis is: does it matter to the asset why equities are selling off, or does it only matter that equities are selling off? Wednesday’s data point says it doesn’t matter. Bitcoin fell 2.21% on a macro shock and fell 5.52% on a chip-specific shock. In both cases, it tracked equities downward at a consistent amplification ratio. The correlation is not sensitive to the driver of the equity decline — it is sensitive only to the direction and magnitude. That means Bitcoin’s near-term price is not determined by any Bitcoin-specific variable. It is determined by whatever moves equities. On Tuesday that was Nvidia’s governance retracement. On Wednesday it is PCE inflation data. For anyone holding Bitcoin as a macroeconomic hedge or as an asset with a Bitcoin-specific investment thesis — store of value, digital gold, decentralized money — none of those theses are what is driving the price this week. What is driving the price is PCE data, Nvidia governance, and tariff headlines. Those are equity catalysts. Bitcoin is amplifying them.

Bitcoin fell 2.21% to $58,564 on Wednesday as PCE inflation data for October came in above consensus expectations and the Trump administration floated new tariff proposals on semiconductor imports, with The Company Chronicle reporting BTC at $58,616 on Coinbase early Wednesday — down 2.2% over 24 hours — and CoinGabbar’s market update noting that PCE data added a Fed-December-decision dimension to the equity-crypto correlation that had already driven Tuesday’s 5.52% decline; the Nasdaq’s 3.98% intraday plunge directly transmitted into Bitcoin’s 2.21% decline via the correlation that has characterized November’s price action — an amplification ratio of approximately 2:1 on a macro-driven session, slightly lower than Tuesday’s 3.5:1 ratio on the chip-specific day because Wednesday’s shock affected all risk assets more broadly; and the two-session cumulative decline from $60,458 to $58,564 means Bitcoin has given back nearly all of Monday’s governance-driven rebound in 48 hours, with no independent catalyst having driven the asset’s direction in either down session.

PCE data’s effect on Bitcoin differs from Nvidia’s in one structural way: PCE is a Fed signal. If inflation stays above target, the Fed holds in December, maintaining the risk-free rate and reducing the relative attractiveness of risk assets including crypto. That adds a monetary policy transmission on top of the direct equity correlation — a second link to rates running below the first. Bitcoin is now absorbing two simultaneous headwinds: equity sell-off and a narrower path to a December cut.
