The Anchor · CLARITY Act Ethics Package Resolved at White House Summit — The Obstacle That Was Blocking 9 Senate Holdouts Is Gone · Bessent "1-Yard Line" Is the Most Consequential Policy Signal for Crypto Legislation in 2026

The CLARITY Act ethics package — conflict-of-interest rules governing presidential and congressional trading in digital assets — was the specific legislative obstacle that had made nine Republican senators unwilling to provide the cloture votes CLARITY needed to advance. Those senators were not opposed to crypto market structure legislation; they were unwilling to vote for a bill that could financially benefit a sitting president who held a significant WLFI token position without explicit conflict-of-interest guardrails. The White House's agreement to include those guardrails in the bill removes the stated objection from the single most consequential group of holdouts. Bessent's "1-yard line" characterization is the signal the crypto market has been waiting for — not just that CLARITY is alive (it has been alive at 20% odds), but that the administration has made the specific concession that the holdout senators required, and that the administration believes that concession is sufficient to move those votes. BTC's jump to $64,059 (+1.56%) is the market pricing a higher CLARITY passage probability than the 20% that was priced on Monday — not a certainty, but a meaningful upward revision. The CFTC Advisory tomorrow is still consequential: the jurisdictional guidance it provides on digital commodities versus digital securities is the regulatory complement to CLARITY's legislative framework, and a clear CFTC position tomorrow would strengthen the floor under any Senate vote regardless of the summit's ethics package outcome. Goldman's NEOS acquisition is the institutionalization signal that the crypto market receives regardless of CLARITY: Goldman paying $2.25 billion for an investment manager that includes $1 billion in Bitcoin ETF exposure is a statement that Bitcoin ETFs are now a permanent fixture of the institutional asset management landscape, and that Goldman's wealth management business needs direct Bitcoin ETF infrastructure for the next decade's client demand. The ETH staking record — 34.4% of all supply — is the signal that long-term ETH holders have not used the recent price weakness as an exit point. What the ethics package resolves and what it does not: the ethics package agreement removes the conflict-of-interest objection from the holdout senators' stated positions, which is the necessary condition for those senators to vote yes. But necessary is not sufficient — the holdouts also have objections on yield rights (whether tokenized yield-bearing stablecoins count as securities) and on law enforcement data access requirements embedded in the bill. Those objections are not resolved by the ethics package alone. Bessent's "1-yard line" characterization suggests the administration believes the ethics package was the primary obstacle and that the remaining objections are negotiable at the margin, not structural impediments. The market should read "1-yard line" as the administration's assessment that the votes are achievable with the ethics package plus minor adjustments — not as a guarantee that the votes are already committed. The gap between "achievable" and "committed" is where September's legislative action will be won or lost.

The September dual catalyst remains intact and is now more credible: CLARITY September 14 with a higher passage probability than it had Monday, and FOMC September 16 with the first expected rate cut of the cycle — a macro tailwind for risk assets that crypto would benefit from regardless of CLARITY's outcome. The week ahead — CFTC Advisory tomorrow, Bessent's summit signal digested by market participants — is the period in which the market reprices CLARITY odds from 20% to whatever the summit's ethics breakthrough actually implies.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent described the Senate as now at the "1-yard line," the most optimistic characterization any senior administration official has given the bill's prospects since it stalled on cloture votes in August.

The summit brought together the most senior simultaneous gathering of executive, industry, and regulatory crypto leadership in 2026 — Trump alongside the CEOs of Coinbase, Ripple, Gemini, Robinhood, Polymarket, and Kalshi, with CFTC Chair and SEC Chair in attendance. The ethics package is the specific output that moves CLARITY from stalled to potentially viable before Thune's September procedural vote. The remaining obstacles — holdout senators' positions on yield rights definitions and law enforcement data access in the bill — are now negotiating items rather than structural blocks, per Bessent's characterization. The CFTC Advisory meeting Wednesday will provide the regulatory complement to Tuesday's legislative signal: a clear CFTC jurisdictional position on digital commodities versus digital securities would supply the inter-agency alignment that CLARITY needs from the regulatory side independently of the ethics package's legislative impact.

The record staking ratio during a period of below-cost-basis pricing indicates that the holders who remain in ETH are disproportionately long-duration participants committed to network operation rather than short-term traders seeking liquidity exits, a composition that reduces the circulating supply available for spot selling and provides structural support for price stability at current levels.

The CFTC Innovation Advisory meeting Wednesday August 20 will address crypto, AI, and prediction markets — and ETH's classification as a digital commodity versus a digital security is among the most consequential questions the Advisory will address. A clear CFTC position that ETH is a digital commodity (consistent with the CFTC's long-held view) would provide the regulatory complement to CLARITY's market structure framework that the SEC's canceled rulemaking left absent. The Advisory's output is guidance, not binding regulation, but it advances the inter-agency position that CLARITY's floor debate requires — and arriving the day after the White House summit's ethics package breakthrough, it completes the regulatory-legislative alignment sequence that CLARITY's September procedural vote needs to have a credible cloture pathway.

Quick hits

  • The Goldman-NEOS valuation multiple deserves unpacking. Goldman paid $2.25 billion for NEOS Investments, which manages approximately $1 billion in Bitcoin ETF exposure across its product lineup. At face value, that is a 2.25x price-to-AUM multiple — expensive for a traditional asset manager but within range for a growing alternative asset platform. The premium reflects what Goldman is actually buying: not the current $1 billion in Bitcoin ETF AUM, but the platform infrastructure (client relationships, distribution network, product approval, compliance architecture) for growing that AUM to $10 billion or more as institutional Bitcoin ETF demand grows over the next decade. Goldman's wealth management clients have been asking for Bitcoin ETF exposure through their existing Goldman relationships; NEOS gives Goldman that capability without building it internally. The $2.25 billion price is Goldman's estimate of the long-term value of having that Bitcoin ETF distribution infrastructure embedded in its platform.
  • The 34.4% ETH staking ratio has a supply dynamics implication that matters for price. When more than one third of all circulating ETH is locked in validators, the effective liquid supply available for spot trading and exchange selling shrinks by that proportion. In a market where price is set at the margin — by the least price-sensitive sellers — reducing the available selling supply by one third while demand holds constant is structurally price-supportive. The record staking ratio during a period of below-cost-basis pricing means that as price has fallen toward or below the average cost basis of market participants, the holders who have not sold have chosen instead to stake — locking their ETH for validator economics rather than selling at a loss. This is behavior consistent with long-duration conviction rather than capitulation, and it is one reason ETH's price has been more stable at current levels than the broader Fear and Greed metric would predict.
