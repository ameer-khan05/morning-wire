The Anchor · $1 Billion in Liquidations — Crypto’s Counter-Trend in a Risk-On Day

Equities rallied 2%, oil fell 5%, and Bitcoin fell 2% in the same Monday session. That divergence is the most important data point in today’s crypto beat. Crypto was supposed to benefit from the same risk-on signal that lifted equities — Iran peace talks, oil de-escalation, end of the equity losing streak. It did not. The $1 billion in liquidations is the mechanical explanation: forced long selling cascades when price falls below margin levels, and the cascade itself drives further price decline. But the cascade was triggered by crypto-specific concerns, not by the macro picture. The regulatory signal from the new Democratic congressional majority on digital assets policy remains absent — the new Congress does not convene until January, and the market is not confident that its first digital assets statement will be constructive. The Binance hack’s ongoing recovery is a persistent exchange-risk signal that has not fully cleared. And ETH’s 200-day moving average violation is a technical trigger that prompts systematic selling from trend-following funds regardless of macro conditions. The result is a market that is processing its own structural concerns on a day when every other risk asset was pricing the optimistic scenario. Bitcoin at $74,549 and Ethereum at $2,118 are where crypto is while the rest of the market celebrates Abu Dhabi.

By the Numbers
$1B
Forced liquidations that hit the crypto market on Monday — the mechanical driver of Bitcoin’s break below $75,000 and Ethereum’s 5.3% single-day decline. Liquidations of this scale occur when leveraged long positions are forcibly closed by exchanges as collateral falls below maintenance margin requirements, triggering a cascade: forced selling drives price lower, which triggers more margin calls, which produces more forced selling. The $1 billion figure represents the total value of long positions liquidated across exchanges; the actual price impact is amplified by the speed and sequence of the cascade. It is the worst single-session liquidation event since the post-election rally’s erasure began.

Bitcoin fell to $74,549 on Monday — down 2.0% from Sunday’s close of $76,051 — as $1 billion in forced liquidations drove a cascade that broke Bitcoin below the $75,000 technical support level analysts had identified as the next key demand zone before $70,000; the liquidation cascade was triggered by regulatory uncertainty and institutional de-risking in a session where US equities rallied 2% on Iran peace signals, creating the clearest divergence between crypto and risk assets since the post-election period; Bitcoin holds above its 200-day moving average at approximately $72,500, which remains the technical bull-bear divide for Bitcoin specifically, distinguishing it from Ethereum which broke below its own 200-day intraday; at $74,549, Bitcoin has now retraced the entire post-election rally and an additional 2.0% below the pre-election level, entering territory that analysts describe as testing genuine fundamental demand rather than rally-reversal mechanics.

Bitcoin breaking $75,000 on a day when equities are rallying is the week’s most diagnostic crypto data point. The post-election $100 billion rally’s erasure was understandable — it was a rally without a confirmed catalyst. Bitcoin continuing to fall below the pre-election level in a risk-on equity session is a different signal: it describes an asset with crypto-specific headwinds that macro tailwinds cannot currently overcome.

Ethereum fell 5.3% to $2,118 on Monday, breaking below both the $2,100 psychological level and its 200-day moving average for the first time since August — a technically significant violation that ETH has not experienced since the summer and that typically triggers systematic selling from trend-following funds whose models treat the 200-day as the boundary between bull and bear conditions; the ETH/BTC ratio fell to its lowest level since July, reflecting Ethereum’s sharper underperformance relative to Bitcoin in a session where both declined but ETH declined at 5.3% versus Bitcoin’s 2.0%; ETH’s weaker relative performance reflects its higher sensitivity to the dual concerns currently weighing on crypto — regulatory uncertainty about DeFi and smart contract platforms, which affects ETH’s specific utility case more directly than Bitcoin’s store-of-value positioning — and the reduced on-chain activity that has made the fee-burn mechanism less effective at supporting ETH’s price through supply reduction.

ETH below its 200-day while BTC holds above its own 200-day is the technical expression of a fundamental divergence: Bitcoin is in the phase where “digital gold” store-of-value positioning provides a floor, and Ethereum is in the phase where “utility platform” positioning provides less support when on-chain activity is declining. The two assets are not in the same position in their cycles.

The broader crypto market declined Monday on three converging concerns: continued uncertainty about US digital assets regulation under the new Democratic congressional majority, whose first formal policy signal on crypto market structure legislation is not expected until January 2027 at the earliest; the ongoing shadow of the Binance October 29 hack, which has not been fully resolved and continues to represent exchange-specific risk that institutional investors factor into their position sizing; and institutional de-risking that reflects the same judgment Goldman Sachs made about AI coding tools — that the risk-adjusted case for broader deployment has not yet been confirmed by the regulatory and security infrastructure the asset class requires; the crypto market’s failure to participate in Monday’s risk-on equity rally despite the same macroeconomic de-escalation signals driving equities higher describes a market with idiosyncratic headwinds that macro tailwinds cannot currently resolve.

Crypto decoupling from equities on a risk-on day is not novel — it has happened before during periods of crypto-specific regulatory pressure. What is unusual is that the decoupling is occurring while the post-election $100 billion rally’s erasure is still fresh. The market priced a regulatory floor in November that it now believes arrives in January at the earliest. That two-month gap is the current price of crypto.
