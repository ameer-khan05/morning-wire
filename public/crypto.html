The Anchor · Four Consecutive Days — When Multiple Pressures Arrive at Once and None Reverse

Four consecutive days of Bitcoin losses is not unusual in a volatile asset class. What makes this week’s run unusual is the structure: each of the four days has added a new source of pressure without removing the previous ones, compounding rather than cycling. Day one (Monday): Bitcoin at $75,837, pre-CPI. Normal volatility range. Day two (Tuesday): The October CPI data prints hot — inflation not resolving, December Fed rate-hike odds surge to 87.3%, dollar strengthens, BTC falls to $74,826 (▼1.3%). The macro headwind installs. Day three (Wednesday, Veterans Day): Bond market closed — equities recover on AI earnings, but BTC falls anyway (to $73,937, ▼1.2%). Crypto decouples from the equity recovery, demonstrating that it is processing its own headwinds independently. The ETF outflow data confirms: fourth session of institutional de-risking. Day four (Thursday): The bond market reopens. The 10-year yield ticks up to 4.99%, resuming the rate pressure that Wednesday’s holiday had paused. Jensen Huang resigns amid a spy scandal — a shock to the AI hardware sector that is also the AI-tech national-security story of the year, raising uncertainty across technology risk assets. BTC falls again to $73,208 (▼1.0%). The pressures are not independent events. They are the same underlying drivers — inflation expectations, dollar strength, rate outlook, institutional risk appetite — manifesting in different forms on each day. The structure of the correction is now established enough to identify: this is a regime shift, not a pullback, and the conditions for reversal require at least one of those drivers to change direction. None has changed direction this week.

The US bond market reopened on Thursday after Wednesday’s Veterans Day closure, with the 10-year Treasury yield ticking up 2 basis points to 4.99% — resuming the rate-pricing session that Tuesday’s hot October CPI data had established, with December 25-basis-point Fed rate-hike odds holding at approximately 87.3% — and removing the structural relief valve that the holiday closure had provided to both equities and crypto on Wednesday; for crypto specifically, the 10-year yield’s move toward 5.0% matters for two reasons: it strengthens the dollar (Bitcoin is priced in dollars, and dollar strengthening mechanically reduces its spot value), and it raises the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding alternative assets versus Treasuries that now offer 5% — a level that continues to remove marginal buyers from crypto; the bond market’s reopening at 4.99% confirms that Tuesday’s CPI-driven rate repricing is not a one-day event but a durable level reflecting the bond market’s assessment of inflation trajectory into the December FOMC decision on December 11.

The 10-year at 4.99% is relevant for crypto in a way it is not fully reflected in crypto market commentary: at 5%, a risk-free dollar-denominated asset that returns 5% annualized makes the opportunity cost of holding BTC — which yields nothing, has declined 3.5% this week, and faces an uncertain regulatory and macro environment — measurable for the first time in years. The comparison was not meaningful at 1-2% yields. At 5%, it is. That changes the marginal institutional buyer calculation.
