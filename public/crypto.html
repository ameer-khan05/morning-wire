The Anchor · The $55K Low — What Bitcoin’s Break Below Every Support Level and Saturday’s Rebound Tell You About the Equity-Correlated Crash and Its Recovery Conditions

Bitcoin hit $55,106 on Friday. It is now at $60,284 on Saturday morning. Neither price is the signal. The relationship between the two prices is. Bitcoin’s crash to $55,106 and its rebound to $60,284 follow a specific mechanical pattern that is worth reading carefully. The low came when Nvidia fell 14% and the Nasdaq dropped 5.28% — a risk-off equity event that liquidated levered crypto long positions at scale. $1.42 billion in Bitcoin long futures were cleared in 24 hours. When a crash is driven primarily by forced liquidations of levered positions — rather than by genuine selling pressure from holders who chose to exit — the technical bottom tends to be followed by a rebound as the liquidation cascade exhausts itself. The $60,284 Saturday rebound has that profile: it came after the $1.42 billion in longs cleared, not because of new positive catalysts. What it does not resolve is the structural November pressure that has been driving the outflow streak since the month began. US Bitcoin funds lost $124.5 million more on Friday — that is not liquidation behavior, that is institutional redemption behavior. The liquidation rebound and the outflow streak are two separate dynamics operating simultaneously. The rebound can hold at $60,000 and the outflow streak can still continue. Recovery depends on Monday’s equity market reopening, the December FOMC outlook, and whether the institutional redemption behavior that has driven November’s $3.5+ billion in cumulative outflows finds a reason to reverse.
