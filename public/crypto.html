Bitcoin fell 0.7% to $80,634 on Thursday as PCE inflation data released in the morning reinforced Chairman Warsh’s December hike thesis and Alphabet’s 11.75% earnings-miss drove a risk-off equity session that weighed on all risk assets, partially reversing Wednesday’s FOMC hold relief rally and leaving Bitcoin below both its Wednesday close of $81,215 and the $82,500 daily close level that analysts identified as the threshold between a relief reversal and a confirmed directional recovery; Ethereum declined 1.6% to $2,322 as altcoins were broadly weaker in the macro-driven session, the total crypto market capitalization fell to approximately $2.66 trillion, and the dual headwind of hot PCE data and the worst Nasdaq session in a month confirmed that the post-FOMC relief bid was a single-session trade rather than the start of sustained recovery.

Thursday’s crypto session is the data-dependent resolution that Wednesday’s Warsh hold set up: PCE came in consistent with hawkish expectations, December hike probability stays elevated, and the ceiling on the post-FOMC relief rally that Warsh’s language established on Wednesday holds.

The Anchor · PCE Data Confirms the Warsh Ceiling — Thursday’s Inflation Reading Converts Wednesday’s Language Risk Into Realized Data Risk

The reason Wednesday’s Warsh hold was more durable than a single-session event is that it set up a data test. PCE came in Thursday and delivered that test’s answer. Warsh’s December language on Wednesday converted into data backing on Thursday. A language-only ceiling shifts if the next speaker is more dovish; a data-backed ceiling only shifts if the next data reading is materially different. PCE being consistent with the hawkish environment means the ceiling is now sticky. Bitcoin’s 0.7% decline from the Wednesday relief-rally peak is the market pricing exactly that: the full FOMC bounce does not survive into a confirming PCE print. BTC at $80,634 is back near Monday’s pre-FOMC level — the round trip is complete. Ethereum’s slightly larger 1.6% decline is typical when the macro driver is rate repricing rather than a BTC-specific catalyst: institutional risk-reduction hits Bitcoin first, then spreads to alts.

BTC oscillated $80,021–$81,215–$80,634 in three days without testing either boundary. The next move requires a materially dovish data reading to lower the ceiling or a risk-on catalyst strong enough to push through $82,500 despite it.

Ethereum declined 1.6% to $2,322 on Thursday — a slightly larger percentage move than Bitcoin’s 0.7% — as the dual headwind of PCE-confirmed December rate-hike expectations and the macro risk-off session compounded Ethereum’s existing protocol-specific drag from the Glamsterdam upgrade’s continued absence of an activation date; altcoins were broadly weaker across the session, with the total crypto market capitalization falling to approximately $2.66 trillion, reversing the $2.71 trillion level established during Wednesday’s FOMC relief rally.

ETH’s larger percentage decline is a structural pattern in macro-driven sessions: institutional risk reduction hits BTC first (more liquid), then spills to ETH. Glamsterdam’s missing activation date adds protocol-specific drag on top of the same macro headwind.

PCE inflation data released Thursday came in consistent with the hawkish data environment Chairman Warsh described in his Wednesday press conference, confirming his December hike signal as data-grounded rather than rhetorical positioning; the 10-year Treasury yield held at 5.01% rather than declining on the PCE release, indicating that the bond market had already priced the hawkish reading and that Thursday’s data did not provide the materially softer inflation reading that would have been required to shift December hike expectations lower.

A language ceiling can be revised by a softer speaker; a data ceiling requires a softer print. Thursday’s PCE converts the ceiling from the former to the latter — Bitcoin’s 0.7% response is the correct pricing of that shift, not an overreaction.
