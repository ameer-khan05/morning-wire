The Anchor · Crypto’s Three-Day Slide — When the Macro and the Technical Both Point the Same Way

Three consecutive days of Bitcoin losses during a period when equities are both falling (Tuesday) and recovering (Wednesday) is the clearest possible evidence that Bitcoin is not moving with risk assets right now — it is moving with its own headwinds, and those headwinds have not changed direction. The macro headwind: the October CPI report pushed December Fed rate-hike odds to 87.3% on Tuesday, strengthening the dollar and reducing the attractiveness of dollar-denominated alternative assets. BTC fell 0.4% that day, 1.2% Wednesday. The dollar is working against crypto at the margin. The structural headwind: spot BTC and ETH ETF outflows have continued for four consecutive sessions now, with $201 million exiting BTC ETFs and $185 million exiting ETH ETFs on November 9 alone — institutional capital that entered crypto via ETF infrastructure is now leaving through the same mechanism. When ETF holders sell, they sell at the market, and four consecutive days of institutional selling is a regime signal, not a noise event. The technical headwind: Ethereum entering its third consecutive session below the 200-day moving average converts a two-day technical confirmation into an established downtrend. Most systematic trend-following strategies have now fully exited their ETH positions or are actively shorting. The organic selling, the institutional outflows, and the systematic selling are all adding simultaneously — a compound pressure that explains why ETH fell 2.5% on a day when equities are rebounding on AI earnings. These are independent mechanisms now converging.

Ethereum dropped 2.5% to $2,055 on Wednesday — its third consecutive session below the 200-day moving average — converting Tuesday’s two-day technical confirmation into an established downtrend that most systematic trend-following strategies treat as a full sell signal, adding mechanical selling on top of the organic and institutional outflow pressure already weighing on ETH; Ethereum has now fallen approximately 4.3% over three sessions, underperforming Bitcoin’s 2.4% cumulative decline over the same period, with the ETH/BTC ratio continuing to slide and reflecting the market’s sustained judgment that ETH carries higher regulatory and structural risk relative to Bitcoin; $185 million exited ETH spot ETFs on November 9 alone, and four consecutive sessions of ETH ETF outflows compound the picture: institutional capital that entered through regulated ETF structures is now exiting at an accelerating pace.

Three sessions below the 200-day moving average is not just technical — it changes who is holding ETH. After one session below, holders waiting for a bounce remain. After three sessions, systematic funds whose models require exit after confirmed downtrends have mostly completed their sales. The position base is now skewed toward longer-term holders and speculative longs; the pressure to sell is now skewed toward forced sellers (MARA, leveraged positions) and new shorts. That is a different market structure than the one that existed Monday.

The New York Stock Exchange announced plans to issue tokenized shares on the Avalanche blockchain under its NYSE Exchange Coin framework on Wednesday — a move that makes the NYSE the first major US securities exchange to commit to issuing tokenized equity on a public blockchain infrastructure, selecting Avalanche over competing L1 and L2 networks based on its throughput, finality speed, and regulatory compliance architecture; the NYSE Exchange Coin framework will allow institutional investors to hold, transfer, and settle NYSE-listed equities as blockchain tokens, with the same regulatory protections as traditional share ownership but with settlement finality measured in seconds rather than T+1 days; the announcement arrives during a week of broad crypto price weakness, demonstrating that institutional blockchain adoption is running on a structural timeline independent of speculative crypto price cycles — NYSE chose Avalanche because of its infrastructure characteristics, not because AVAX’s price was high.

NYSE on Avalanche is the institutional adoption event that the crypto ecosystem has been waiting for — not a hedge fund buying BTC, but the world’s largest securities exchange building its tokenized equity infrastructure on a public blockchain. The choice of Avalanche over Ethereum for this use case will be one of the more-discussed infrastructure decisions of the year. It signals that enterprise blockchain adoption is evaluating chains on throughput and finality, not on ecosystem size alone.

MARA Holdings, one of the largest publicly traded bitcoin mining companies, sold bitcoin holdings on Wednesday to repay outstanding loan obligations — adding forced selling pressure to a market already experiencing sustained spot ETF outflows and three consecutive days of organic price declines; MARA’s sale is the most visible sign this week of leverage unwinding across the BTC ecosystem: mining companies and other bitcoin-collateralized borrowers who took on debt at higher BTC price levels are now meeting margin requirements or servicing debt by liquidating BTC at current prices rather than waiting for a recovery; the pattern of miners selling BTC to repay loans rather than holding for a price recovery indicates that the debt service obligations are time-sensitive rather than optional, confirming that the pressure is structural rather than speculative; in the same week, Democrats winning Congress means that Senator Elizabeth Warren, who becomes chair of the Senate Banking Committee in January, will oversee crypto regulation — a development that multiple crypto analysts cited as a headwind for the digital asset market through at least mid-2027.

MARA selling bitcoin to repay loans is not unusual by itself — mining companies regularly manage treasury exposure. What makes it notable this week is the context: forced selling during a period of sustained ETF outflows and downward price momentum compounds each of those pressures simultaneously. The leverage unwind that arrives when prices fall below debt-service-comfortable levels is the mechanical selling that converts a correction into a prolonged downtrend.
