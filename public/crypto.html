The Anchor · BitMEX Closes — The Exchange That Invented the Instrument That Defined This Week’s Bitcoin Market

BitMEX’s closure at 04:00 UTC Wednesday is the end of the exchange that invented the perpetual swap — the derivative that underlies virtually all leveraged crypto trading globally and that produced Tuesday’s $634 million Bitcoin short squeeze in one hour at Binance, Bybit, and other exchanges that adopted BitMEX’s innovation. The perpetual swap is not closing. Only the inventor is. The significance of the coincidence between BitMEX’s closing day and Xi Jinping’s arrival in Washington is not primarily symbolic. It is structural. BitMEX launched in 2014 as a professional derivatives venue that believed institutional-grade crypto leverage products would eventually be needed; it was right. The perpetual swap — invented by Arthur Hayes and his team in 2016 — solved the problem of providing Bitcoin exposure at leverage without the expiry mechanics of traditional futures, and the product was copied by every major exchange within five years. The $634 million short squeeze that ran through Bitcoin on Tuesday was a perpetual-swap event: it required no market makers, no exchange-specific functionality, and no BitMEX involvement. The instrument Hayes built now operates on the biggest exchanges in the world without him. BitMEX’s market share fell from above 50% in 2018–2019 to 0.08% at closure — not because its product failed but because its product succeeded so completely that it was replicated everywhere. That is an unusual form of obsolescence: killed by your own invention’s adoption. Bitcoin at $85,600 on Wednesday — near 8-month highs following Tuesday’s post-squeeze high of $87,250 — is the market holding its position while waiting for Thursday’s Trump-Xi bilateral. The post-FOMC recovery stands at +14.8% from last Wednesday’s $75,972 intraday low, a recovery that has cleared two rounds of accumulated short interest in consecutive days and left Bitcoin’s short structure at a post-FOMC minimum. What happens next depends almost entirely on whether the summit produces a constructive outcome: tariff relief or AI safety framework would validate the risk-on thesis that has been buying Bitcoin through every resistance level this week; a disappointing bilateral tests the organic demand that remains after the forced buying is done. $84,000 — the resistance level that was cleared twice in two days — is the support that matters. Ethereum at $2,725 holding above $2,700 is the ETH equivalent: the post-squeeze consolidation finding a technical floor at a round number that had been resistance one week ago.

BitMEX permanently ceased all operations at 04:00 UTC Wednesday, ending an 11-year run that began in 2014 with Arthur Hayes, Ben Delo, and Samuel Reed founding the exchange and culminated in 2016 with the invention of the perpetual swap — a derivative product so commercially successful that it was copied by every major crypto exchange globally and now drives the majority of leveraged trading volume at Binance, Bybit, Deribit, and CME. The closure was orderly: all open positions had been force-closed in advance, user assets remained safe, and the final operational shutdown proceeded at the announced deadline with no extensions.

BitMEX’s market share trajectory tells the full story of what happened: above 50% globally in 2018–2019, then declining as Binance, Bybit, and others replicated the perpetual swap at lower fees and with broader asset support, reaching 0.08% at closure — not because BitMEX’s product failed but because it succeeded so completely that competitors made it obsolete by adopting it. The $100 million Bank Secrecy Act penalty in January 2025 — following a 2024 guilty plea on AML deficiency charges — removed the path to regulatory rehabilitation that might have sustained a business with 0.08% market share. Tuesday’s $634 million Bitcoin short squeeze was a perpetual-swap event that ran entirely through BitMEX’s competitors. The inventor closed the next morning.
