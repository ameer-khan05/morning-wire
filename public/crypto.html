The Anchor · The 24‑Hour Mirror — What BTC’s $3,530 Round-Trip from Monday to Tuesday Says About Equity‑Correlated Crypto Rallies

Bitcoin closed Monday at $63,988. It opened Tuesday at roughly the same level. By Tuesday afternoon it was at $60,458. That is a $3,530 move in one direction, one day after a $3,000 move in the other direction. Neither move was driven by Bitcoin. The Bitcoin round‑trip — from $55,106 on November 20 to $63,988 on November 23 to $60,458 on November 24 — is a precise expression of what equity‑correlated crypto looks like in a week defined by a single equity event. Nvidia’s governance crisis started the selloff. Nvidia’s governance resolution started the rally. Nvidia’s partial retracement started the reversal. Bitcoin did not start any of these moves. It amplified them. The 5.52% daily decline on a day when the Nasdaq fell 1.57% is the amplification ratio in action: Bitcoin moves approximately 3.5x the Nasdaq’s daily percentage on correlated days. That ratio is not new information. What Tuesday adds to the picture is the speed of the reversal: the equity‑driven rally lasted exactly one day before the equity‑driven decline took back more than half of it. For anyone holding Bitcoin because of the AI trade, Tuesday is a demonstration that the AI trade in crypto works in both directions at high speed. For anyone holding Bitcoin because of its independent store-of-value thesis, Tuesday is a demonstration that the independent thesis is not driving the price in November. The Hormuz crisis — which sent Brent crude up 4.60% to $90.64 and produced an identifiable risk‑off signal — added pressure but was not the primary driver. Bitcoin’s safe‑haven thesis had already failed to materialize on Monday when Iran’s tanker mine attacks began. It did not emerge on Tuesday either.
