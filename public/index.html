Oxford, Newcastle, and Cambridge researchers solved a 30-year mystery behind certain inflammatory bowel disease cases, finding that carriers of the HLA-DRB1*01:03 genetic variant are substantially more likely to develop antibodies that block IL-10 — the gut’s principal anti-inflammatory signaling protein — explaining for the first time how a known genetic risk factor produces severe intestinal inflammation; a separate study identified a small cluster of brain cells that prevents dangerous blood pressure swings even when average readings appear normal, revealing a hidden cardiovascular risk mechanism with direct implications for patients whose readings fall within clinical normal ranges.

The marker was associated with IBD risk for 30 years without a known mechanism. Anti-IL-10 antibody blockade as the pathway gives researchers both a diagnostic marker — test carriers for the antibodies — and a therapeutic target in one result.
