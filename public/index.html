Sydney → Circulation Research → world-first → cardiomyocytes regenerate in humans after heart attack → overturns medical consensus → proteins identified → lab model developed → not fast enough alone, but therapeutically targetable · Longevity (Sep 11): Maria Branyas (117+) → advanced aging + powerful biological protection simultaneously → two opposing forces required → reframes longevity pharmaceutical target · Prostate cancer: two-drug combo → reverses cellular identity changes → slows tumor growth (preclinical)

A world-first study in Circulation Research confirmed for the first time in humans that heart muscle cells regenerate after a heart attack — overturning the medical consensus that cardiac damage is permanent — with University of Sydney researchers identifying the proteins driving the process and developing a living tissue sample model for therapeutic screening; the natural regeneration is too weak to replace all lost cells but the mechanism is now confirmed and characterizable. A study of Maria Branyas, who lived past 117, found that extreme longevity requires two biological processes simultaneously: advanced aging at the cellular level and powerful protective mechanisms counteracting it — the two forces in opposition, not one absent. A two-drug combination reversed the cellular identity changes that allow prostate cancers to evade treatment, slowing tumor growth in preclinical experiments.

Three overturned assumptions: hearts regenerate, extreme longevity requires powerful protection against aging (not slower aging), prostate cancer's evasion mechanism can be reversed. None delivers an immediate treatment — but all three convert assumptions that blocked inquiry into tractable problems. Before today, those assumptions made the path from hypothesis to therapy undefined.
