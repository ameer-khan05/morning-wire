Bitcoin fell 2.49% to $83,462 and Ethereum declined 2.86% to $2,647 as rate hike expectations mounted sharply after New York Fed President Williams’s hawkish signal — with traders now pricing 75.3% odds of an October rate hike and 58.6% for December — while the Senate’s rejection of the CLARITY Act removed the near-term prospect of regulatory clarity for crypto assets, adding a regulatory headwind to the macro pressure from rising rates and a stronger dollar. The post-FOMC recovery that had brought Bitcoin from $75,972 to $87,250 over the prior week now stands at roughly +9.9%.

The double headwind of rate hike fears and CLARITY’s failure is the combination crypto traders were specifically concerned about. Rising rates increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding digital assets; absent a regulatory framework, institutional allocators remain constrained in the size and structure of positions they can hold. The Senate bill’s failure does not prohibit crypto activity — it simply leaves digital asset categorization unresolved, which is the friction that institutional capital needs cleared to deploy at scale.

All major US equity indices fell Thursday as the 10-year Treasury yield spiked to 5.11% — its highest level since 2007 — after New York Fed President Williams said another rate hike by year-end was “reasonable,” triggering a global bond sell-off that pushed Japan’s 10-year yield to its highest since 1996, with the S&P 500 shedding 0.66% to 7,706, the Nasdaq declining 0.54% to 26,936, the Dow losing 0.74% to 51,512, and the Russell 2000 falling the hardest at 1.55% to 2,862 as rate-sensitive small-caps bore the brunt of the yield spike.

The Russell 2000’s 1.55% fall versus the Nasdaq’s 0.54% is the precise inverse of Wednesday’s relationship — when small-caps outperformed while large-caps waited on the summit — and reflects exactly how rate hike expectations land differently across capitalization categories. Small-cap companies carry more floating-rate debt and less pricing power to pass through higher borrowing costs; when rate hike probability spikes, the Russell sells off harder. Williams’s signal reversed Wednesday’s Russell outperformance in a single session.

NASA’s Roman Space Telescope successfully activated its 300-megapixel infrared camera, a landmark that makes Roman capable of capturing Hubble-quality images across fields of view 100 times larger than Hubble and enables the most sensitive dark matter and dark energy surveys in astronomical history. Scientists separately reported that Ariel, one of Uranus’s moons, likely harbored a subsurface ocean more than 100 miles deep beneath its fractured icy shell at some point in its past — adding another candidate to the roster of outer solar system bodies with evidence of ancient liquid water. Stem cell transplants regenerated stroke-damaged brain tissue and restored motor function in mice, and a Swedish study of 3.7 million people linked rare TORCH infections in pregnancy to substantially higher risks of autism and intellectual disability in offspring.

Roman’s 300-megapixel camera changes the class of cosmological questions that are answerable: wide-field statistical surveys of dark matter distribution, dark energy behavior, and planetary transit populations that are impractical with Hubble’s narrow field become routine with Roman. The Ariel ocean finding arrives as missions to the outer solar system compete for funding — evidence of a subsurface ocean in Uranus’s second-largest moon strengthens the case for a dedicated Uranian system mission beyond the Voyager 2 flyby data that remains the only in-situ reference.
