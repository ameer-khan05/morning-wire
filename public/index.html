Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 — the 2.8-trillion-parameter coding model that topped the Arena.ai leaderboard on its July 17 API launch, ranked fourth on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index ahead of Claude Opus 4.8, and introduced architecture delivering 6.3x faster long-context decoding — releases full open weights on Hugging Face today under Modified MIT license, becoming the largest open-weight model ever released; the release is contested by the White House, whose OSTP Director accused Moonshot on July 22 of executing large-scale covert distillation of Anthropic's Fable model, with Treasury Secretary Bessent threatening Entity List designation and sanctions; 25 US tech companies including Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, and Palantir signed an open letter July 24 defending open-weight AI against premature restrictions.

The White House allegation — that Moonshot developed a platform to switch API access methods to evade detection while distilling Fable, and separately accessed banned NVIDIA GB300 chips through Thailand — is the first direct government accusation of frontier-model IP theft at scale by a Chinese AI lab. No enforcement action was announced as of the K3 weight release this morning. The 25-firm open-weights letter, signed three days before the release, was the industry's preemptive attempt to establish the principle that downloadable open-weight models are US competitive advantage before enforcement was announced. China's Ministry of Commerce simultaneously began consulting domestic AI firms on export controls that could restrict future weight downloads — a reciprocal move that could bifurcate the global open-weight AI ecosystem.

The ethics resolution is the specific drafting gap that three days of weekend negotiations could not close without White House involvement. SORLA Protein Shields Against Alzheimer's Tau Tangles — Sanford Burnham Prebys, Science Advances · Extra SORLA: Less Tau Accumulation, Less Brain Atrophy, Healthier Neuron Connections · LASSS Compound Supercharges Muscle Repair in Aging · Mineral Sunscreen Without White Cast SORLA SORLA (Sanford Burnham Prebys, Science Advances): extra SORLA → suppresses tau tangles + less brain atrophy + healthier neurons in Alzheimer's models · also suppresses amyloid-beta · dual-target candidate · LASSS compound: boosts muscle repair protein in aging · zinc oxide sunscreen: redesigned particles, no chalky cast

Researchers at Sanford Burnham Prebys published Science Advances research showing that the SORLA protein — already documented to suppress amyloid-beta generation — also suppresses tau tangle formation, reduces brain atrophy, and preserves healthy neuron connections in Alzheimer's mouse models; mice with additional SORLA showed significantly less tau accumulation and cognitive decline, while mice lacking SORLA experienced more severe damage, establishing SORLA as a dual-target therapeutic candidate against both of Alzheimer's hallmark pathologies. Separately, a sulfur-based compound called LASSS was found to supercharge a key protein involved in muscle repair in aging adults, and researchers redesigned zinc oxide particles to create a mineral sunscreen providing full UV protection without the chalky white cast that limits use in darker skin tones.

SORLA's dual-mechanism profile is the finding's clinical significance. Most Alzheimer's drug candidates have targeted either amyloid-beta or tau individually, and the field's high Phase III failure rate has been attributed partly to single-pathway approaches that don't address the disease's compound biology. SORLA's effectiveness against both pathways in mouse models makes it a target for approaches that could attack both hallmarks simultaneously: gene therapy to boost SORLA expression, SORLA-mimetic compounds, or small molecules that stabilize endogenous SORLA levels. The translation gap from mouse models to human trials remains the open question — both the amyloid and tau fields have documented strong mouse-model results that did not reproduce in human clinical populations. SORLA adds a new candidate to an unusually active Alzheimer's pipeline, arriving after a period in which amyloid-clearing antibodies achieved the first clinical approvals in years.
