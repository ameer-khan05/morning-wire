Monday's "deal on the verge" became Tuesday's "Iran must compensate for all casualties" in under 24 hours — the most dramatic single-day reversal of Hormuz diplomacy since the conflict began. The Trump compensation demand is qualitatively different from prior US conditions: blockade withdrawal and sanctions relief are negotiable; retrospective compensation for proxy-war casualties spanning decades is not a deal term in any standard conflict-resolution framework. It is either a maximalist opening bid designed to trade down to a practical condition, or it is a political-domestic signal not intended to be taken as a literal negotiating condition. The oil market's 4.2% move suggests traders are reading it as the former — a bid that will trade down — rather than the latter, which would have produced an even larger move. If the casualty-compensation demand is a bid, it creates space for a counter that looks like Iranian concession; if it is a genuine condition, the August 17 UNSC vote is irrelevant because no UNSC resolution can impose retroactive war reparations. Tomorrow's CPI will determine whether the oil spike complicates the inflation picture before the geopolitical picture can clarify. These two variables — the Hormuz deadlock and the CPI print — are the week's two defining events, and neither is resolved yet.
