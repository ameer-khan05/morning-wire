Sleep EEG Predicts Dementia Risk — ML Analysis of 7,105 Adults: 10-Year Brain-Age Gap = 40% Higher Risk · Vanderbilt: First TAOK-1 Inhibitor for Alzheimer's · Obesity-Alzheimer's Pathway Identified · AQP4 Gene Modulates Sleep-Dementia Link Individually +40% Sleep EEG brain age: ML analysis of 7,105 adults across 5 cohorts → 10-year gap = 40% higher dementia risk · non-invasive pre-symptomatic biomarker · Vanderbilt: first selective TAOK-1 inhibitor compound for Alzheimer's · obesity-Alzheimer's biological pathway: new mechanistic research · AQP4 gene: personalized sleep-dementia risk modulation

A pooled analysis of 7,105 adults across five community cohorts found that machine-learning-derived "brain age" scores calculated from overnight sleep electroencephalography predict dementia risk years before clinical symptoms appear — a 10-year gap between a person's biological brain age and chronological age corresponding to a 40% increase in dementia risk, in what researchers describe as the first large-scale validation of sleep EEG as a non-invasive pre-symptomatic dementia screening tool. The study analyzed 13 microstructural features of sleep brain waves including sleep depth and memory-consolidation spindles, offering a home-compatible test that could expand dementia detection into primary care settings. Vanderbilt researchers separately announced the first selective compound designed to inhibit TAOK-1, a protein linked to Alzheimer's that was previously considered difficult to target.

New research also uncovered a specific biological pathway connecting obesity to Alzheimer's disease, providing a mechanistic link between two of the most prevalent chronic conditions globally. A separate study found that individuals carrying certain variants of the AQP4 gene — which governs the brain's waste-clearance system active during sleep — show accelerated grey matter loss with shorter sleep, indicating that dementia risk from poor sleep is not uniform but genetically personalized. Together these findings position sleep quality, body weight management, and genetic screening as an integrated dementia-prevention framework.
