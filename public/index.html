Scientists Solve 50-Year Blood Type Mystery: New MAL Blood Group System · Reading Slows Brain Aging in Older Adults · 87-Trial Review: 4–6 Daily Whole-Grain Servings Cut Heart Risk MAL MAL blood group (Sep 19 2026): AnWj antigen → 1972 mystery → solved → MAL gene → 6,646-bp deletion → Mal protein: not produced → new blood group system → transfusion safety → NHS Blood + Bristol · Reading (Sep 19 2026): memory + executive function → brain aging: slowed → older adults · Whole grains (Sep 19 2026): 4–6 servings/day → cardiovascular benefit → 87 clinical trials → large meta-review

Scientists at NHS Blood and Transplant and the University of Bristol solved a 50-year blood type mystery by identifying the genetic cause behind the rare AnWj antigen — a 6,646-base-pair deletion in the MAL gene that prevents cells from producing functional Mal protein — establishing the new MAL blood group system and opening the path to improved transfusion safety for patients with extremely rare blood types. A new study found that regular reading benefits memory and executive function in older adults and may slow brain aging. A large review of 87 clinical trials found that eating 4 to 6 servings of whole grains daily delivers meaningful cardiovascular benefits.

The MAL blood group discovery is a landmark in transfusion medicine: the AnWj antigen was first identified in 1972 when a pregnant woman’s blood showed an anomaly no one could explain, and for 50 years blood banks managed these patients without understanding the molecular basis. Knowing the MAL gene deletion is the cause allows for genetic screening and improved compatibility assessment. The reading and whole grains findings are notable precisely because they are accessible: in a week when aging medicine discussed proteins and blockbuster drugs, the data also support two interventions that require no prescription and carry no side-effect profile.
