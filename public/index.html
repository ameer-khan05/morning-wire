President Trump rejected extending the US-Iran ceasefire when asked by reporters Monday — allowing the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding to expire without renewal — and then threatened to attack Oman if it interferes with US Hormuz operations, removing both the diplomatic framework and the neutral mediation infrastructure that had managed the crisis since June. Iran responded through a senior official threatening a "fully offensive" military posture if diplomatic efforts fail. Hormuz enters Day 49 with no active ceasefire, no Oman back-channel safely assumed operational, and no successor framework under negotiation — the most acute diplomatic breakdown since the war began February 28. Markets priced the escalation: S&P 500 fell 0.18%, Dow 0.40%, VIX rose 3.83%, and gold hit $4,480.30 (+0.97%) at a new 2026 high; 10-year Treasury yields rose to 4.68% simultaneously — the stagflation-risk configuration of rising gold, rising yields, and falling equities that reflects inflation-through-oil fear more than deflationary growth concern.

Bitcoin was the day's notable exception to the risk-off selloff, surging to $64,059 (+1.56%) as the White House crypto summit produced a concrete legislative outcome: the administration agreed to the CLARITY Act ethics package — the conflict-of-interest rules around presidential crypto holdings that had frozen the bill by blocking nine Republican Senate holdouts — with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent declaring the Senate is now at the "1-yard line" on crypto market structure legislation. Goldman Sachs's acquisition of NEOS Investments for $2.25 billion (adding $1 billion in Bitcoin ETF exposure) and ETH staking reaching a record 34.4% of all supply signal continued institutional crypto adoption regardless of CLARITY's legislative outcome. On the tech front, OpenAI made its Daybreak frontier capabilities available through Amazon Bedrock, expanding enterprise distribution to the AWS ecosystem, and Microsoft launched more than a dozen AI models including MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, which outperformed Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI competitors on the CyberGym security benchmark.

The Islamabad MOU had two functions: it was a ceasefire, and it was a container — the diplomatic architecture inside which both sides could escalate their demands without escalating militarily, because the framework itself provided a structure for negotiation to continue even when the parties' positions were incompatible. The MOU's expiration removes both functions simultaneously: there is no ceasefire, and there is no container. The Oman threat removes the third component: the neutral facilitator. What remains is the deterrence calculation alone — two sides with the capability to inflict unacceptable damage on the other, with no communication channel, no ceasefire, and no mediation infrastructure. The market's VIX ▲3.83% and gold-yields-equities stagflation configuration is the rational response to that reality. Read against this backdrop, the CLARITY Act ethics breakthrough is an interesting anomaly: crypto's policy catalyst was strong enough to produce a 1.56% BTC rally on the same day that geopolitical escalation produced broad equity selloffs and a new gold record. The two events are genuinely independent — CLARITY's outcome does not depend on Hormuz, and Hormuz's resolution (or further deterioration) does not depend on CLARITY. But they share a September convergence: CLARITY September 14, FOMC September 16, and the Iran situation's trajectory all converge in the same month. Tuesday's divergence between BTC and equities is a preview of that September complexity — different asset classes pricing different catalysts on the same timeline.
