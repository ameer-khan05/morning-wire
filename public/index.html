Stanford researchers published the Alzheimer’s Resilience–Vulnerability Connectome — ARC — a resting-state fMRI system that predicts Alzheimer’s risk years before memory symptoms appear by analyzing brain connection patterns, with participants scoring more Alzheimer’s-like on the ARC showing faster memory decline and faster amyloid beta accumulation over time; and UCSF scientists developed B-SYNC CAR-T, genetically engineered immune cells that activate inside the brain to target two proteins on diffuse midline glioma tumor cells, eliminating the deadly childhood brain cancer in every mouse tested and providing lasting protection against recurrence — with the first human trial targeted for April 2027.

Both findings address the same clinical gap: identifying a brain disease early enough that intervention changes the outcome. Stanford’s ARC shifts Alzheimer’s detection to the asymptomatic phase; UCSF’s B-SYNC targets a childhood glioma whose median survival today is approximately one year.
