The first-ever Xi-Trump-Putin trilateral summit in Shenzhen concluded Thursday with a joint peace declaration affirming that nuclear war must never be fought, an agreement to boost global oil production and refined petroleum supply, proposals for UN reform, and a warning to the International Criminal Court not to “abuse its power” — but the final statement contained no mention of Ukraine or Iran despite Trump’s pre-summit statements that both would come up; Xi described the meeting as “positive, constructive, and fruitful;” Trump said the US, China, and Russia are “uniquely positioned” to reduce the world’s nuclear weapons stockpile; and Trump departed Shenzhen Thursday, with a press briefing planned for Friday.

The absence of Ukraine and Iran from the peace declaration is the summit’s most consequential outcome. The joint statement built a framework — great-power coordination on nuclear risk, energy supply, and global institutions — without resolving the two active conflicts that most of the world expected it to address. For Ukraine and for Iran’s neighbors, the summit’s significance is precisely what it chose to omit.

ISS National Lab announced three new biomedical research batches: Redpoint Oncology will evaluate antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) cancer therapies using patient-derived tumor organoids grown in microgravity; Stanford University and the Palo Alto Veterans Institute will study engineered human muscle tissue in zero gravity to identify sarcopenia treatments; and Wake Forest will continue maturing bioprinted liver tissue with blood vessels aboard the station; a sweeping analysis of GLP-1 receptor agonist clinical trials found that semaglutide and tirzepatide produce broadly consistent weight loss across age, race, and ethnicity with one exception: women consistently lose more than men; and a Cleveland Clinic study found that patients who switch from semaglutide to tirzepatide or proceed to bariatric surgery achieve the best two-year weight loss outcomes.

The ISS ADC cancer research leverages microgravity to grow tumor organoids in three-dimensional structures that more closely mimic human tumor behavior than flat cell cultures — a capability that could accelerate ADC development for cancers resistant to conventional treatment. The GLP-1 gender finding is clinically actionable: treatment protocols that do not account for sex-based response differences are leaving weight loss potential on the table for half of all patients.
