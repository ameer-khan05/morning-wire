Oman's role as Strait of Hormuz mediator carries specific weight: Oman has maintained diplomatic relations with Iran throughout every prior period of US-Iran tension and brokered multiple back-channel communications since 2013. Ocean Deoxygenation Accelerating — Oxygen Disappearing From Oceans, Lakes, Rivers at Alarming Rate · LHS 1140 b: First Confirmed Atmosphere on Rocky Habitable-Zone Planet · APOE2 Gene Protects Neurons via DNA Repair · Coffee Linked to Lower Liver Cancer Risk O₂ ▼ Ocean deoxygenation: oxygen disappearing from oceans, lakes, rivers at alarming rate · LHS 1140 b: first atmosphere confirmed on rocky habitable-zone exoplanet · APOE2: longevity gene reduces neuronal DNA damage · coffee: major study links to lower liver cancer + cirrhosis risk

A sweeping new study documents ocean deoxygenation accelerating across the world's oceans, lakes, rivers, and coastal waters — oxygen disappearing at rates threatening aquatic life and weakening the biological and chemical processes that regulate Earth's climate, including the carbon-fixing capacity of marine phytoplankton whose productivity depends on dissolved oxygen gradients; separately, astronomers confirmed the first atmosphere detected around a rocky planet inside another star's habitable zone — LHS 1140 b, a super-Earth orbiting a red dwarf — providing direct evidence that such planets can retain atmospheres against stellar-wind pressure, the key barrier to surface liquid water. The APOE2 longevity gene was found to protect brain cells by reducing DNA damage and improving neuronal stress recovery, and a major long-term study linked daily coffee consumption to meaningfully lower risks of liver cancer, cirrhosis, and liver disease mortality.

Ocean deoxygenation is the third concurrent marine stressor alongside warming and acidification, and the new study's documentation of accelerating rates across fresh and salt water alike is the first to capture the full scope of oxygen loss across all aquatic systems simultaneously. LHS 1140 b's detected atmosphere — analyzed through JWST transmission spectroscopy — is significant because red-dwarf habitable zones were previously thought hostile to atmosphere retention due to stellar flare activity. The finding reopens the question of atmospheric persistence around the most common class of habitable-zone planets in the galaxy.
