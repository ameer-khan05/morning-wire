By the Numbers
24%
Reduction in Alzheimer’s and dementia risk linked to bisphosphonate osteoporosis drugs in a study of 121,492 older adults published Monday in Alzheimer’s & Dementia — the largest study yet to identify a pharmacological connection between bone-density treatment and cognitive protection. Researchers in Hong Kong found that people on nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates had a 16% lower risk of developing Alzheimer’s compared to non-users, and 24% lower compared to those on other osteoporosis drug classes. The finding does not prove causation and needs confirmation in a clinical trial, but the magnitude of the association across 120,000 patients elevates it to the most consequential pharma-adjacent dementia finding of the year — arriving the same week that MIT confirmed pink noise during sleep boosts the brain’s own amyloid-clearing mechanism.

Goldman’s engineers-only restriction and Wall Street’s “selloff overdone” call are the same week’s contradictory signals: institutions are building governance frameworks that treat AI tools as role-specific rather than universal, and analysts are simultaneously arguing the regulatory risk to those same tools is lower than markets priced.
