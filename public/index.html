A study of nearly 90,000 adults found that those consuming the most Neu5Gc — a sugar molecule present in red meat and absent from human metabolism due to an ancient mutation in the CMAH gene — had a 63% higher risk of developing type 2 diabetes, with scientists suspecting the molecule triggers chronic inflammation as a foreign substance, potentially explaining a hidden mechanism linking red meat to metabolic disease beyond weight gain; separately, researchers discovered that male and female skin replenishes and repairs itself through fundamentally different cellular mechanisms, with female cells operating in coordinated groups while male cells act independently.

The Neu5Gc finding is significant because it identifies a specific molecular mechanism rather than a statistical association — the standard criticism of dietary epidemiology is that confounders explain the correlation, but a molecule that humans actively cannot produce, and that triggers an immune response when consumed, provides a plausible non-confounder pathway. The male-female skin repair difference carries implications for drug development: wound treatments calibrated on one biology may systematically underperform in the other.
