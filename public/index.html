Daraxonrasib earns the “breakthrough” label for a specific reason: it targets RAS mutations, the genetic driver of 90% of all pancreatic cancers, which were considered undruggable for four decades. A 60.7% tumor-shrinkage rate in a cancer where many patients see no response to chemotherapy at all is what oncologists mean by that word — not an improvement in median survival from 11 months to 14, but a treatment that attacks the disease at its genetic root.
