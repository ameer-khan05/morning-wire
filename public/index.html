The UAE publicly accused Iran of attacking two ADNOC-linked oil company vessels in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday — calling the strikes "piracy" and a direct threat to global energy security — as the crisis enters Day 46 with the UNSC vote scheduled for Sunday. The two vessels bring total targeted since the conflict began to 16; no injuries were reported, but the attack involves the UAE for the first time as a direct victim: the Gulf's most commercially cautious state, which has historically maintained a carefully managed relationship with Tehran, has now publicly accused Iran of attacking its state oil company's vessels — a threshold that will reshape the UNSC diplomatic context Sunday.

The Oman-mediated near-deal framework (Iran's condition: US ends military operations before reopening) and the Joint Chiefs' back-channel communication with Iranian military counterparts — both established this week — remain the most viable off-ramp. But the UAE attack changes the Gulf political geometry: Russia and China must recalculate the cost of blocking a resolution that could help a Gulf state they want as a commercial partner, and Iran must assess whether attacking UAE state oil company assets is compatible with negotiating in good faith through Oman. Sunday's UNSC vote is the most consequential diplomatic event in the 46-day crisis.

Stanford/Nature: Blood Immune Cells Flood Aging Human Brain — Uniquely Human, Begins Midlife, May Drive Alzheimer's Neuroinflammation · Estrogen-Only HRT Later in Life: Lower Dementia Risk (21,000+ Study) · Keratin from Wool Outperforms Collagen for Bone Regeneration Midlife Stanford/Nature: peripheral blood immune cells → migrate to aging brain → differentiate into microglia → uniquely human (absent mice/NHP) → begins ~age 50 → may explain Alzheimer's/Parkinson's neuroinflammation → "engineer peripheral immune cells to treat neurological diseases" · Estrogen-only HRT: 21,000+ study → lower dementia + fewer Alzheimer's brain markers · Keratin (wool): outperforms collagen for bone regeneration

Stanford researchers published findings in Nature showing that large numbers of peripheral blood-derived immune cells migrate into the aging human brain and differentiate into microglia — the brain's resident immune population — in a phenomenon uniquely absent in mice and non-human primates, beginning around age 50, and potentially explaining why neuroinflammation is so prevalent in late-life neurodegenerative diseases including Alzheimer's and Parkinson's. The discovery overturns the assumption that the adult brain's microglia are self-renewing from a fixed resident pool and opens what the researchers call "new opportunities to engineer peripheral immune cells to treat or prevent neurological diseases." A study of more than 21,000 women found estrogen-only hormone therapy used later in life was associated with lower rates of dementia and fewer Alzheimer's brain pathological markers — distinguishing estrogen-only from the combined estrogen-progestin therapy whose mixed outcomes dominated prior HRT and cognitive risk research.

Researchers separately found that keratin extracted from sheep's wool helped damaged bone regenerate tissue more structurally organized and similar to healthy bone than tissue grown with conventional collagen scaffolds — pointing toward a lower-cost biological scaffold alternative in orthopedic tissue engineering. The three findings span cellular mechanism discovery, population-scale clinical evidence, and biomaterial innovation — and all three revise prior models of how the underlying biological system works, the category of science that most directly reshapes therapeutic and policy approaches.
