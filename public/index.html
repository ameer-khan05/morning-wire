Pakistani and Qatari mediators presented Washington and Tehran with a formal 10-day ceasefire proposal on Day 25 of the Iran-US conflict, which would allow both sides to negotiate a longer-term Hormuz arrangement during the truce; as of Saturday morning neither side had accepted, with both seeking improved leverage before committing. Yemen's Houthis announced a formal maritime blockade targeting Saudi Arabia and claimed operational control of the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, opening a new front even as mediators pushed for an off-ramp. Egypt joined the mediation push, citing the threat to Red Sea–Suez Canal commercial traffic from an executed Bab al-Mandeb blockade.

The 10-day proposal is structurally more significant than the July 4 truce: it is designed as a negotiating framework with a longer-term Hormuz arrangement as the explicit goal, not just a pause. "Neither side accepting" is the preliminary positioning phase — the July 4 truce was also delayed 72 hours before it took effect. The Houthi blockade announcement simultaneous with ceasefire talks is the complicating variable: any durable framework must include a Houthi stand-down, or it resolves the Iran-US kinetic component while leaving a running Bab al-Mandeb crisis unaddressed. The 23-day nuclear clock is the constraint that gives the 10-day window its leverage.

Stanford: Natural Molecule Suppresses Appetite Like Ozempic — Without Side Effects · Vancomycin Revived Against Resistant Bacteria: pghi-4 Blocks Resistance Enzyme · Alzheimer's Sleep Loss: Brain's Own Immune Cells Are the Culprit · APOE2 Gene Protects Neurons Natural Stanford: naturally occurring molecule → appetite suppression + weight loss like GLP-1, without GI side effects · vancomycin + pghi-4: blocks resistance enzyme → antibiotic restored · Alzheimer's sleep: microglia cause sleep disruption · APOE2 gene: protects neurons from DNA damage

Stanford Medicine researchers identified a naturally occurring molecule that suppresses appetite and reduces body weight comparably to GLP-1 drugs like Ozempic, without the nausea, vomiting, and GI side effects that drive discontinuation — a finding that could reshape the obesity drug market if it advances to trials. Scientists separately revived vancomycin against resistant bacteria by pairing it with pghi-4, a small molecule that blocks the bacterial enzyme driving resistance, restoring clinical utility against gram-positive pathogens including MRSA. Researchers also identified the brain's own immune cells — microglia — as the surprising cause of sleep loss in Alzheimer's disease, opening a new therapeutic target distinct from tau and amyloid pathways.

The Stanford molecule finding matters most at the mechanism level: naturally occurring implies the body already produces and tolerates it, which typically means a faster path to understanding therapeutic doses and a lower baseline toxicity risk than a synthetic GLP-1 analog. GLP-1 side effects — primarily GI distress — are the leading driver of discontinuation in weight management programs; a molecule achieving comparable efficacy via a different mechanism directly addresses that gap. The vancomycin revival is a complementary strategy to new antibiotic development: restore existing drugs using resistance-blocking molecules, rather than starting from scratch on new drug design that takes a decade.
