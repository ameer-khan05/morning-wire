A sweeping review of more than 153,000 adults found that calcium and vitamin D supplements — alone or in combination — do little to prevent fractures or falls in most older people, directly challenging the widely held clinical assumption that underlies supplementation guidelines across most developed countries and the $3 billion supplement market premised on bone health. Common preservatives in processed foods are linked to substantially higher rates of high blood pressure and cardiovascular disease in new research. Researchers uncovered a molecular warning sign of inflammatory bowel disease that lingers in patients even when they are in clinical remission and feel well, offering a new monitoring tool for a disease that standard symptom-based assessment cannot fully track.

The calcium and vitamin D finding is significant at population scale: clinical guidelines in most developed countries recommend supplementation for older adults as standard fracture prevention. A 153,000-person review not finding that benefit puts those guidelines under direct pressure for revision. The food preservative finding adds a cardiovascular risk dimension to ingredients that appear in virtually every category of processed food. The IBD remission biomarker is the kind of result that changes clinical practice — objective evidence of subclinical disease activity when symptoms have resolved gives clinicians a tool that symptom assessment alone cannot provide.
