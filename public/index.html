Earth set two consecutive records for most anomalously hot day: November 20 reached 2.12°C above the pre‑industrial average and November 21 reached 2.17°C, breaking the previous record twice in 24 hours according to Copernicus Climate Change Service data, with temperature records shattering across northern Ontario and multiple monitoring stations; US measles cases climbed to 5,838 and deaths rose to 11 as Virginia reported the first measles‑linked death in a child under 5 in that state, adding a fourth state to the death toll; ISS crew prioritized advanced biomedical research Monday, sequencing microbe DNA, producing stem cells, and testing blood samples in microgravity — studies providing insights unobtainable on Earth; and a House panel issued its final report warning that China’s biotech dominance is shifting drug development overseas, recommending BINSA passage and 11 additional policy reforms.

Earth hitting 2.17°C above pre‑industrial on November 21 — breaking the record it set the previous day at 2.12°C — is the kind of data point that climate scientists have been warning about for decades. The 1.5°C threshold was a policy target. Two degrees was a danger zone. 2.17°C on a single November day is evidence that the trajectory has moved past both thresholds on individual event terms, even as annual averages remain below them. The science is not ambiguous about what this means.
