OpenAI CEO Sam Altman appeared in person before the UN Security Council on Wednesday while Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei joined remotely in the first high-level AI briefing in UN Security Council history — convened by France, the Council’s September president, alongside Yoshua Bengio, Hugging Face’s Clément Delangue, and China’s DeepSeek and Moonshot — with Altman arguing for international coordination on shared evaluation benchmarks and mechanisms for exchanging AI threat information across governments. Amodei separately called for the AI industry to slow its development pace and announced Anthropic would offer independent evaluators permanent access to its models. Anthropic also launched a clinical AI partnership with OpenEvidence, giving US and European physicians free access to AI-powered clinical decision support.

The Security Council session is the governance complement to Thursday’s Trump-Xi bilateral: the UNSC hearing sets a 15-member multilateral reference frame for AI and international security on the same day China’s leader arrives to negotiate a bilateral US-China AI safety framework. The two tracks — multilateral UNSC framing and bilateral summit drafting — are running simultaneously for the first time, and the resulting frameworks will either reinforce each other or produce competing standards that other governments will have to choose between.

BitMEX permanently closed at 04:00 UTC Wednesday, ending its 11-year run as the exchange that invented the perpetual swap — the derivative that now underlies virtually all leveraged crypto trading globally at Binance, Bybit, Deribit, and CME — following a $100 million Bank Secrecy Act penalty in January 2025 and a market share collapse from above 50% in 2018–2019 to 0.08% at closure. Bitcoin fell 1.90% to $85,600 as crypto markets tracked equities lower in cautious summit-day positioning, while Ethereum declined 4.81% to $2,725 but held above the $2,700 support level established through Tuesday’s surge.

BitMEX closes the day Xi Jinping lands in Washington — a coincidence of timing that is not without significance: the exchange that made leveraged crypto mainstream shuts down as the world’s two most powerful leaders begin the negotiations that will define the macro regulatory framework for the industry it helped build. The perpetual swap outlives the exchange that invented it; Tuesday’s $634 million short squeeze was a perpetual-swap event that ran entirely without BitMEX involvement. That is BitMEX’s legacy in one sentence.

Researchers have found evidence that long COVID damages dopamine-releasing neurons in the brain, potentially explaining the persistent fatigue, low motivation, slowed movement, and memory difficulties that characterize post-COVID syndrome — a mechanistic discovery that could direct new treatment development toward dopamine-pathway interventions for millions of affected patients. A large study found the cardiovascular benefits of GLP-1 drugs including Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound disappear within approximately two years of stopping treatment, with former users facing a 22% higher risk of heart attack, stroke, and death. Scientists also solved the 54-year mystery of the AnWj blood group antigen, identifying the MAL gene and establishing a new named blood group system.

The long COVID dopamine finding is significant not only for treatment development but for diagnostic credibility: dopamine neuron damage is a measurable, objective biological abnormality that establishes post-COVID fatigue as a neurological disease process rather than a psychosomatic complaint — a distinction that matters enormously for clinical recognition, insurance coverage, and research funding. The GLP-1 finding raises a structural question facing every healthcare system that has begun widespread prescribing: if the benefits reverse within two years of stopping, the drugs require indefinite use to maintain their cardiovascular effects, which transforms the cost-benefit calculus of the prescribing decision entirely.
