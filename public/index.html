The United States and Iran agreed to extend their 60-day ceasefire past its Monday August 17 expiry under the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding — keeping the Oman diplomatic framework operational as Hormuz enters Day 48 of closure. Iran disclosed that a deal had been agreed in principle through Oman, but tied any Hormuz reopening to the end of the US naval blockade operation entirely — a condition that requires the US to withdraw Operation Vela Nova's enforcement presence from the Strait's transit lanes, the mechanism that has held oil prices above $85 Brent throughout the closure.

The ceasefire extension prevents the most acute risk of conflict resumption since the war began February 28, but Iran's disclosed condition substantially raises the bar for a negotiated reopening. The Trump administration must now weigh maintaining Vela Nova — which has served as the oil market's price stabilizer — against satisfying Iran's minimum condition for reopening the world's most critical energy chokepoint. Trump's Friday "US territory" statement, still unretracted, remains in the diplomatic record as Iran calibrates its next move.

Aging Brain Opens to Blood Immune Cells From Middle Age — Rewrites Neural Isolation Model · Estrogen-Only HRT: Lower Dementia Risk + Fewer Alzheimer's Signs in 21,000-Patient Study · COVID-19 Reactivates Epstein-Barr + Cytomegalovirus — One Viral Family Strongly Linked to Long COVID Mid-Age Brain immunity: large numbers of blood immune cells enter brain from middle age → previously believed isolated → opens neuroinflammation, Alzheimer's research fronts · Estrogen-only HRT: 21,000+ patients → lower dementia risk + fewer Alzheimer's pathology signs · COVID-19: reactivates Epstein-Barr + cytomegalovirus → one viral family strongly associated with long COVID · Arctic permafrost: ancient carbon released to ocean

New research finds that the aging brain is far less immunologically isolated from the body than previously understood — large numbers of immune cells from the bloodstream begin entering the brain as early as middle age, a finding that rewrites the fundamental understanding of the brain's immune privilege and opens new research fronts in neuroinflammation and Alzheimer's disease. A separate study of more than 21,000 participants found that women who used estrogen-only hormone therapy later in life were significantly less likely to develop dementia and showed fewer signs of Alzheimer's pathology — the largest HRT-dementia study to confirm a protective association from estrogen-only therapy.

COVID-19 can reactivate dormant pathogens including Epstein-Barr virus and cytomegalovirus, with one viral family strongly associated with long COVID in findings that link reactivation events — not just SARS-CoV-2 directly — to persistent symptom burden. Thawing Arctic permafrost is releasing ancient carbon into the ocean, with researchers estimating roughly 10% may convert to greenhouse gases, a climate feedback loop with direct implications for warming trajectories. Exercise and healthy blood sugar levels in midlife may slow cognitive decline decades later, adding to evidence that cognitive aging is shaped by behaviors well before old age.
