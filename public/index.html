Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced sweeping new Iran sanctions Monday, declaring "at dawn begins an economic D-Day" in a Financial Times opinion piece that described the measures as "the single greatest financial offensive ever marshalled against an adversary." The package arrives as the 60-day ceasefire window formally closed without a deal, locking the Hormuz crisis into its conflict phase on Day 55; Iran simultaneously threatened to seize vessels violating Strait transit rules, adding an escalatory posture to the announcement. Separately, US-Canada trade talks collapsed Friday night — both sides citing contradictions between the written text and what Canada believed it had agreed to — with Prime Minister Carney calling the breakdown a "miscalculation" and 50% tariffs on billions of dollars of Canadian goods taking immediate effect. Canada matched the tariffs dollar for dollar.

The two developments are simultaneously unfolding maximum-pressure campaigns on different trade fronts. Iran's "economic D-Day" comes after the UAE's trade suspension removed Iran's second-largest commercial partner last week; Canada's rupture is an unprecedented breakdown with a country that shares the longest undefended border in the world and $2 trillion in annual bilateral trade. Iran has publicly warned neighboring countries not to join the US economic campaign, and Pezeshkian's statement that Iran "cannot continue with war forever" points to a split within the Iranian leadership about the sustainability of the current confrontational posture. The diplomatic geometry for the Hormuz crisis has narrowed further: the ceasefire window is formally closed, and the economic pressure track is now the primary instrument on Day 55.

AI Identifies DNA "Initiator" Gene-Switch in ~60% of Human Genes — 500K Sequences Analyzed · Decades-Old Compound Treats Obesity by Burning Energy (Not Suppressing Appetite) · Ozempic Activates Hunger-Linked Neurons — Opposite of Scientists' Expectation · Pancreatic Cancer: 21+ Teeth = Nearly 2 Years Longer Survival Post-Surgery 60% AI gene-switch: AI model scans ~500K DNA sequences → identifies "initiator" control element (gene activation switch) in ~60% of human genes → largest systematic mapping of a gene-activation mechanism → implications for understanding gene regulation, cancer, developmental disorders · Obesity compound: decades-old molecule → makes body burn more energy rather than reducing appetite → different from GLP-1/appetite-suppression pathway → potential complement or alternative to Ozempic-class drugs · Ozempic/GLP-1: activates hunger-linked neurons → opposite of expected mechanism → neurons appear essential for sustaining fat loss, not just suppressing appetite · Pancreatic cancer: 21+ teeth → ▲1.9 years median post-surgery survival vs. fewer teeth (339-patient study)

Researchers using AI to analyze approximately 500,000 DNA sequences have identified the "initiator" — a genetic control element involved in activating gene expression — in roughly 60% of human genes, representing the largest systematic mapping of a gene-activation mechanism to date. The findings have implications for understanding how genes are turned on in cancer, development, and disease, where initiator-element disruption has been suspected but not mapped at this scale. Separately, scientists announced that a decades-old compound may offer an obesity treatment pathway distinct from GLP-1 receptor agonists: rather than suppressing appetite, the compound appears to make the body burn more energy — a mechanistic distinction that could prove complementary to or, for some patients, more effective than appetite-suppression-based treatments.

Two additional findings reshape the understanding of existing therapies. Ozempic (semaglutide) appears to work in the brain in nearly the opposite way scientists expected: rather than suppressing hunger signals, it activates hunger-linked neurons that appear essential for sustaining fat loss — a mechanistic revision that may explain why the drug's weight-loss effects persist even when appetite suppression is incomplete. In oncology, a 339-patient study of pancreatic cancer surgery found that patients with at least 21 natural teeth survived nearly two years longer after surgery than those with fewer — a striking association pointing toward oral microbiome composition or systemic inflammation from tooth loss as variables in post-surgical outcomes for one of medicine's most lethal cancers.
