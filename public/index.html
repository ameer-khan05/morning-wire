Intellia Therapeutics released data showing its in-vivo CRISPR gene therapy lonvo‑z remains effective four years after a single treatment for hereditary angioedema, with the FDA’s Priority Review decision expected by March 2027 — it would be the first FDA-authorized therapy to edit genes inside the living body; a 113,000-person longitudinal study found cardiovascular disease warning signs — including elevated blood pressure and specific blood proteins — can appear 30 years before a cardiac event; researchers identified a new subtype of depression affecting approximately one in four people with severe, recurrent, or persistent depressive disorder; and a meta-analysis of 149 studies found cannabis users develop psychosis an average of 2.5 years earlier than non-users.

The CRISPR milestone and the heart disease study describe the same underlying shift in medicine: intervention is becoming earlier and more precise. Lonvo‑z edits a specific gene after a single treatment. The heart disease study identifies specific biomarkers three decades before an event. Both require surveillance systems and clinical capacity that the US healthcare system does not uniformly provide.
