NASA’s Roman Space Telescope now has enough fuel for at least 22 years of operation — more than double its original 10-year design life — extending the most powerful wide-field infrared telescope ever built into a multi-decade mission with implications for cosmology, dark energy research, and exoplanet detection. UCLA researchers developed ready-made cancer-fighting T cells from cord blood stem cells that can attack solid tumors through two separate detection systems, controlling cancer in mice with a single dose — a potential foundation for off-the-shelf immunotherapy. SpaceX announced a new $100 billion launch site that would launch thousands of rockets.

Roman’s 22-year fuel reserve is a mission-planning windfall: a telescope designed for 10 years with 22 years of operational capacity doubles the discovery window for every research program on its schedule and opens the door to observation campaigns that would not have been feasible under the original timeline. The UCLA T-cell therapy represents a significant step in “ready-made” cancer immunotherapy — one of the field’s most commercially significant frontiers — by using cord blood as a scalable donor source rather than patient-specific cells, which are expensive and time-consuming to manufacture. Semaglutide’s expanding therapeutic profile continues: 40% fewer asthma attacks and 20% fewer COPD flare-ups are clinically meaningful findings, though the 7% hair-loss risk in genetically susceptible men adds a new consideration to the drug’s benefit-risk calculus.
