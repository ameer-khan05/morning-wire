The Pentagon is in talks to lend approximately $5 billion to AI cloud startup Fluidstack through its Office of Strategic Capital to shore up the US AI data-center supply chain — the largest single OSC loan ever proposed, advised by Erebor Bank, the hard-tech national bank Palmer Luckey launched in February 2026. The WSJ reported the loan would fund domestic supply-chain and component manufacturing capacity for data centers rather than directly building new facilities; the OSC has expanded its lending authority from $984 million to more than $210 billion, making the $5 billion proposal both large and structurally achievable. OpenAI separately launched its Agents API in public beta Thursday, giving all API developers a managed Codex harness with session orchestration, context compaction, hosted sandbox infrastructure, and support for custom tools and MCP servers — no extra fee, billed through models used. The EU's GPAI systemic risk deadline is three days away, Monday September 15.

Two government-scale AI infrastructure moves in 48 hours: the Pentagon's $5 billion loan proposal treats AI data-center capacity as a strategic national resource requiring active government supply-chain management, while OpenAI's Agents API opening gives developers the same managed infrastructure that has been running Codex as a product since its launch. Both represent the industrialization of AI infrastructure: the commodity layer of AI is moving from research and enterprise tooling to national-security-grade strategic supply chains. The EU's compliance deadline Monday arrives at the moment the US government is writing its first major AI infrastructure lending facility.

The 18th BRICS Summit opened Saturday in New Delhi under India's chairship with Iranian President Pezeshkian attending the September 12-13 gathering — Iran's highest-profile multilateral diplomatic engagement since the February US-Israeli strikes — as Bloomberg frames it: "Iran turns to BRICS summit as US war deepens its isolation." The summit's internal tension is structural: the UAE, a BRICS member since 2024, suspended trade with Iran after August missile attacks, making two members of the same bloc active adversaries. India, hosting, must navigate a ceasefire discussion while not alienating the Gulf states whose energy trade it depends on. Sweden's Riksdag election is tomorrow, September 13, with the Red-Green bloc holding a polling lead of 50-52% against the Tidö coalition at 44-47%.

The BRICS summit's real geopolitical test is whether its member states can agree on language about the West Asia conflict that holds both Iran and the UAE as members without fracturing the bloc's stated consensus framework. The foreign ministers' meeting in May failed to agree on a joint communique; India issued a chair's statement instead. The summit's outcome document will face the same test at heads-of-state level. Sweden's election running on Sunday as the BRICS summit concludes Saturday is a parallel Western data point: if the Red-Green bloc's polling lead holds, the new government will face a sharply different European geopolitical environment than the current right-wing coalition managed.

Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy): 40% Fewer Asthma Attacks, 20% Fewer COPD Flare-Ups · UK Study ~80,000 Patients · ERS Congress Barcelona · Long-Lived Bats: Immune Defenses Unlock Decades of Disease Avoidance −40% Asthma Semaglutide (Sep 9-12 2026): UK study → ~80,000 patients (four groups ~20,000 each) → GLP-1 vs sulfonylureas comparison → asthma attacks: ~40% fewer → COPD flare-ups: ~20% fewer → ERS Congress Barcelona → ScienceDaily: "Ozempic and Wegovy linked to nearly 40% fewer asthma attacks" → GLP-1 respiratory mechanism: anti-inflammatory + weight reduction pathway · Bats (Sep 2026): long-lived species → immune defenses → damaged cell elimination strategies → decades of cancer/disease avoidance → longevity research implications

Semaglutide — the active compound in Ozempic and Wegovy — was linked to nearly 40% fewer asthma attacks and approximately 20% fewer COPD flare-ups in a large UK real-world study of roughly 80,000 patients across four groups, presented at the European Respiratory Society Congress in Barcelona; ScienceDaily headlines "Ozempic and Wegovy linked to nearly 40% fewer asthma attacks." Researchers compared GLP-1 users to patients on sulfonylureas and found significant respiratory benefits beyond the drug's established effects on diabetes and obesity, with anti-inflammatory mechanisms and weight reduction both cited as possible pathways, though the team called for clinical trials to confirm the observational finding. Long-lived bat species were separately found to combine powerful immune defenses with unusual strategies for eliminating damaged cells, potentially explaining how they avoid cancer and disease for decades.

The semaglutide respiratory finding joins a pattern of GLP-1 off-label benefits accumulating in observational data: cardiovascular protection, kidney outcomes, and now substantial reductions in asthma and COPD exacerbations. Each finding is observational and requires randomized trial confirmation, but the scale of the respiratory effect — 40% fewer asthma attacks is large for any intervention — and the biological plausibility of the anti-inflammatory and weight-loss pathways means the result will accelerate clinical trial planning. The bat longevity finding adds a model system: understanding how bats maintain immune competence while eliminating damaged cells across multi-decade lifespans identifies the specific biological mechanisms that longevity researchers need to target pharmacologically.
