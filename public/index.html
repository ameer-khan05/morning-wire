The Department of Defense opened GenAI.mil, a secure AI portal giving 3 million DoD personnel access to ChatGPT Mil, Grok for Government (xAI/Starshield), and Google Gemini — the largest single AI deployment in U.S. government history, with 1.7 million unique users already onboarded. Anthropic's Claude is notably absent, after the Trump administration flagged concerns about the company. The Senate simultaneously voted 99-1 to lift a 10-year federal moratorium on state-level AI regulation, a sweeping policy reversal that allows states to enact their own AI laws and dismantles the uniform federal framework that major AI companies had relied on to operate across state lines without a patchwork of local rules.

Runway's Solaris — announced August 31 — is the first model in a new category the company calls Interface World Models: AI that generates fully interactive interfaces in real-time, frame by frame, responding to clicks, drags, and typed instructions with no underlying code. The system is in early-access research with no public launch date. Wipro separately launched an enterprise AI operating model combining advisory, deployment, and transformation services — reflecting the market's shift from AI proof-of-concept to full operational deployment.

Sleep Apnea Pill AD109: 44% Breathing Interruption Reduction in Phase 3 Trial — FDA Fast-Tracked · Cancer Cells Build Sugar Shield to Evade Immune Detection · Microglia: 4-8 Years to Mature in Humans vs. Weeks in Mice · Sweeteners Persist 44% AD109 (OSA Phase 3): once-nightly pill → aroxybutynin + atomoxetine → breathing interruptions ▼44% → improves oxygen levels + disease severity → first neuromuscular OSA therapy → treats underlying cause, not symptoms → FDA fast-tracked → decision expected 2027 → cancer / immune evasion: cancer cells build thick glycocalyx (sugar-rich coating) → high blood sugar strengthens shield → immune cells cannot penetrate → microglia / brain: 4-8 years to mature in humans (vs weeks in mice) → impacts neurodevelopmental research → sucralose/stevia: physiological effects persist far beyond sweet taste

A Phase 3 trial of AD109, a once-nightly pill combining aroxybutynin and atomoxetine, cut breathing interruptions by approximately 44% in people with obstructive sleep apnea while improving oxygen levels and overall disease severity — earning FDA fast-track designation for what would be the first drug to treat OSA by targeting its underlying neuromuscular causes rather than managing symptoms through airway pressure. Obstructive sleep apnea affects approximately 1 billion people globally and has been treated almost exclusively with CPAP devices; AD109 addresses the condition at its physiological root. Separately, researchers discovered that cancer cells build a thick, sugar-rich coating that functions as a physical shield against immune cell penetration, with high blood sugar potentially strengthening the shield — creating a mechanistic link between metabolic disease and cancer immune evasion.

Human microglia — immune cells that shape developing brain circuits — take 4 to 8 years to fully mature, a dramatically longer timeline than the weeks the process takes in mice, new research found. The finding challenges the reliability of mouse models for studying neuropsychiatric and neurodevelopmental conditions that depend on microglial function, suggesting decades of preclinical research may have missed developmental windows that are critically important in human disease. Two widely used zero-calorie sweeteners, sucralose and stevia, were found to have physiological effects that persist far longer than the sweet taste, with potential gut microbiome and metabolic implications that researchers are still characterizing.
