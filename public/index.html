The American Heart Association’s Scientific Sessions 2026 closed Sunday at McCormick Place in Chicago with two cardiovascular findings: rural residents face nearly 3 times the risk of dying from a heart attack before reaching a hospital compared to urban residents, and retired NFL linemen have significantly elevated artery plaque risk compared to age-matched controls; separately, a University of California Santa Cruz study found psilocybin rewires the brain by reorganizing connections between nerve cells in ways that differ from prior expectations — findings that could inform non-hallucinogenic mental health treatments — and researchers excavating a 2,800-year-old Scythian tomb in Siberia found roughly 1,000 cannabis seeds with chemical evidence of ceremonial use, the earliest confirmed large-scale cannabis ritual in the archaeological record.

The rural heart attack finding is the AHA session’s most policy-consequential data point: a 3-times disparity in pre-hospital cardiac death by geography is a resource allocation and emergency medical services problem that is tractable — unlike genetic or behavioral risk factors, it can be addressed with infrastructure investment in rural EMS and defibrillator deployment.
