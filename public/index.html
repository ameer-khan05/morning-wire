Iran Day 31 — Conflict Expands Geographically: Egypt Struck at Damietta Port (First Time) · Iran Hits US Bases in Kuwait & Bahrain · Senate Rejects War-Powers Limit (Again) · 14 Nations Back Maritime Defense Alliance · Hamas Disarmament Deal Announced · Brent $88.97 ▲1.04% Day 31 Day 31: Egypt struck — drones hit Damietta port ships · Iran: Ahmad al-Jaber Air Base (Kuwait) + Sheikh Isa Air Base (Bahrain) struck · Senate: war-powers limit rejected again · 14 nations: Maritime Defense Alliance · Hamas disarmament deal · Brent $88.97 ▲1.04% · UNSC deadline: Aug 17 (17 days)

The US-Iran conflict entered Day 31 with its sharpest single-day geographic expansion yet: drone strikes hit two ships at Egypt's Damietta port — the Energos Winter storage vessel and the GasLog Salem tanker — drawing Cairo into the conflict for the first time; Iran simultaneously struck US military facilities at Ahmad al-Jaber Air Base in Kuwait, targeting aircraft shelters, satellite communications systems, and storage facilities, and attacked Sheikh Isa Air Base in Bahrain; the Senate voted down for a second time an effort to limit President Trump's war powers, 14 countries formally backed Saudi Arabia's proposed Maritime Defense Alliance to protect international shipping routes, and President Trump announced a historic agreement on Hamas disarmament in Gaza — a separate diplomatic track that underscores how broadly the administration is managing simultaneous Middle East crises.

Egypt's inclusion changes the conflict's geography in a significant way. Damietta sits at the eastern mouth of the Nile Delta, one of the ports through which North Africa's LNG import and re-export infrastructure operates — the GasLog Salem is a liquefied natural gas tanker, and the Energos Winter is a floating storage and regasification unit. A deliberate attack on European LNG supply infrastructure is a different category of strike from hitting military or oil shipping targets at Hormuz, with implications for European energy security that extend the conflict's economic blast radius beyond the oil market. Brent crude rose to $88.97 (▲1.04%) as the new geographic expansion repriced upward, though not sharply, as the market awaits confirmation of Egypt's response.

SORLA Protein Defends Against Tau Tangles in Alzheimer's Disease — New Prevention Target · Metformin Acts Directly in Brain Hypothalamus to Control Blood Sugar (New Mechanism) · Multivitamin Preserves Muscle Strength in Older Adults · Silver Diamine Fluoride Prevents Cavities Without Drilling SORLA SORLA: defends against tau tangles → new Alzheimer's prevention candidate · metformin: brain hypothalamus mechanism (previously unknown) → new target for diabetes + metabolic disease research · multivitamin: preserves muscle strength and stamina in older adults (RCT) · silver diamine fluoride: ▼ cavities in >50% of baby teeth without drilling

Researchers identified SORLA — a protein that shuttles molecules between cell compartments — as a powerful defense against the toxic tau tangles that drive Alzheimer's disease progression; SORLA appears to intercept and clear tau before it can aggregate into the neurofibrillary tangles that damage neurons, positioning it as a new target for preventive intervention in a disease where tau pathology remains poorly druggable. Separately, metformin — a decades-old, widely prescribed diabetes drug — was found to act directly in the brain's hypothalamus to regulate blood sugar through a mechanism that was not previously understood, opening a new research avenue for diabetes and metabolic disease treatment and potentially explaining why metformin's clinical effects exceed what its peripheral mechanism alone would predict.

Two additional findings round out the science beat: a randomized controlled trial found that a daily multivitamin preserved muscle strength and stamina in older adults, with gains measurable in functional tasks of daily living and not fully explained by nutritional deficiency correction alone; and silver diamine fluoride, a liquid treatment applied topically to teeth, stopped cavity progression in more than half of treated baby teeth without drilling or anesthesia — a result with significant implications for pediatric dentistry, particularly in communities with limited access to sedation-capable dental offices. The SORLA and metformin findings share a structural theme: familiar targets and familiar drugs whose mechanisms turn out to be richer than the original model assumed.
