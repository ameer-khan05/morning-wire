President Trump said Friday that he plans to declare the Strait of Hormuz "a territory of the United States" after the war with Iran ends — a statement without legal precedent under UNCLOS or international maritime law, where sovereignty over Hormuz has been shared between Iran and Oman since 1979, and where no international legal mechanism permits unilateral territorial claims over international transit waterways. The remark, made at a Long Island campaign event supporting Republican gubernatorial candidate Bruce Blakeman, arrived 24 hours before Sunday's diplomatic preparations for Monday's UNSC ceasefire vote, forcing Russia and China to factor a US territorial claim into their abstention calculus on a resolution intended to pressure Iran — not expand US sovereignty.

The Strait of Hormuz enters Day 47 with Iran maintaining that the waterway will not reopen until US offensive military operations end — no breakthrough has been reported in the negotiations that began in June. The UNSC votes Monday on a revised ceasefire resolution that has been reworked since Friday's UAE-ADNOC vessel attacks; the Oman diplomatic back-channel and the Joint Chiefs-IRGC communication line both remain open as the primary off-ramp architecture ahead of the vote.

480,479-Patient Study: Stair Climbing Cuts Cardiovascular Mortality 39%, All-Cause Mortality 24% — American Journal of Cardiovascular Drugs · Orange Juice: 1,705 Genes Altered in Immune Cells After 60 Days — Inflammation + Blood Pressure Down · Gut Microbiome: Fiber Depletion → Microbes Consume Mucus Lining 39% Norfolk & Norwich meta-analysis: 480,479 patients, 9 studies → stair climbing → 39% lower cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.61) + 24% lower all-cause mortality (RR 0.76) → heart attack, stroke, heart failure all reduced · Orange juice: 1,705 differentially expressed genes after 60 days, inflammation + blood pressure genes less active · Gut: fiber depletion → microbes eat mucus lining

A systematic review and meta-analysis of nine studies involving 480,479 patients, published in the American Journal of Cardiovascular Drugs, found regular stair climbing is associated with a 39% lower risk of dying from cardiovascular disease (relative risk 0.61) and a 24% lower risk of dying from any cause (RR 0.76), with risk reductions also recorded for heart attack, stroke, and heart failure. Researchers at Norfolk and Norwich University Hospital concluded that stair climbing represents an accessible, no-equipment form of exercise that confers cardiovascular protection across a population large enough to produce statistically reliable results — usable in daily routines without dedicated exercise time. A second study published in Molecular Nutrition & Food Research found 60 days of daily orange juice consumption altered the expression of 1,705 genes in immune cells, with genes linked to inflammation (IL6, IL1β) and blood pressure regulation (NAMPT, NLRP3) becoming measurably less active.

The orange juice study suspects hesperidin and other citrus compounds as the mechanism — natural compounds acting at the gene-expression level inside circulating immune cells rather than merely as dietary antioxidants. A third finding shows that when dietary fiber intake falls, gut microbes shift from fermenting fiber to consuming the protective mucus lining of the gut wall, degrading the intestinal barrier in a process associated with increased inflammation and systemic permeability risk.
