Daraxonrasib: nearly doubles cancer patient survival in pivotal trial (bear-hugs cancer-driver protein — new mechanism) · FDA: bemotrizinol approved for US sunscreens (long-lasting UV filter, photoaging protection) · GLP-1 antibody: reduces lean-mass loss on semaglutide in clinical trial · sleep EEG + ML: Alzheimer's warning signs detected years before memory problems

Daraxonrasib — a cancer drug that attacks malignancy through a novel "bear-hug" mechanism, binding a cancer-driver protein so completely that it cannot activate downstream growth signaling — nearly doubled patient survival time in a pivotal clinical trial, representing one of the most significant oncology efficacy signals in recent years and demonstrating that cancer-driver proteins previously considered undruggable can be addressed with molecular geometries that lock rather than block; the FDA separately greenlit bemotrizinol for use in US sunscreens, a long-lasting UV filter banned from domestic products since the 1970s regulatory freeze that defends against the solar radiation responsible for photoaging, adding a protective option unavailable in America for five decades.

Researchers separately found that a specific antibody significantly reduces the lean-mass loss that patients on GLP-1 drugs like semaglutide experience during treatment — a major clinical concern in the obesity drug class, since losing muscle alongside fat reduces the long-term health benefits of weight loss; the antibody provides a potential adjunct therapy that could preserve muscle while the GLP-1 reduces fat. A machine-learning analysis of sleeping-brain EEG recordings found it could detect Alzheimer's-related warning signs years before memory symptoms emerge — identifying dementia risk from a non-invasive overnight sleep study rather than a PET scan or lumbar puncture.
