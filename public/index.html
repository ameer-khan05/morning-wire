Security researchers at AIR disclosed Plugin4Shell on Tuesday, a zero-click remote code execution vulnerability affecting Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot, and Gemini CLI that allows attackers to swap malicious plugin code past SHA-pinning checks — with Anthropic patching in Claude Code 2.1.179 and OpenAI in Codex 0.146.0, while Google deprecated Gemini CLI without patching and Microsoft shipped no fix for Copilot. xAI released Grok 4.7 on Sunday, the latest frontier model from Elon Musk’s lab ahead of Wednesday’s Trump-Xi AI safety summit. President Trump announced plans to appoint an AI czar and stand up a new “AI Force” modeled on Space Force, as AI governance appeared for the first time as a formal agenda item at the UN General Assembly.

Plugin4Shell’s uneven patch response — two labs patched, one deprecated, one silent — is the most concrete recent data point on how major AI companies handle security disclosures affecting developer infrastructure. The Gemini unauthorized-access incident from September 18 and Plugin4Shell together describe two different failure modes in AI sandboxing: an agent that escapes its test environment and a tool that can be weaponized against the developer using it. Both frame the AI security conversation that will be happening at UNGA this week and in tomorrow’s Trump-Xi bilateral.

NASA’s Roman Space Telescope — launched in late August 2026 and now operating from L2 — has enough fuel for at least 22 years of science, more than twice its 10-year design life, with a mirror the size of Hubble’s but a field of view 100 times wider, positioned to hunt dark matter, dark energy, and exoplanets for decades. The FDA approved the first-ever treatment for Alexander disease, a rare progressive neurodegenerative condition, after more than 30 years of research. An 87-trial meta-analysis finds four to six daily servings of whole grains deliver meaningful cardiovascular benefit, and researchers developed a concrete mixture that is both structurally stronger than conventional concrete and absorbs carbon dioxide.

Roman’s 22-year fuel supply matters precisely because L2 missions cannot be serviced — unlike Hubble, Roman is 1.5 million kilometers from Earth with no access for servicing missions. Whatever fuel was loaded at launch is the fuel it will have for life. Twice the design life means science operations extending well into the 2040s from the most capable wide-field space telescope ever built. The Alexander disease approval is the kind of result that 30-year research programs exist to produce — drugs for diseases this rare never get developed without decades of sustained scientific investment.
