A large real-world study found semaglutide — the drug class sold as Ozempic and Wegovy — was associated with nearly 40% fewer asthma attacks and approximately 20% fewer COPD exacerbations, the first respiratory benefit established at population scale for this drug class. MIT engineers reported injectable "mini livers" — designed to support patients whose failing livers can no longer perform essential functions, potentially extending the window to transplant. An experimental compound called AP503 strengthened bones in mice by activating GPR133, a receptor that simultaneously boosts bone formation and slows bone loss.

Semaglutide's respiratory findings are potentially the drug class's largest discovered benefit since broad deployment — the weight-loss and cardiovascular effects drove adoption, but the mechanism appears to extend into immune and airway regulation. If causal, semaglutide becomes relevant for the large population with obesity-related asthma. The MIT mini liver project addresses one of transplant medicine's most constrained bottlenecks: the gap between liver failure and donor organ availability. AP503 offers a potential osteoporosis mechanism with a distinct receptor target from existing drugs.
