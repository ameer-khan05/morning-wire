Focused Ultrasound Delivers microRNA Across Blood-Brain Barrier — Multiple Glioblastoma Genes Targeted, Improved Animal Outcomes · Mystery Tick-Borne Virus Spreading Unnoticed in Northeast US · Time-Restricted Eating Protects Cognitive Function Beyond Weight Loss GBM Focused ultrasound + microRNA: blood-brain barrier crossed → multiple GBM genes targeted → improved outcomes in animal studies → new delivery mechanism for treatment-resistant brain cancer · tick-borne virus: Northeast US, spreading undetected, misdiagnosed as summer illness · time-restricted eating: cognitive protection independent of weight loss

Focused ultrasound successfully delivered microRNAs across the blood-brain barrier to target multiple glioblastoma genes simultaneously in animal studies, improving survival outcomes — a meaningful advance for one of the most treatment-resistant cancers, where the blood-brain barrier has historically blocked most therapeutic agents from reaching tumor cells; the microRNA approach targets tumor gene regulation mechanisms rather than killing cancer cells directly, making it complementary to existing surgical, radiation, and chemotherapy protocols. Separately, a mystery tick-borne virus may already be spreading undetected across the northeastern United States, with researchers warning that cases are likely being misdiagnosed as common summer illnesses because existing diagnostic panels don't include it.

New research on time-restricted eating — limiting food consumption to a shorter daily window — finds it protects certain thinking skills as people age through mechanisms independent of weight loss, suggesting cognitive benefits may persist even in individuals who don't lose weight on the approach. The three findings share a research infrastructure signature: each was made possible by imaging and surveillance tools that have matured rapidly over the past five years — focused ultrasound for brain drug delivery, genomic tick surveillance for novel pathogen detection, and continuous metabolic monitoring in aging cohorts.
