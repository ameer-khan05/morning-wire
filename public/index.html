The Trump administration published new AI chip export rules Tuesday closing the loophole that let Chinese companies rent Nvidia’s most advanced GPUs through data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil — Commerce’s 132-page interim final rule requires new export licenses for those five countries and bans remote computing access to restricted chips for Chinese firms worldwide — sending the SOX semiconductor index down 5.3% and AI stocks to their worst session since Jensen Huang’s resignation, on the same day Anthropic finalizes IPO pricing on 825 million Class A shares in the $70–$78 range targeting a $1.4 trillion valuation with ANT debuting Nasdaq Thursday, while Nvidia’s first post-Jensen Huang quarterly results arrive after the bell tonight expected at $2.47 EPS on $109.15 billion revenue.

The chip export rule is the week’s first supply-side disruption to AI hardware revenues. It does not remove Nvidia’s domestic business but closes the revenue stream from Chinese firms accessing compute through third-country data centers. The SOX moved on two simultaneous headwinds — the curb and Jensen’s governance uncertainty — entering the same session as Anthropic’s pricing. Wednesday night’s IPO outcome will be the market’s first read on how buyers are weighting those two pressures simultaneously.

AriBio’s mirodenafil — an oral PDE5 inhibitor sold as Mvix in South Korea for erectile dysfunction — slowed Alzheimer’s cognitive decline by 31% versus placebo in a Phase III trial of more than 1,500 early-stage patients over 52 weeks, presenting topline data at CTAD 2026 and published in the Washington Post Tuesday as potentially the first pill to meaningfully slow Alzheimer’s progression; MIT published findings in Science Translational Medicine that precisely timed bursts of pink noise during sleep strengthen the brain’s cerebrospinal fluid cleaning system — impaired in older adults — with potential applications for reducing Alzheimer’s-linked protein buildup; and Apple launched the Apple Health AI Usage Study to research how people interact with conversational health AI tools.

The AR1001 result is the week’s most significant biomedical finding. Lecanemab required intravenous infusions to slow Alzheimer’s progression; a pill that slows decline by 31% — even in the monotherapy subgroup only — changes the access calculation for one of the world’s largest disease burdens. The FDA pathway for a repurposed drug already approved in South Korea is shorter than for a novel compound, making the POLARIS-AD result potentially actionable on a two-to-three-year clinical timeline.
