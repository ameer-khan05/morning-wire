Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia’s CEO on Thursday after 33 years at the helm — the company’s board accepted his resignation following disclosure of affairs including one with an alleged Chinese spy and another with an Nvidia vice president, with CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO immediately — sending Nvidia shares into a double-digit plunge that pulled the Nasdaq 0.5% lower to 25,404 and the S&P 500 0.3% lower to 7,531 on the first day bond markets reopened after Veterans Day, with the 10-year yield ticking to 4.99% and resuming rate pressure from Tuesday’s hot CPI data; Nvidia separately debuted GR00T 2 at the CoRL robotics conference without its founding CEO, achieving 71% task success across 50 manipulation tasks from a single demonstration — the most capable humanoid robot foundation model benchmark to date. The Strait of Hormuz escalated from a risk environment to a confirmed disruption: Iran reported two oil tankers on fire after striking naval mines, with 37 ship attacks counted in November alone — the highest monthly total since the conflict began — pushing Brent crude 3.7% to $91.32 while WTI fell 1.5% to $85.31. Bitcoin extended its losing streak to a fourth day at $73,208 (▼1.0%) and Ethereum continued its confirmed technical downtrend at $2,037 (▼0.9%), finding no safe-haven bid in Hormuz’s geopolitical escalation and no macro relief from a bond market that reopened with rate pressure intact. The NIH published its largest-ever study linking PFAS “forever chemicals” to aggressive breast cancer — women with the highest PFOS blood levels had a 93% higher rate of ER-negative breast cancer, a finding that affects the hundreds of millions of people exposed to PFAS through drinking water and consumer products; and a first-of-its-kind randomized controlled trial found that non-invasive neuromodulation significantly improved Long COVID symptoms across seven domains simultaneously.

The NIH published its largest-ever PFAS study Thursday, finding that women with the highest PFOS blood levels had a 93% higher rate of aggressive ER-negative breast cancer — a finding implicating the “forever chemicals” present in the blood of approximately 97% of Americans — while women with the highest PFOA levels had a 67% higher rate; a randomized controlled trial published in Neuromodulation found that non-invasive autonomic neuromodulation improved Long COVID symptoms across 7 domains simultaneously — the first multi-domain RCT for the condition; colorectal cancer rates are rising after 20 years of decline; and GLP-1 drugs may also preserve memory and brain health.

The NIH PFAS finding is the most consequential public health result of the week: not because PFAS is a new risk — regulators have known for years — but because the Sister Study’s size and prospective design gives this specific 93% ER-negative association the strongest evidence yet, with direct implications for exposed-population screening protocols and ongoing manufacturer liability litigation.
