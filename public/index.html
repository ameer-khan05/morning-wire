Walking 3,000 steps a day slows Alzheimer’s tau protein buildup by nearly half and cuts cognitive decline by roughly 50% in patients with early disease signs, according to a nine-to-eleven-year study — a disease-modifying result, not just a risk-reduction one; a separate discovery identified a rare mutation that blocks harmful protein accumulation entirely, keeping high-risk patients mentally sharp into their 80s; an Alaska landslide triggered the second-highest tsunami ever recorded at 1,580 feet; and researchers uncovered the mechanism behind long-haul COVID taste loss lasting more than a year.

The step-count finding is the week’s most immediately actionable result: 3,000 steps is below what most active people already walk, and it operates specifically in people who already have early disease signs — the patient population where it matters most. The mutation discovery adds the longer-term implication: if the protective mechanism can be replicated pharmacologically, the biological pathway that keeps some people’s amyloid from triggering tau cascades becomes a drug target for those who don’t carry the natural variant.
