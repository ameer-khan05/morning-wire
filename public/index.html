Oral GLP-1 Drugs Penetrate Deep Brain Reward Circuit — Quiets Pleasure-Driven Eating & Addiction via CNS Pathway · 600K Veterans: GLP-1s Cut Substance Use Disorder Risk Across 6 Drug Classes · Vancomycin Revived Against Resistant Bacteria · Blood Pressure: Potassium Beats Sodium Reduction Alone GLP-1 Oral GLP-1 drugs: penetrate deep brain reward circuit → quiet pleasure-driven eating + addictive behavior (new CNS pathway, distinct from peripheral satiety) · 600K veterans: GLP-1s ▼ substance use disorder risk across alcohol, nicotine, cannabis, opioids, cocaine · vancomycin revived: new chemistry beats VRE resistance · potassium: more effective than sodium cut alone for blood pressure

Oral small-molecule GLP-1 drugs — newer tablet-form successors to injectable semaglutide — were found in NIH-funded research to penetrate deep into the brain's reward circuit and suppress pleasure-driven eating through a central nervous system pathway entirely distinct from the peripheral satiety signaling that injectable GLP-1 receptor agonists rely on; the discovery provides the first mechanistic explanation for why the drug class's clinical effects extend well beyond appetite and weight loss to include reductions in addictive behavior across multiple substance classes. A separate population-scale study of more than 600,000 US veterans confirmed the effect, finding GLP-1 users significantly less likely to develop substance use disorders involving alcohol, nicotine, cannabis, opioids, and cocaine simultaneously.

Scientists separately reported a successful chemistry modification to vancomycin — a last-resort antibiotic whose effectiveness against vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE) had been declining as bacteria evolved a cell-wall alteration that the drug's original binding mechanism cannot address — by redesigning the binding site to work against the altered target, restoring potency against bacteria that had become clinically untreatable. A large population study also found that increasing dietary potassium reduced elevated blood pressure more effectively than sodium reduction alone, reframing the dietary guidance given to hundreds of millions of hypertension patients globally: the sodium advice is correct but incomplete without the potassium component.
