Nevada's transportation authority unanimously approved permits Friday for Tesla, Waymo, and Uber to operate paid robotaxi services across Clark County — including all of Las Vegas — in the largest single regulatory robotaxi authorization in US history. Tesla received 5,000 permits with statewide expansion rights, while Waymo and Uber's Aviari service received 1,000 permits each; commercial launches are expected before year-end. Tesla said it would be "extremely happy" to get 2,500 robotaxis on Nevada streets by 2027. Anthropic simultaneously disclosed $65 billion in annualized revenue as of July and filed confidentially for an IPO with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan, with Q2 preliminary revenue exceeding $11.5 billion — a greater than 14-fold year-over-year increase from $787 million in the same period last year.

The Nevada approval grants Tesla the ability to expand its geofence statewide with advance notice — a flexibility that makes the Nevada authorization qualitatively different from city-by-city Waymo permits. Tesla also quietly canceled its Solar Roof program this week, ending the residential solar tile product that Elon Musk once described as pivotal to the company's energy-transition vision after years of limited production and delivery delays. On the AI infrastructure side, Anthropic's $65 billion annualized run rate — sevenfold growth since end of 2025 — adds a second AI-sector IPO to a fall calendar that already includes OpenAI's anticipated public filing, potentially making late 2026 the most significant period for AI company public listings in the market's history.

The Hormuz crisis reached Day 53 with Iran resuming attacks on commercial shipping it deemed noncompliant with its transit demands, prompting the United States to reimpose its naval blockade after a brief ceasefire pause that had not produced the full diplomatic settlement Washington sought. Fifteen nations signed a statement to establish a multinational maritime defense coalition following a Saudi Arabia Defense Ministry announcement — the most significant multilateral security response to the Hormuz crisis since the closure began. A fragile ceasefire discussion remains active but stalled: progress depends on Iran pledging to fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end its nuclear weapons program — terms Tehran has not itself endorsed. Brent crude held near $93.64 on Friday, with the disruption premium intact.

The 15-nation coalition's formation signals that regional and global powers have shifted from waiting for a US-Iran bilateral resolution toward building independent defensive maritime capacity — a posture that could both protect commercial shipping and risk being perceived by Tehran as further encirclement. Iran's resumed shipping attacks on Day 53 reverse the partial improvement that had emerged in mid-August; the US reimposed blockade in response restores the confrontational dynamic that has characterized most of the 53-day crisis. The ceasefire condition requiring Iran to end its nuclear program — rather than merely freeze it — is a maximalist demand that diplomatic analysts say is unlikely to be accepted without a fundamentally different negotiating framework than the Oman back-channel has produced.

GDF15 Hormone Protects Liver From Inflammation and Scarring — Previously Unknown Mechanism (McMaster) · AI Predicts Vaccine Response Before Injection: "Immune Readiness" in 4,000+ People · Swedish Study 376,000: At-Home Colorectal Screening Cuts Cancer Death 43% · Alzheimer's Compound Clears Protein Clumps in Mice 43% McMaster: GDF15 hormone → activates previously unknown mechanism → protects liver from inflammation + fibrosis (scarring) → second major biological function for GDF15 (known for appetite/weight-loss) · AI immune readiness: antibody pattern analysis in 4,000+ people → predicts vaccine response strength before injection → personalized vaccination timing · Swedish 376,000-person study: at-home colorectal test → 43% cancer death risk reduction (one of largest screening intervention findings at population scale) · Alzheimer's: compound prevents amyloid clumping in mice → nerve cells survive longer

McMaster University researchers found that GDF15 — a hormone already known for regulating appetite and supporting weight loss — has a second major biological function: it activates a previously unknown mechanism that protects the liver from inflammation and fibrosis (scarring). The discovery is significant because liver disease affects approximately 1.5 billion people globally; GDF15 drugs already in clinical development for metabolic disease may have unanticipated hepatoprotective effects. Separately, AI researchers identified "immune readiness" signals by analyzing antibody patterns in more than 4,000 people, demonstrating the ability to predict how strongly an individual will respond to a vaccine before injection — a capability that could enable personalized vaccination scheduling.

A large-scale Swedish long-term study of 376,000 people found that a simple at-home colorectal cancer screening test cut the risk of dying from colorectal cancer by 43% — one of the largest relative risk reductions for any cancer screening intervention at population scale, strengthening the evidence base for expanding access to non-invasive colorectal testing in settings where colonoscopy remains logistically or financially constrained. An Alzheimer's research team published results showing an experimental compound prevented amyloid protein clumps from forming in mouse brains, helping nerve cells survive longer and reducing amyloid buildup through a mechanism distinct from the antibody-based amyloid-clearing drugs currently in clinical use.
