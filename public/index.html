By the Numbers
167
Years the Riemann hypothesis remained unproven, from Bernhard Riemann’s 1859 paper to Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge’s November 20, 2026 proof — developed with Claude’s assistance and verified by Lean FRO and Mathlib on November 21. A more compact 76,000-line version was confirmed correct by Sutherland on November 26. The Clay Mathematics Institute offers a $1 million Millennium Prize for the Riemann hypothesis; the prize claim process begins now. The result is the first proof of a Clay Millennium Prize problem developed with the direct assistance of an AI model, and the first confirmed by formal automated verification at this scale.

A large study found that recreational exercise is associated with a 24% lower dementia risk while physically demanding occupational labor is associated with a 20% higher risk — a counterintuitive finding that inverts the assumption that physical activity protects the brain regardless of context; UC San Diego researchers reported in Cell Metabolism that gut bacteria’s modification of bile acids is linked to exceptional human longevity and that boosting this microbial activity improved metabolic health in aged mice; later-born generations have better memory and lose it more slowly, with dementia risk at any given age falling for decades; and a study found anxiety in younger generations began rising roughly 15 years ago, aligning with the period of rapid smartphone adoption.

The exercise-type dementia finding reframes the question: it is not whether the body is physically active but whether the activity is chosen or compelled. Voluntary exercise produces stress-reduction and neurological benefits; sustained occupational physical stress without recovery appears to impose a net cognitive cost across a working lifetime.
