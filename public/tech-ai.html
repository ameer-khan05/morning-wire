The Anchor · Gemini 4 Carbon and the Alphabet Miss — The Same Company, One Day, Two Opposite Stories About AI

Google launched its best-ever frontier AI model on Thursday and Alphabet reported its weakest recent earnings on the same day. That is not a contradiction — it is a description of where Alphabet stands in the AI investment cycle. Gemini 4 Carbon topping the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 59 is the research-output side: Google’s AI teams are producing benchmark-leading models. Alphabet’s negative $9.1 billion free cash flow is the investment-cost side: building those models requires capital expenditure that currently exceeds the incremental revenue they generate for the core search business. Search revenue growing at 12% is a deceleration from 16% in the prior quarter — not a collapse, but a slowing in the business that is supposed to be the primary beneficiary of the AI enhancements. Microsoft’s Azure 48% growth makes the comparison precise: both companies made large AI infrastructure commitments; Thursday shows those commitments generating very different Q3 revenue outcomes at each platform. The question Thursday’s results crystallize is not whether Alphabet’s AI investment will return, but how many more quarters of negative FCF occur before the revenue expression catches up to the capability output.

Google launched Gemini 4 Carbon on Thursday, a frontier model for cybersecurity and software engineering that topped the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0 at a score of 59 — ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 58 — with 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro for finding real software vulnerabilities, 71% on CWE-bench for producing working patches, and complete resistance to all tested prompt-injection attacks on Gray Swan’s Indirect Prompt Injection Benchmark, a benchmark its predecessor Argon failed at a 0.7% attack rate; access initially goes to a curated group of cyber defenders via the Fairwind Program, with gradual expansion to partners and eventually broader availability, at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens.

Prompt-injection resistance is what makes Gemini 4 Carbon deployable in workflows processing untrusted content — malicious documents, suspicious code, adversarial environments — without becoming an attack vector itself. Fairwind’s restricted access reflects Google’s caution about not deploying offensive capability ahead of defensive use.

Updates to OpenAI’s public API specification on Thursday exposed an unannounced GPT Image 2.6 model alongside a new “markup” image-edit parameter, with the markup parameter pointing to vector-based masking for localized inpainting and outpainting — precision image editing capabilities that allow modifications to specific regions of an image using vector masks rather than the pixel-level brushing approach of current tools; OpenAI has not confirmed the model or the timeline for its release, and the disclosure emerged through developers monitoring API specification updates rather than through official announcement.

Vector masks allow programmatic, repeatable edit-region specification — relevant for API pipelines where pixel brushwork is too imprecise. The spec leak follows OpenAI’s pattern of staging-before-announcing; markup’s presence indicates a capability that is built, not planned.
