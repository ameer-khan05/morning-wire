Beijing-based Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 — a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model with a 1 million token context window, reasoning always on, and native vision — topped a major coding leaderboard on its July 17 launch, reigniting the China-US AI rivalry debate by demonstrating that a Chinese lab operating under US chip export restrictions can deliver a frontier open model on the benchmarks that matter most to enterprise developers; full open weights arrive July 27 under a Modified MIT license at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, roughly half the per-task cost of the top Western frontier models; in the same week, Google, Microsoft, Salesforce, Snowflake, and ServiceNow formally endorsed Anthropic's Model Context Protocol as the enterprise AI connectivity standard, and the MCP protocol published its largest revision addressing enterprise identity and architectural concerns; and Anthropic, Blackstone, and Hellman & Friedman launched Ode with Anthropic, a standalone enterprise AI services company built on the acquisition of Fractional AI, as France's competition authority published a 3,700-page advisory opinion finding that OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic together control more than 84% of the AI agent market.

Kimi K3 is the week's most significant AI rivalry development. The model's coding leaderboard result arrived from a Chinese lab operating under US export restrictions on the advanced chips used to train frontier models — restrictions designed in part to impede exactly this kind of result. The full open weights on July 27 under Modified MIT are the detail that transforms a benchmark into a deployable enterprise asset: any company, anywhere, can run a 2.8 trillion-parameter frontier model without API dependency, without per-token cost commitments to a Chinese provider, and without the licensing restrictions that closed-model providers impose. The MCP formal endorsement the same week adds a US-designed connectivity standard that the major enterprise software platforms have now committed to, meaning the enterprise AI stack for the rest of 2026 is a Kimi K3-class open coding model connected through MCP to Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, and Snowflake systems. Ode with Anthropic is the commercial response to the same enterprise demand that Kimi K3 is addressing technically: the market for deploying AI in large organizations is worth billions, and no one has captured it at scale yet. France's 84% market-share finding is the regulatory signal that intervention in AI agent markets is coming — and that European regulators are approaching it with more technical sophistication than any prior tech investigation.

Beijing-based Moonshot AI launched Kimi K3 on July 17 — a 2.8 trillion-parameter open-weight model with a 1 million token context window, reasoning always on, and native vision — and reported that it topped a major coding leaderboard, reigniting debate about whether US chip export restrictions are impeding Chinese labs from reaching frontier performance. Full open weights are scheduled for July 27 under a Modified MIT license, with API access priced at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens — roughly half the per-task cost of leading Western frontier models like GPT-5.6. The model was developed despite export controls on the most advanced AI chips, which the US imposed in part to slow Chinese frontier model development.

The open weights on July 27 are the structural detail that separates Kimi K3 from prior Chinese model announcements. A benchmark result from a closed API proves capability but does not change enterprise deployment options. Open weights under Modified MIT change the deployment landscape: any organization can run the model on-premises, fine-tune it on proprietary data, and deploy it in production without API cost or dependency on a Chinese cloud provider. Enterprise legal and security teams that would not approve API calls to a Chinese AI service can approve a locally deployed open-weight model with a permissive license. That deployment flexibility is why the open-weights date is the most strategically significant detail in the announcement, not the benchmark position itself.

MCP Goes Enterprise — Google, Microsoft, Salesforce, Snowflake, ServiceNow Formally Endorse · MCP 2026-07-28 Revision: Enterprise Identity + Stateless Architecture · 18 Months De Facto Standard → Official Industry Consensus · Ode with Anthropic: Blackstone-Backed Enterprise AI Services Co MCP MCP: Google + Microsoft + Salesforce + Snowflake + ServiceNow endorse · MCP 2026-07-28 revision: enterprise identity + stateless architecture · 18 months de facto → officially endorsed · Ode with Anthropic: Anthropic + Blackstone + H&F enterprise AI services · Fractional AI acquisition

Google, Microsoft, Salesforce, Snowflake, and ServiceNow formally endorsed Anthropic's Model Context Protocol as the enterprise AI connectivity standard this week — converting 18 months of de facto adoption into official vendor commitment — as MCP simultaneously published its largest revision addressing the enterprise identity and stateless architecture concerns that had kept IT departments from deploying it in production environments. Separately, Anthropic, Blackstone, and Hellman & Friedman launched Ode with Anthropic, a standalone enterprise AI services company built on the acquisition of Fractional AI, targeting the deployment-and-integration services market that pure model API providers do not address.

The five-platform endorsement of MCP answers the enterprise IT objection that open protocols do not come with vendor support. Microsoft and Salesforce commitment means their support teams will assist with MCP integrations — the same level of support commitment that drove enterprise adoption of REST APIs over SOAP in the 2010s. The MCP 2026-07-28 revision addressing enterprise identity is the technical change that unblocks IT: without authentication and authorization controls at the protocol level, MCP could not be used in multi-tenant enterprise environments where AI agents must operate with user-scoped permissions. That revision, timed with the endorsements, suggests coordinated enterprise readiness messaging. Ode with Anthropic is the services layer being built on top of this stack: the company that deploys Anthropic models through MCP into Salesforce and ServiceNow workflows for Fortune 500 enterprises.

France Competition Authority: OpenAI, Google, Anthropic Control 84% of AI Agent Market — 3,700-Page Advisory Opinion · Regulators Built Their Own AI Agents to Investigate · Intervention Remedies Expected · Meta Launches Consumer Subscriptions Globally 84% France competition authority: OpenAI + Google + Anthropic = 84%+ AI agent market · 3,700-page advisory opinion · regulators built + deployed own agents · inquiry Jan–Jul 2026 · formal intervention remedies expected · Meta: Instagram Plus + Facebook Plus + WhatsApp Plus launched globally

France's competition authority published a 3,700-page advisory opinion on the AI agent market finding that OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic together control more than 84% of it — the result of a January-to-July 2026 investigation in which regulators built and deployed their own AI agents to evaluate the market firsthand rather than relying solely on company submissions. The technically sophisticated approach is expected to produce formal intervention remedies rather than voluntary commitments. Separately, Meta officially rolled out global consumer subscriptions — Instagram Plus at $3.99 per month, Facebook Plus at $3.99 per month, and WhatsApp Plus at $2.99 per month — marking the first time Meta has monetized its social platforms through direct consumer subscription at global scale.

The French competition authority's methodology is the precedent-setting element of the investigation. Building and deploying AI agents to evaluate the market creates an evidentiary record that is difficult for companies to challenge on technical grounds: regulators who have operated the tools can speak to performance, switching costs, integration dependencies, and competitive dynamics from firsthand experience. That evidence base makes the expected formal remedies more durable against legal challenge than an investigation based solely on company-submitted data. The 84% market share finding is also the number that other European national competition authorities and the European Commission will cite in their own investigations — the French opinion is likely to be the template for EU-level AI agent market intervention in 2027.

Quick hits

  • Kimi K3's pricing at $3/M input and $15/M output is the most interesting strategic signal in the announcement. Those prices are competitive with mid-tier Western models, not just with Chinese alternatives — Moonshot is not competing on cost alone, it is competing on cost plus open-weight optionality. An enterprise that uses Kimi K3 via API today and switches to self-hosted open weights July 27 has no migration cost: same model, different deployment. That optionality is unique among frontier model providers and changes the risk calculus of enterprise procurement decisions significantly.
  • The MCP 2026-07-28 enterprise identity revision is worth watching for the specific authorization mechanism it introduces. Enterprise AI agent deployments face a fundamental security question: when an AI agent acts on behalf of a user inside Salesforce or ServiceNow, what permissions does it have, and can those permissions be audited? A protocol-level answer to that question — rather than a per-integration workaround — is what makes AI agent deployment auditable at scale. If the revision delivers a credible protocol-level authorization model, enterprise AI agent deployment timelines compress by months.
