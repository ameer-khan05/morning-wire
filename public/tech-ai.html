The Anchor · Nvidia’s $115.8B Quarter — What the First Post-Jensen Earnings Report Says About the AI Chip Cycle’s Durability Without Its Founder

Nvidia’s $115.8 billion Q3 2026 quarter answers the central question of Jensen Huang’s resignation: the AI chip cycle does not need its founder to sustain 103% year-over-year revenue growth. The beat is unambiguous. The harder question the report raises is not whether Nvidia can grow revenue without Jensen Huang — it clearly can — but whether the AI hardware cycle can lift equities in a 5.15% yield environment. Wednesday’s session answers that: not yet. Despite Nvidia shares rising nearly 3% in premarket on the results, the broader market continued falling as Federal Reserve Governor Waller’s half-point hike warning sent the 10-year yield to 5.15% and triggered a risk-off session that overrode the AI hardware beat. Interim CEO Colette Kress guided 75% year-over-year revenue growth for fiscal 2028, projecting AI hardware demand well into next year. The guidance is the most important forward signal the earnings report delivered. If the market is eventually forced to price the AI chip cycle correctly — and a $7.1 billion consensus beat suggests it has been underpricing it — the disconnect between Nvidia’s results and the ongoing sell-off is a timing problem. The rate pressure that is preventing the AI earnings from lifting the market will eventually ease. When it does, Nvidia has $115.8 billion quarters as its baseline.

The $7.1 billion consensus beat — on $115.8 billion in revenue — is the most significant AI hardware earnings result of 2026. That the market sold off anyway is the rate environment’s clearest signal yet: at 5.15% yields with a Fed that may accelerate its hiking path, the AI earnings premium has a ceiling, even when the earnings themselves are exceptional.
