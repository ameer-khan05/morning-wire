The Anchor · The 167-Year Proof — What It Means That the Riemann Hypothesis Is Solved, and That an AI Model Was Present When It Happened

The Riemann hypothesis is not an obscure result. It is the most famous unsolved problem in mathematics, one of seven Clay Millennium Prize problems, and directly connected to the distribution of prime numbers — a question at the foundation of number theory and, indirectly, of modern cryptography. Bernhard Riemann posed the hypothesis in his 1859 paper on prime numbers: that all nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part equal to one-half. Number theorists have known for over a century that the truth or falsity of this statement has profound implications for how prime numbers are distributed. No one proved it in 167 years. Levent Alpöge, an Anthropic mathematician, released a formal proof on November 20, 2026. He explicitly credited Claude as a key contributor. The Lean Formal Reasoning Organization and the Mathlib Initiative verified the proof on November 21. Luke Sernau released a more efficient version — 76,000 lines in a compact form, 300,000 in the full readable version — on November 25; both versions were confirmed correct by Sutherland on November 26. Two independent organizations have now confirmed it. The Clay Mathematics Institute prize claim process begins. The historical context matters: seven Clay Millennium Prizes were announced in 2000 for the seven most important unsolved problems in mathematics. This is the first to be solved. And it is the first solved with the assistance of an AI model — a collaboration in which the mathematician and the AI both contributed to a proof that neither would have produced alone. What proportion of the proof is Alpöge’s and what proportion is Claude’s is a question the mathematical community will work through. What is not in question is that the proof is correct.

The Riemann hypothesis is connected to the distribution of prime numbers through the zeta function, which means its proof resolves a fundamental uncertainty in number theory — not by changing applied cryptography (RSA and elliptic curve cryptography do not depend on the hypothesis being false), but by closing the theoretical gap that has made prime distribution estimates imprecise for 167 years. The question of how much of the proof Claude contributed versus Alpöge will shape the mathematical community’s norms for crediting AI tools in formal mathematics.

Nvidia halted AI server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia following US export rules introduced on November 16 that require case-by-case export licenses for advanced AI chips and the servers that contain them sent to those four countries plus Brazil; manufacturers including Dell and Supermicro have informed customers in those countries that Nvidia will not authorize their orders to be shipped, with the Financial Times, Tom’s Hardware, and multiple regional outlets reporting the freeze; the new rules target China’s practice of accessing restricted US AI compute by renting cloud capacity from Southeast Asian data centers — the region had become a preferred location for Chinese AI developers seeking to circumvent direct chip restrictions — and regional cloud providers now have a 90-day window to obtain export licenses by verifying their customer portfolios exclude restricted Chinese entities; the shipment freeze directly threatens Southeast Asia’s multi-billion-dollar data-center build-out, particularly in Malaysia and Indonesia where hyperscale AI facilities have attracted heavy institutional investment.

The practical effect of the November 16 rule is to shift the burden of China-access verification from Nvidia to Southeast Asian cloud providers — who must now certify their customer base is China-entity-free or lose access to Nvidia supply. That is a fundamentally different compliance model than the previous one, where the chip-level export control was enforced at the point of sale. The 90-day window is tight for data-center operators who have already committed infrastructure spending on the assumption of uninterrupted Nvidia supply.
