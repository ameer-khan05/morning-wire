OpenAI's S-1 registration statement is expected to go public on SEC EDGAR imminently — the final formal step before a September IPO managed by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley targeting a $1 trillion public valuation, up from the $852 billion private mark set in a $122 billion March fundraise. The S-1's public release is the moment the artificial intelligence boom becomes a public market event in the most consequential way possible: investors will see OpenAI's revenue, cost structure, and risk factors for the first time, and the company's unprofitable $2 billion per month revenue run rate — generating losses of roughly $1.22 for every $1 earned — will be priced against a $1 trillion target by the market that invented the current AI cycle. Anthropic separately disclosed its first profitable quarter: $10.9 billion in Q2 2026 revenue at +130% year-over-year growth, achieving its first operating profit of $559 million two years ahead of its own internal schedule.

The Anthropic result arrived alongside a $9.1 billion, 20-year compute supply agreement with Riot Platforms — the Bitcoin miner that has converted its Rockdale, Texas campus into AI data center capacity — providing 191 megawatts of power beginning in stages from December 2027. The Riot deal is the most consequential AI infrastructure commitment signed this week: 191 MW at $9.1B over 20 years represents a long-duration fixed compute supply at a time when frontier labs are competing for power access, and Bitcoin miners have proven data center infrastructure that Riot is repricing for AI workloads rather than mining economics. Meta's Muse Glimmer open-source release and Gemini's 1 billion MAU milestone complete a tech weekend in which the three major strategic AI positions — commercial closed (OpenAI IPO), independent profitable (Anthropic), and open-source ecosystem (Meta) — each produced a landmark event simultaneously.

OpenAI's public S-1 is not just a financial disclosure — it is the event that converts the AI era from a venture-funded experiment into a public equity market claim. The S-1 will reveal the precise revenue trajectory of the company that built ChatGPT, GPT-4, and the model family that triggered the current AI investment cycle; it will disclose how much OpenAI spends on compute, how those costs are growing relative to revenue, and what the company believes its risks are as it competes against Google, Anthropic, Meta, and Chinese labs simultaneously. The $1 trillion target is not a valuation that Goldman and Morgan Stanley set independently — it is the number the market produced in the post-IPO pricing models circulating among institutional investors based on OpenAI's $2B/month revenue, its subscriber and API customer base, and the multiple the market is currently applying to frontier AI companies. The critical financial question the S-1 will answer is not "how much revenue does OpenAI generate" — that has been reported — but "at what rate is the gap between revenue and cost narrowing," because the $1 trillion target requires investors to price a path to profitability that the company has not yet demonstrated. Anthropic's first operating profit provides the nearest comparable data point: $10.9B revenue, $559M operating profit, achieved two years ahead of Anthropic's own schedule and before OpenAI has reached the same milestone. The Anthropic result is both a confirmation that frontier AI lab economics are moving toward profitability at scale and a benchmark against which OpenAI's own profitability timeline will be measured. The Riot Platforms deal context: Riot is a Bitcoin miner that announced in 2025 it would convert excess data center capacity from mining to AI workloads. The $9.1B, 20-year deal with Anthropic confirms the conversion economics: a Bitcoin mining facility's power and cooling infrastructure translates directly to AI training and inference capacity at economics that compete with purpose-built hyperscaler data centers. The 191 MW commitment represents roughly 9% of Anthropic's current compute capacity — a meaningful supply addition staged from December 2027 — and the 20-year duration locks in cost certainty for a period that spans three full AI model cycles at minimum. For Riot, the deal converts the company from a cyclical Bitcoin miner into a long-duration AI infrastructure play backed by a frontier AI lab customer, which explains the 20% overnight share surge on the announcement. The AI-to-mining infrastructure arbitrage that Riot is executing is a 2026 version of the 2019 cloud-to-colocation transition: physical infrastructure built for one compute workload being repriced for a higher-value workload when the original workload's economics deteriorate.

Meta's Muse Glimmer open-source release — 30 billion parameters, Apache 2.0, runnable locally on a 24GB GPU — is a strategic move in the three-way competition between closed frontier (OpenAI, Anthropic), open ecosystem (Meta), and Chinese open-weight (Alibaba Qwen, DeepSeek) AI. Meta's open-source position serves a strategic purpose that closed labs cannot replicate: every developer who downloads and deploys Muse Glimmer builds on Meta's model architecture, Meta's training approaches, and Meta's infrastructure patterns, creating ecosystem lock-in without the user cost of a commercial API. The EU AI Act enforcement live date of August 2 adds a new regulatory variable: European Commission authority to review frontier models before release now applies to OpenAI's S-1 timing (any model releases in the IPO window must clear EU review) and to Meta's open-source releases (Apache 2.0 models still require documentation under the Act's transparency obligations).

Anthropic reported Q2 2026 revenue of $10.9 billion — a 130% year-over-year increase — and its first operating profit of $559 million, a milestone the company's internal planning had not projected until two years later, reflecting both the pace of enterprise AI adoption and the leverage Anthropic has achieved in its cost structure as Claude scales across enterprise customers. The revenue figure makes Anthropic one of the fastest-growing enterprise software companies in history at its revenue scale; the operating profit converts it from a loss-funded frontier lab into a viable commercial entity capable of funding its own compute needs without continuous external capital. On the same week, Anthropic closed a $9.1 billion, 20-year compute supply agreement with Riot Platforms — a Bitcoin mining company that has converted its Rockdale, Texas campus to AI data center capacity — providing 191 megawatts of power with initial capacity available December 2027 and full buildout by June 2028.

The Riot deal is strategically significant for the AI infrastructure market: Bitcoin mining facilities possess the power connections, cooling infrastructure, and physical scale of data centers, built to handle sustained high-power compute loads — exactly the operational profile of AI training and inference workloads. As Bitcoin's mining economics have compressed, these facilities are repricing their infrastructure to AI customers at rates that compete with hyperscaler data center economics. Riot's conversion from miner to AI infrastructure provider, anchored by a $9.1B Anthropic agreement, establishes the arbitrage model that other mining companies with excess data center capacity will attempt to replicate.

Every developer who builds on Muse Glimmer builds on Meta's model architecture and training approach, creating ecosystem gravity that does not require commercial API relationships — a distribution strategy that hyperscalers and closed labs structurally cannot replicate at zero marginal cost.

Quick hits

  • The $1 trillion OpenAI IPO target requires a clarification on what "valuation" means at this stage. The $852 billion figure is a private financing valuation set by investors in March 2026 who agreed to pay that implied price per share in a $122 billion raise. The $1 trillion target is the number that OpenAI and its underwriters are using in pre-IPO investor conversations as the implied price they expect public market buyers to pay at listing. Neither number is what public market investors have voted on — that happens at the moment of pricing and in the first day of trading. Technology IPOs have frequently listed below their pre-IPO targets (Robinhood, Rivian) or far above (Snowflake). The $1 trillion target is the opening bid in a negotiation between OpenAI and public markets that will be resolved in September.
  • The EU AI Act's enforcement start on August 2 deserves more attention than it has received in US coverage. The Act's provisions that began enforcement on August 2 specifically target GPAI (general-purpose AI) model providers — which explicitly includes OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and Google. The documentation and transparency obligations require these providers to submit technical documentation of their model's training data, capabilities, and safety evaluations to the European Commission, and the Commission now has the authority to demand model reviews before or after deployment. For OpenAI, this means any new model releases during the IPO window must clear EU regulatory documentation in parallel with SEC disclosure requirements — a dual-track regulatory burden that S-1 risk factors will almost certainly flag as an ongoing compliance cost.
