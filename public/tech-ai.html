Two China-state-aligned threat groups carried out the most advanced AI-powered espionage campaigns ever documented in October 2026: Storm-0940 weaponized the ARTEX AI agent — built on the DeepSeek large language model — in a supply chain attack on ConnectWise’s Cloud Backup service, using stolen credentials to have ARTEX attempt sign-ins to 2,250 Microsoft 365 customer environments at more than 2,900 attempts per minute and reaching unauthorized privileged cloud access in under three hours in multiple cases; Vapor Panda independently deployed ARTEX against more than 200 organizations across 11 days starting October 17, targeting foreign ministries in Latin America, Europe, Asia, and the Middle East, semiconductor and defense firms in the U.S., and international think tanks — all nine days after the tool’s developer closed its source code specifically to prevent further weaponization; cybersecurity firms CrowdStrike and Fortune attributed both campaigns to Chinese state actors with high confidence, calling October a “Black October” for AI-powered cyber-espionage; separately, OpenAI cut its fundraising target from $30 billion at $1.4 trillion to $20 billion at $1.25 trillion as the Nasdaq’s worst week in three months — driven by Intel’s 29% crash, the Binance breach, and three hyperscaler guidance misses — compressed the private market AI multiples that investors were using to price the round.

ARTEX is the proof-of-concept. The question for every security team is whether their defenses move faster than that window.

The Anchor · ARTEX — When AI Tooling Goes From Open-Source to State Weapon in Nine Days

ARTEX was publicly available for security professionals on October 1. Its developer closed the source on October 8 after linking it to Storm-0940’s attacks. By October 17, Vapor Panda was running it against 200 organizations. Nine days. The ARTEX story is the clearest proof yet of what “open-source AI security tooling” means when state actors are in the threat model. The tool automates the most time-consuming parts of penetration testing: network reconnaissance, credential validation, privilege escalation. What takes a human red-teamer days, ARTEX executes in hours. Storm-0940’s ConnectWise attack was more sophisticated still — using a trusted managed-service provider’s backup infrastructure as the access vector means ARTEX operated inside target perimeters that would have blocked a direct external attack. The 2,900 authentication attempts per minute figure establishes the agent-automation level: human attackers do not attempt 2,900 logins per minute. AI agents do. ARTEX is not an AI assistant that writes phishing emails. It is an autonomous agent that executes full attack chains — from reconnaissance to privileged access — end-to-end, once given an objective and an initial credential foothold. The implication for enterprise defenders is that perimeter tools calibrated to human attacker cadences are structurally insufficient against agent-speed attacks.

Storm-0940, a China-state-aligned threat group, used the ARTEX AI agent — built on DeepSeek’s large language model — to attack 2,250 Microsoft 365 environments via a supply chain compromise of ConnectWise’s Cloud Backup service, leveraging stolen administrator credentials to have ARTEX attempt sign-ins at a rate exceeding 2,900 per minute and reaching unauthorized privileged cloud access in under three hours in multiple confirmed cases; CrowdStrike attributed the campaign with high confidence to Storm-0940 based on infrastructure, tooling, and behavioral markers consistent with prior Chinese espionage operations, confirming nine organizations as definitively compromised, with the true total likely higher as investigation continues.

ConnectWise’s Cloud Backup is a managed service provider trusted by its client environments in a way that direct external attacks are not. Using ConnectWise as the access vector means ARTEX was operating from inside client trust perimeters before any of the 2,250 targets had reason to generate an alert. That is the supply chain threat model applied to AI-agent-speed attacks.

Beginning October 17 — nine days after ARTEX’s developer closed its source code specifically to prevent further weaponization — a second China-state-aligned group, Vapor Panda, launched an 11-day AI-powered campaign using ARTEX against more than 200 organizations worldwide, including foreign ministries across Latin America, Europe, Asia, and the Middle East; semiconductor and defense companies in the U.S.; and think tanks with access to sensitive policy research; CrowdStrike attributed the campaign to Vapor Panda with high confidence based on the use of BloodAlchemy malware consistent with prior Chinese espionage tradecraft, and noted that closing ARTEX’s source code did not prevent state actors who had already obtained a copy from continuing operational deployment.

Vapor Panda’s target list maps China’s strategic intelligence priorities precisely: diplomatic communications, chip-industry intellectual property, defense research, and the policy community that shapes U.S.-China relations. ARTEX did not create those priorities — it accelerated the pace at which a single group can pursue all of them simultaneously.
