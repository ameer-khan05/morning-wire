Nvidia reported fiscal Q2 2027 revenue of $96.22 billion — up 106% year-over-year and $4.05 billion above the $92.17 billion analyst consensus — with data center revenue alone reaching $89.02 billion (up 117% year-over-year on Blackwell platform dominance), EPS of $2.22 beating the $2.10 estimate, and non-GAAP gross margins at 75.0%. Shares surged 9% Thursday, lifting Nasdaq 1.5% to 26,543.57 alongside Salesforce +11.2% and CrowdStrike +9% in the broadest tech earnings rally of the year. On the same day, Nvidia disclosed it is in advanced discussions to acquire Hugging Face — the dominant open-source AI model, dataset, and developer hub — for approximately $13 billion, a deal that would vertically integrate the world's dominant AI hardware company with the platform through which open-source AI models are distributed, fine-tuned, and discovered. AWS separately announced plans to deploy 2 million additional Nvidia GPUs — including Blackwell Ultra and Rubin systems — across its global infrastructure in 2027 and 2028, extending the hardware demand cycle that tonight's $96.22 billion result confirmed is structural, not cyclical.

The Nvidia Q2 result answers the question that has defined the AI infrastructure investment debate for three years: is the hyperscaler AI capital expenditure cycle sustainable, or is it beginning to show absorption saturation? At $96.22 billion — $4 billion above consensus, 106% year-over-year — the answer is unambiguous. The hyperscalers are not moderating their Blackwell commitments; they are executing on them faster than the analysts who track their public statements anticipated. The data center segment at $89.02 billion (+117%) confirms that compute demand for AI training and inference has not hit the plateau that skeptics expected as the "easy wins" of the first LLM supercycle were absorbed. The AI capex cycle is accelerating into the next hardware generation, not plateauing. The Hugging Face deal talks add a dimension that the earnings result alone does not contain: Nvidia is not content to own only the hardware layer of the AI stack.

The Anchor · Nvidia's $96.22B Is Not Just a Beat — It Is a Structural Statement About Who Controls the AI Value Chain, and the Hugging Face Talks Are Its Logical Extension

The Nvidia Q2 result — $96.22 billion, +106% year-over-year, data center $89.02 billion — is the most unambiguous validation of the AI infrastructure investment thesis yet produced by any single earnings report. Not because the number is large (though $96 billion in a single quarter is a figure with few historical precedents), but because it is $4 billion above a consensus that was itself extraordinarily high. The analyst community that tracks Nvidia most closely — 58 of 61 at Buy or Strong Buy, with target prices that already reflected a beat — still missed by $4 billion. That is a miss-to-the-upside so large that it requires a structural explanation, not just a demand explanation: the hyperscalers are not just buying more Blackwell GPUs than consensus expected; they appear to be ordering ahead, building inventory buffers, or accelerating deployment timelines in ways that are outpacing even the best-informed outsider estimates of their Blackwell purchasing plans. Data center at $89.02 billion — up 117% year-over-year from the prior-year's $41 billion — means the compute purchase by the four major hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) in a single quarter has grown by $48 billion in twelve months. The AI capex cycle is not showing absorption saturation. It is showing demand acceleration in the segment that the skeptical case had most frequently targeted as the one most likely to plateau first. The Hugging Face acquisition talks — confirmed by Bloomberg and reported in detail by TechCrunch — are not a separate story from the earnings beat; they are its logical extension. Nvidia controls approximately 81% of the AI accelerator market, which means it controls the infrastructure on which AI models are trained and the compute on which they are served. Acquiring Hugging Face would add the dominant platform through which those models are distributed, discovered, fine-tuned, and shared by the 5 million developers who use Hugging Face as their primary repository for open-source AI work. That is a vertical integration from silicon to developer platform — from the atoms that run AI to the community that builds with AI — with no existing precedent in the history of computing platforms. The closest analogy might be if Intel had acquired GitHub at the moment when the open-source software ecosystem was being built, owning both the chips that run open-source software and the platform through which open-source software is created. The antitrust implications are substantial and would be contested on both sides of the Atlantic. But the strategic logic is as clear as the $96.22 billion result: Nvidia is building toward a position in the AI industry that makes the hardware dominance story look like the beginning, not the destination.

The AWS 2 million GPU announcement extends the hardware demand visibility beyond the current quarter in a way that is underappreciated in the immediate reaction to the $96.22 billion beat. Blackwell Ultra and Rubin represent Nvidia's next two hardware generations after the current Blackwell platform that drove today's $89 billion data center quarter. AWS committing to 2 million additional GPUs from those future generations means the demand pipeline for Nvidia hardware has locked-in purchase commitments extending through 2028 — a visibility window that typically only exists for commercial aircraft orders, not enterprise technology purchases. This changes the character of the Nvidia earnings story from "current quarter beat" to "multi-year hardware platform with pre-committed demand at scale."

The data center segment's $89.02 billion represents Nvidia's core business at scale: the sequential growth trajectory — $22 billion in Q2 FY25, $35 billion, $47 billion, $65 billion, and now $89.02 billion — shows no sign of the absorption plateau that skeptics have predicted at each step.

The beat magnitude requires a structural explanation beyond demand strength. At $4.05 billion above the consensus established by analysts who track hyperscaler capital expenditure commitments in detail, the implication is that the analysts' models of hyperscaler purchasing — built from public earnings commentary, capex guidance, and supply-chain data points — are underestimating either the volume of Blackwell orders placed or the pace at which those orders are being fulfilled. The data center revenue growth from $65 billion to $89.02 billion — $24 billion in a single quarter — means the AI compute purchase market grew by an amount roughly equivalent to the entire S&P 500 healthcare sector's quarterly revenue between two consecutive Nvidia reports. The AI infrastructure cycle is not a demand story anymore; it is a production and fulfillment story, and Nvidia's ability to manufacture and deliver Blackwell GPUs faster than consensus models assumed is the variable that explains why the beat was $4 billion rather than $1 billion.

Nvidia is in advanced discussions to acquire Hugging Face — the dominant open-source AI model, dataset, and developer platform — for approximately $13 billion, confirmed by Bloomberg and TechCrunch on Thursday, though Business Insider notes no agreement has been signed and the deal could still fall through. The acquisition would place the platform used by more than 5 million developers to share, discover, and fine-tune open-source AI models under the ownership of the company that controls 81% of the AI accelerator market — vertically integrating Nvidia from the hardware that runs AI models to the community that builds with them. Nvidia's prior $235 million investment in Hugging Face's 2023 round valued the company at $4.5 billion; the reported $13 billion deal price implies nearly a 3× premium to that valuation.

If the acquisition closes, it would restructure the competitive dynamics of the open-source AI ecosystem described in Wednesday's edition. The Kimi K3 open-weight model reaching the top of coding benchmarks — the structural signal that open-source was approaching frontier parity on the most commercially valuable AI task — takes on different meaning if the platform through which Kimi K3 and its successors are distributed is owned by Nvidia. A Nvidia-owned Hugging Face would retain the open-source character of the hosted models (which Hugging Face has built its community trust on), but Nvidia would control the terms of access, the data about which models developers adopt, and the fine-tuning tools that make the platform sticky. The AWS 2 million GPU commitment provides a parallel demand signal: the largest cloud provider is pre-committing to Nvidia's next two hardware generations at scale, confirming that the hardware layer's trajectory is as visible to hyperscalers as today's $96.22 billion revenue makes it to the market.

Quick hits

  • Nvidia's initial after-hours dip to $206.36 — despite a $4 billion revenue beat — reflects a phenomenon that has characterized every major Nvidia earnings cycle since fiscal 2024: the options market prices for a large move, the result delivers that large move, and the initial response is muted or negative as investors look for the "catch" in the guidance rather than celebrating the beat. Thursday's 9% gain represents the market's second read: the guidance was constructive (not the cautious Q3 guide that bears were waiting for), the Hugging Face deal talks add strategic narrative, and the AWS 2 million GPU commitment provides multi-year demand visibility. The pattern of "AH dip, next-day rally" on Nvidia beats has now occurred in multiple consecutive earnings cycles, which tells you something about the systematic nature of the options-positioning unwind that drives the initial move versus the fundamental re-rating that follows on the open.
  • OpenAI's retirement of the DALL-E GPT interface effective August 30 — directing users to ChatGPT Images — is a quiet signal about the consolidation of AI product surfaces that is happening beneath the headline model releases. The proliferation of specialized AI interfaces (DALL-E for images, specific GPT plugins for tasks, etc.) that characterized 2023-2024 is giving way to a more integrated surface model where ChatGPT is the primary interface and model capabilities are accessed through it rather than through standalone interfaces. This is both a product simplification move and a data-centralization move: all image generation activity flowing through ChatGPT Images rather than through a separate DALL-E interface means OpenAI has a more complete view of how users interact with image generation in context with text, which has training-data implications for future multimodal model development.
