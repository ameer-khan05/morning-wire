The Anchor · Jensen Huang’s Resignation — The End of an Era in AI Hardware

Jensen Huang founded Nvidia in 1993 and ran it for 33 years, transforming a graphics chip company into the most valuable semiconductor company in history and the hardware backbone of the AI era. His departure is not a succession — it is a rupture. The circumstances are as damaging as the timing. Huang admitted to a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, a person US intelligence has identified as an alleged Chinese spy who had access to Nvidia’s most sensitive AI hardware roadmaps, export strategies, and customer data during the relationship. The national security implications go beyond Nvidia’s stock price: Nvidia’s H100 and B200 GPU architectures are the most restricted US technology exports to China, and the question of what Alice Huang may have learned — and from whom — is now an active intelligence investigation. The board also received a report that Huang simultaneously had a relationship with an Nvidia vice president who reported directly to him, adding a corporate governance dimension to what was already a national security matter. CFO Colette Kress, who has served as Nvidia’s CFO since 2013, is named interim CEO. She is a steady operational executive; she is not Jensen Huang. The company’s GR00T 2 robotics debut at CoRL and Isaac Lab 3.0 release are proceeding on their own technical merits — Nvidia’s products are not Huang personally — but the AI hardware market has just lost the CEO who was most able to synthesize a future-state vision for where compute, models, and robotics converge, and to sell that vision credibly to every company in the ecosystem. The Anthropic IPO roadshow, pricing the AI industry at $1.33 trillion in public markets this week, is now doing so without its most prominent hardware advocate in his seat.

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia’s CEO and board member on Thursday, ending a 33-year tenure after the company’s board accepted his resignation following disclosure that he had maintained a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, a person US intelligence considers an alleged Chinese spy who was believed to have access to Nvidia’s most sensitive AI hardware roadmaps and export strategy during the relationship — and that he simultaneously had an extramarital relationship with an Nvidia vice president who reported directly to him, as first reported by The New York Times on Wednesday; the board appointed chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim CEO effective immediately, with an executive search process for a permanent CEO beginning at once; Nvidia’s shares fell sharply on the announcement, dragging the Nasdaq lower on a day when bond markets had just reopened after Veterans Day at a 10-year yield of 4.99%, compounding the downward pressure from resumed rate trading; the FBI and Department of Justice confirmed they are investigating the nature and scope of Alice Huang’s access to Nvidia’s proprietary information.

The national security dimension is distinct from the corporate governance one and is likely to be more consequential. Nvidia’s GPU export controls to China are among the most sensitive and contested technology policy decisions of the decade. If Alice Huang had access — through Huang — to Nvidia’s export compliance strategies, customer lists, or hardware roadmaps for China-adjacent markets, the intelligence damage is not measured in stock price but in what China’s semiconductor development programs may have learned about US technology architecture and restriction strategies.

Nvidia debuted GR00T 2 and Isaac Lab 3.0 at the Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL) on Thursday — the company’s most capable humanoid robot foundation model to date — with GR00T 2 averaging 71% task success across 50 distinct manipulation and navigation tasks from a single demonstration, rising to 95% success with five demonstrations; GR00T 2’s single-demonstration performance represents the clearest benchmark yet for a robot foundation model capable of generalizing from one human example to independent execution in novel environments, the core capability that determines whether humanoid robots are commercially deployable at scale; Isaac Lab 3.0, Nvidia’s simulation platform for robotics training, enables the reinforcement learning at scale that GR00T 2’s few-shot generalization depends on; the CoRL debut arrived hours after Jensen Huang’s resignation, demonstrating that Nvidia’s technical roadmap — developed across years of research — continues independently of the CEO who built the platform it runs on.

GR00T 2’s 71% single-demo success rate is the number to benchmark against in the robotics foundation model field: it is high enough to be commercially useful for structured environments (warehouses, factory floors) while low enough to require human oversight for unstructured settings. The path from 71% to 90%+ in diverse environments is the remaining technical distance between “impressive demonstration” and “general-purpose robot worker.” Isaac Lab 3.0’s simulation infrastructure is the engine that closes that gap.

Meta launched Muse Ember on Thursday, bringing a 1-million-token context window and full multimodal capability — spanning text, image, audio, and video understanding — to Meta’s execution-focused Muse model line, available through Muse Code and the Meta Model API; Muse Ember’s 1-million-token context positions it among the longest-context frontier models commercially available, enabling workflows that require sustained reasoning over entire codebases, long documents, video archives, or multi-modal data streams without truncation; the launch arrives in a week already defined by Gemini 4 Carbon’s commercial opening and Qwen4-Max-Preview’s release, with three major AI providers simultaneously expanding the frontier of context length and multimodal capability available to developers; Meta’s pricing and infrastructure deployment details were not disclosed at launch.

A 1-million-token context window is not a marginal improvement over 200,000 tokens — it is a qualitative change in what AI models can process in a single inference call. At 1 million tokens, a model can reason over a full enterprise codebase, an entire year of corporate communications, or a complete multi-hour meeting recording without chunking or summarization that loses structural relationships. The question for Muse Ember is whether Meta’s retrieval and attention architecture can maintain reasoning quality across the full context window, not just at shorter ranges.

A coalition of advocacy groups filed suit in federal court Thursday against major AI companies including OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic, seeking a court order to pause new frontier model releases pending an independent risk assessment — citing what they described as “catastrophic” and potentially irreversible risks from insufficiently evaluated AI systems deployed at scale; the suit invokes environmental law precedents for injunctive relief to pause activities where irreversible harm cannot be undone after the fact, in a novel legal theory that the AI industry immediately characterized as legally unprecedented and factually unfounded; on the same day, New York’s Responsible AI Safety and Education (RAISE) Act formally entered into force, requiring large AI developers to conduct safety evaluations before deploying models in certain high-risk categories in New York State; and Australia’s government separately proposed the Australian AI Standards Act, which would create a targeted regulatory body for frontier AI developers — the first standalone AI regulator proposed in the Asia-Pacific region.

The advocacy lawsuit faces long legal odds — courts have generally been reluctant to impose prospective injunctions on novel technology platforms, and the “AI development as environmental harm” analogy will face significant doctrinal resistance. What matters about it is the precedent-setting attempt: if one court grants even a temporary restraining order, it creates a template for regulatory slowdown that the AI industry has never faced from the judiciary. The NY RAISE Act entering force and Australia’s proposed regulator are the quieter governance developments that will have more practical impact than any single lawsuit.
