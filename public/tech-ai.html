The Anchor · Nvidia GR00T 2 — Robots That Learn From Watching Humans

GR00T 2 is not a robot controller — it is a robot foundation model, and the distinction determines how significant Tuesday’s announcement is. A controller tells a robot what to do in a specific environment, trained for a specific task. A foundation model encodes a general capability — in this case, understanding and predicting how physical interactions unfold — that can be adapted to new tasks with minimal demonstration. Nvidia’s GR00T 2 combines recent camera views, robot state, and a written instruction to generate expected future views and the next action sequence simultaneously, training on 402,730 hours of first-person human video alongside robot teleoperation data to learn from observation of humans rather than from robot-specific programming. After just five demonstrations of a new task, GR00T 2 achieves 95% average success across 50 evaluation tasks not in the training data — a five-shot generalization result that prior robot learning systems could not approach. The model covers parallel-gripper arms, dexterous hands, and humanoid robots from a single pretrained checkpoint, meaning the same model that learns to fold laundry can be adapted to assemble electronics or navigate a warehouse. Code releases in December. For a field that has struggled with the fundamental challenge that physical AI requires task-specific programming rather than general adaptation, GR00T 2 is the clearest proof-of-concept yet that a foundation model approach is viable for robotics — not in a demo environment, but across 50 novel tasks at 95% success.

The model was pretrained on 402,730 hours of first-person human video combined with 23,500 hours of robot teleoperation data across 2,897 tasks — 92% of pretraining data is human video, not robot-specific demonstrations. GR00T 2 covers three robot form factors from one checkpoint, releases its code in December, and was developed in collaboration with Nvidia’s Isaac Lab 3.0 simulation environment.

Nvidia presented GR00T 2 at the Conference on Robot Learning in Austin on Tuesday — a 2-billion-parameter world-action model built on Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B that predicts future video frames and robot actions simultaneously, pretrained on 402,730 hours of first-person human video combined with 23,500 hours of robot teleoperation across 2,897 tasks, achieving 40% zero-shot success on 50 tasks not in the training data for a robot trained partly on human video, rising to 95% average success after just five task-specific demonstrations; the single pretrained checkpoint covers parallel-gripper arms, dexterous hands, and humanoid robots — the first robot foundation model to generalize across those three distinct form factors without task-specific retraining; GR00T 2 is built alongside Isaac Lab 3.0, Nvidia’s robot simulation environment, and the full code stack releases in December 2026 as an open model.

GR00T 2’s five-shot learning at 95% accuracy across form factors is the architecture result that makes this a foundation model and not a task controller. The 92% human video composition in pretraining is the method that makes it possible — teaching robots from the most abundant source of physical interaction data available rather than from expensive robot-specific demonstrations alone. This is the robotics field’s clearest proof of that approach.

Fireworks AI Second LLMjacking Breach Third-Party Cloud Provider October 24 Customer Billing Records Datasets Partial Model Weights Palo Alto Unit 42 Investigating Tuesday November 10 2026 Fireworks AI · Second LLMjacking Breach · Model Weights Exposed Fireworks (Tue Nov 10 2026): Oct 24 → breach → third-party → cloud → provider → billing → records → datasets → partial → model → weights → LLMjacking → Palo Alto → Unit 42 → response

Fireworks AI disclosed a second security breach on Tuesday, revealing that on October 23 a third-party cloud provider hosting Fireworks research and development machines experienced a security incident, and that attackers used that access the following day to reach Fireworks’ own development machines and launch an unauthorized workload that exposed customer account metadata, billing records including shipping addresses, invoice history and account balances, datasets belonging to some customers, and incomplete model weights — the breached data stops short of payment card numbers, which Fireworks does not store; the attack is linked to LLMjacking, a financially motivated attack class in which criminals steal access to large language models and resell their capacity through illegal routing gateways rather than exfiltrating data for resale; Fireworks has revoked compromised credentials, isolated affected systems, implemented stricter network access controls, and engaged Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 incident response division to investigate; this is the company’s second publicly disclosed breach in several weeks, making it the clearest case to date of a mid-market AI platform being targeted through third-party supply chain vulnerabilities.

LLMjacking as an attack class is distinct from traditional data theft: the attackers’ goal is to consume AI model capacity — selling API access to stolen credentials rather than exfiltrating the training data or models themselves. That Fireworks’ model weights were also exposed adds a secondary concern: partial model weights are valuable to competitors and can expose proprietary architectures. The two-breach-in-weeks pattern suggests Fireworks’ infrastructure is a persistent target, not a one-time compromise.

AI Infrastructure Loans Steep Discount Investors Selling GPU Depreciation Data Center Debt Risk Peak Valuations Financial Exposure Tuesday November 10 2026 AI Infra Loans at Discount · GPU Depreciation · Debt Risk AI infra (Tue Nov 10 2026): investors → selling → AI → chip → data center → loans → steep → discounts → GPU → depreciation → rapidly → debt → risk → peak → valuations → financial → exposure

Investors began selling AI chip and data-center infrastructure loans at steep discounts on Tuesday, as concerns about rapidly depreciating GPUs and the financial exposure of data-center operators who took on debt at peak AI valuations intensified — a development that reflects the gap between the capital committed to AI infrastructure at the height of the 2024–2025 buildout and the asset values those investments are achieving in a market where newer GPU generations are compressing the residual value of prior-generation hardware faster than financial models projected; the loan discounting is concentrated in facilities that relied on specific GPU generations for revenue projections, and in operators that locked in debt service schedules before the pace of hardware obsolescence was clearly priced; Infineon, by contrast, told analysts Tuesday that the rapid buildout of AI data centers would not slow despite growing resistance in the United States, arguing that European and Asian demand is sufficient to sustain the capex cycle independent of US backlash dynamics.

The AI infrastructure loan discount is the capital markets correcting the gap between AI buildout enthusiasm and AI hardware economics. Data center debt that was underwritten against GPU assets depreciating at 30–40% annually is worth less than the initial loan value regardless of AI demand levels. The Infineon and loan-discount stories are the two sides of the same coin: AI demand may be sustained, but the financial structures built to serve it are being repriced.

German semiconductor giant Infineon told investors on Tuesday that the rapid buildout of AI data centers would not slow despite growing resistance in the United States — arguing that European and Asian demand for AI computing infrastructure is robust enough to sustain the global capex cycle independently of US regulatory or political headwinds — a statement that runs counter to the AI infrastructure loan discount story unfolding simultaneously in US capital markets but is consistent with the view that AI demand is a global structural trend, not a US-specific investment cycle dependent on US policy support; Infineon supplies components across the AI data center supply chain beyond GPUs, including power management, connectivity, and sensor chips, giving the company a broader visibility into data center buildout velocity than pure-play GPU makers; the company’s assessment adds a European industrial perspective to a debate dominated by US financial market signals.

Infineon seeing continued AI data center boom and US investors discounting AI infra loans in the same week is the clearest illustration of the two-speed AI infrastructure reality: the global demand signal is robust, but the US financial structures built to serve the last buildout cycle are being repriced as hardware depreciation catches up with debt schedules. Both can be true simultaneously.
