The Anchor · Friday's Relief Versus Monday's Reality — What Equities Carry Into the FOMC Week

The four-day equity losing streak that CPI ended on Friday was a technically correct market response to a technically correct data point: the S&P 500 sold down across four sessions to price a 25 basis point September hike at approximately 65% probability; Friday's CPI at 3.4% annually confirmed that 25bp at 65% probability was the right number; selling stopped. The relief is mechanical. But the factors that drove those four sessions of selling have not resolved — they have strengthened. The 10-year Treasury closed at 4.89%, CPI is stuck at 3.4%, PPI was hot Thursday, Brent crude surged to $107.40 on Friday's Houthi Mokha seizure, and CME FedWatch has since moved to 69.4% from 65%. Saturday brings the BRICS summit — not a market event directly, but a geopolitical context for the oil price that Monday's session will open in. The Houthi foothold in Mokha has not resolved. There are no reports of a coalition counter-offensive through Saturday morning. What this means for Monday's session is straightforward: the equity market re-opens with the same CPI relief intact (the data has not changed) but with Brent crude that has not retreated, a rate-hike probability that has risen four points, and a geopolitical backdrop in the Middle East that has not improved since Friday's $107.40 Brent print. The structural story for the FOMC week is therefore: equities relieved from the four-day losing streak but facing a Tuesday decision in which the data — 3.4% CPI, hot PPI, $107 Brent, 162K August payrolls — makes a hold argument difficult to construct. The 25bp hike is the base case. What matters is whether the Fed signals pause or continuation. If it signals pause, the market can begin pricing a rate-cut timeline for early 2027 and equity relief extends. If it signals continuation, the four-session relief is the bounce before a second leg. The weekend will not answer that question. Tuesday will.

Brent crude holds at $107.40 through Saturday — Friday's close after a 4.87% single-session surge on the Houthi seizure of Mokha — with no reports of a coalition counter-offensive to dislodge the Houthi foothold near the Bab el-Mandab Strait through Saturday morning. WTI holds at $101.80. The dual chokepoint structure — Iran's naval exclusion zone at the Strait of Hormuz and the Houthi territorial foothold within 50 miles of the Bab el-Mandab — remains intact heading into the weekend, with no market mechanism or diplomatic event scheduled to reduce either risk by Monday's re-open.

The BRICS Summit in New Delhi — now underway on its first day, Saturday — is the only active diplomatic forum where a ceasefire conversation might be seeded, given that Iran's Pezeshkian is attending and India's Modi is chairing. But a BRICS summit communique is not a ceasefire mechanism: it requires the US and Israel to stop operations, neither of which is a BRICS member. What the BRICS summit can produce in the oil context is a signal that one or more members will actively pursue a ceasefire framework — which would be a forward-looking statement, not an immediate operational development. For Monday's oil market: $107 Brent is the structural baseline, the Mokha foothold is intact, and the only near-term development that would move oil meaningfully lower is a ceasefire signal credible enough to reduce the strategic chokepoint premium. That signal is not available as of Saturday morning.

The FOMC rate decision Tuesday, September 16 — four days from Saturday — has moved to 69.4% probability of a 25 basis point hike on CME FedWatch, up from approximately 65% at Friday's close, as the weekend has confirmed rather than challenged the accumulated CPI, PPI, payrolls, and energy data that makes the case for hiking. The 10-year Treasury closed at 4.89% Friday; gold closed at $4,468 with a 0.59% mild safe-haven bid. The 25bp hike is the base case; the statement's signal on whether September is a pause or a step in continued tightening is the key variable for Q4 rate path and equity and crypto pricing through year-end.

The distinction between a pause signal and a continuation signal matters more than the hike itself at this stage: 25bp is already priced at 69.4%. What the market does not know is whether 25bp is the last hike of this cycle or the next one in a sequence. A pause signal allows the market to begin pricing rate cuts in early 2027, which would be a meaningful tailwind for equities and crypto in Q4 regardless of the near-term hike. A continuation signal keeps the rate environment elevated into year-end and reinforces the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets. The Fed's argument for a pause — that $107 Brent and 3.4% CPI are transitory — requires believing the oil market will resolve before the next inflation data point arrives. Given that the Houthi Mokha foothold has not resolved through Saturday morning, that argument has not become easier to make since Friday's close. Tuesday will determine which reading of the same data set prevails.
