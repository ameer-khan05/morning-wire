The Anchor · The Week’s Verdict — Markets Absorb First Rate Hike Since 2023; Anthropic $2T IPO Reframes AI Valuations for Monday

The equity market wrote its verdict on the Fed’s first rate hike since 2023 over two sessions: semiconductors up nearly 3% Thursday and up nearly 3% again Friday, the S&P 500 essentially flat on the week, the 10-year yield below 5% for two consecutive days after Wednesday’s hike. The specific verdict is precise: AI infrastructure spending is rate-insensitive at 3.75%–4.00%. The companies building the chips that AI runs on are not slowing procurement because of a 25 basis point rate increase, and the market is pricing that read explicitly by sending the most rate-sensitive AI capex proxy — semiconductors — higher on both days immediately following the hike. The Berkshire succession generated the week’s dominant corporate headline — Warren Buffett’s 56-year chairmanship ending, Howard Buffett assuming the chairmanship, Greg Abel continuing as operational successor — but Berkshire shares absorbed the news without the kind of volatility that would suggest the market is pricing a large Buffett premium loss. The market’s initial read is that the succession is orderly and that the brand’s operational advantages survive the title transfer; the actual premium test will come when Berkshire next deploys capital and counterparties reveal whether the “elephant gun” still fires at the same terms. Anthropic’s $2 trillion IPO announcement on Saturday resets the AI sector valuation conversation before Monday’s open. At $2 trillion on approximately $65 billion in current revenue and a trajectory toward $190 to $200 billion in projected 2028 revenue, the IPO asks public markets to price a frontier AI lab the way they price high-growth infrastructure franchises during their peak expansion phase. The comparison set — Nvidia at peak AI pricing, Microsoft during its cloud transition — suggests the market has the framework to underwrite the multiple; the question is whether the uncertainty premium on a private company becoming public applies, and at what magnitude. The $100 billion raise itself is the more significant number for Monday’s reaction: if Anthropic successfully raises $100 billion in a single public offering, it establishes that public equity markets can absorb frontier AI capital formation at a scale that has previously required private syndication. That changes the financing landscape for every AI lab that needs to raise capital in the next 18 months.

Friday’s session closed out the post-FOMC week with equities mixed but resilient: S&P 500 at 7,650, Nasdaq at 26,522, Dow at 51,682, with semiconductors rising nearly 3% for the second consecutive session and the 10-year Treasury yield holding at 4.992% — below the 5% level for two consecutive days following Wednesday’s rate hike. WTI crude eased 1.56% to $100.51 on post-FOMC dollar strength as the physical supply disruption from the Yanbu strike remained unresolved but the panic premium from Wednesday faded. Gold eased 0.30% to $4,312 as the equity recovery continued to pull haven demand lower.

The week’s final session confirmed the week’s verdict: the market absorbed the first rate hike since 2023 without major dislocation. The S&P 500 finished the post-FOMC week essentially flat from its pre-hike Wednesday level, semiconductors outperformed twice in a row, and the 10-year yield stayed below 5% — the bond market’s own judgment that the hiking cycle is measured rather than accelerating. Two consecutive days of semiconductor strength in the immediate post-hike window is the AI capex thesis written empirically: chip procurement is not slowing at 3.75%–4.00%, and the market is pricing that read explicitly. Oil giving back 1.5% on dollar strength while the physical disruption is unresolved is the key divergence: the commodity price is moving on currency rather than on the underlying supply situation, which has not materially improved.

Anthropic’s Saturday announcement that it is targeting a $2 trillion November IPO — raising up to $100 billion in the largest US technology public offering in history — is the weekend’s market-moving news and will set the AI sector valuation conversation for Monday’s open. At $2 trillion, Anthropic would list alongside Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, and Alphabet as one of the five most valuable US public companies, establishing the first public-market reference price for a frontier AI model-layer company as distinct from AI infrastructure hardware. The $100 billion raise itself is the structural milestone: if Anthropic successfully raises $100 billion in a public offering, it proves that public equity markets can absorb AI capital formation at the scale that has previously required private syndication.

The implications for adjacent equities are immediate. Microsoft, which holds a significant Anthropic stake alongside its OpenAI relationship, will be directly affected by the IPO pricing. Nvidia, whose GPU hardware underlies Anthropic’s compute infrastructure, will be priced in a new context: if the model-layer company is worth $2 trillion, what does that imply for the chip companies that supply the training and inference compute? AI ETFs and thematic indices will need to incorporate a new $2 trillion member if the IPO proceeds. The November target gives the market roughly six to seven weeks to price these implications before the offering. The revenue trajectory — $65 billion in July 2026 on a path toward $110 billion annualized by year-end — is the number that will anchor analyst models. The valuation multiple ask — approximately 10 to 11 times projected 2028 revenue — is the debate.

The geopolitical calendar for the coming week is the most market-sensitive since the FOMC itself: the UNGA General Debate opens Tuesday, September 22, with the US-Iran war, Ukraine, Gaza, and AI governance on the agenda, and the Trump-Xi White House summit follows on September 24 — a bilateral meeting between the world’s two largest economies with trade terms, Taiwan, and the Iran war expected to top the agenda. Oil markets remain structurally exposed: WTI at $100.51 and Brent at $104.00 reflect dollar-driven easing rather than supply resolution, with the Yanbu refinery strike unresolved and Houthi operations in the Red Sea continuing after the attempted Mecca strike raised the conflict’s symbolic threshold.

The Trump-Xi summit is the coming week’s highest market-variance event. The two possible outcomes — a tariff truce that relieves trade tension or a failure to resolve differences that extends uncertainty — have asymmetric market effects: a tariff relief outcome is broadly positive for equities, particularly in industrials and consumer goods exposed to US-China trade; a failure or an escalation on Taiwan adds risk premium to a market that has already absorbed a rate hike this week. UNGA’s Iran war discussion is the oil market’s key watch: any escalation signal from the General Debate — new Houthi operations, new strikes on Gulf energy infrastructure, or Iranian military movement — has the potential to push WTI back above $104, erasing the dollar-driven easing of the past two days.
