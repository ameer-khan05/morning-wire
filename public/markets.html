Quick hits

  • The specific mechanism by which WTI's Monday morning price affects Tuesday's CPI interpretation is worth mapping precisely, because it is often described vaguely. WTI crude is not directly in the CPI basket — gasoline is, and gasoline prices respond to WTI with a 1-4 week lag depending on how quickly refiners and retailers pass through crude cost changes. The August CPI report being released Tuesday reflects prices collected from early August through mid-August, which means it already incorporates gasoline prices from the period after Friday's WTI close at $77.08. A Monday WTI move to $78.82 is too late to affect gasoline prices already captured in the August CPI sample. Where Monday's WTI move does matter for Tuesday is in analysts' real-time commentary and interpretation: if CPI comes in at 2.8% (slightly above consensus 2.7%), commentary that notes "energy prices are rising again on the Iran premium" gives the 2.8% print an inflationary context that makes it read more negatively than a bare-numbers comparison would suggest. Markets respond to interpretation as much as to numbers in ambiguous-data scenarios, and 2.7-2.9% is the range where this commentary effect is most significant.
  • The eclipse on Tuesday (August 12) is worth noting as a market-microstructure footnote: CPI releases at 8:30am ET, and the August 12 totality over Iceland begins at 09:27 UTC (5:27am ET). The eclipse sequence on Tuesday morning in European time zones means the eclipse occurs during the European pre-market trading window — European equity and fixed-income traders who are eclipse-watching at 11:27am CEST (09:27 UTC) are the same traders whose participation in US-dollar-denominated futures and Treasury markets is typically active in the 6-9am ET window. The effect is not large enough to be a significant market-microstructure concern, but thin pre-US-open European participation in the CPI reaction window has historically amplified initial moves in Treasury futures before US bond markets open at 8:20am ET (Treasury futures) or 8:30am ET (when CPI releases). Tuesday's eclipse-day CPI morning is unusual enough to note without overstating the effect.
