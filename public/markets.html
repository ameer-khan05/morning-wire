Quick hits

  • The core CPI at +0.2% month-over-month (versus the +0.1% consensus) is the one data point in Wednesday's report that NBC News characterized as "stubborn" inflation. Understanding why the market accepted the benign headline (3.4% YoY) over the slightly-above-consensus core (+0.2% MoM) requires knowing what the Fed and bond markets weight most heavily. The Fed has communicated throughout 2026 that it is focused primarily on whether the trend is toward 2% on a 12-month basis — not whether each monthly reading is exactly at target — and 3.4% YoY continuing a multi-month downward trend from 3.5% is on-trend. The core's +0.2% MoM (versus +0.1% consensus) is a mild upside surprise, but 0.2% monthly core implies approximately 2.4% annualized core CPI, which is close enough to 2% on the Fed's trend-watching basis to not disrupt the rate-cut narrative on its own. What would disrupt the narrative is two or three consecutive months of +0.2%+ core while oil remains at $88+, because that combination would suggest that Hormuz energy costs are beginning to flow into core services and goods pricing — the "second-round" inflation effect that the Fed most fears from an oil supply shock. One month of +0.2% core at the current oil level is noise; three months would be signal. Wednesday was one month.
  • Cisco's Thursday earnings report is the AI infrastructure bellwether that markets genuinely need for forward growth visibility in a way that the CPI (a backward-looking price index) cannot provide. Cisco's relevance to the AI infrastructure story is its networking and data center connectivity products — the switches, routers, and optical interconnects that connect AI training clusters together and connect those clusters to the internet. As AI model training has scaled (Anthropic's $71 billion compute commitment announced this week is the clearest illustration), the networking infrastructure that connects the compute nodes has scaled with it. Cisco's AI-related revenue growth (or its absence) is a direct measure of whether the AI infrastructure buildout is translating from announced compute commitments ($71 billion for Anthropic, hundreds of billions across AWS, Azure, Google) into actual silicon-and-fiber deployment at Cisco's product level. A strong Cisco quarter validates the AI infrastructure buildout as a capital expenditure reality, not just an announcement reality — and that validation matters for the entire Nasdaq's AI-premium valuation, which is currently trading at a forward P/E that assumes the buildout is real. A miss would be the AI-capital-expenditure verification problem that would, for the first time in summer 2026, put the Nasdaq's AI premium under genuine scrutiny from a data source more reliable than lab announcements.
