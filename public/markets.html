US equities recorded their best single session since April 2025 Monday: the S&P 500 rose 4.73% to 7,237.17; the Nasdaq surged 6.51% to 24,089.56 — its best day since April 2025 — recovering more than half last week’s 10% weekly loss in a single session; the Dow climbed 3.01% to 49,595.31; and the Russell 2000 rose 3.40% to 2,669, as Nvidia surged 31% — adding approximately $1 trillion in market value — following the unanimous reinstatement of Jensen Huang as CEO after the board received US government sign‑off on the national security question; Anthropic’s IPO debuted Monday alongside new I-bond pricing at 5.51% yield and after-hours earnings from Intuit, Ross Stores, and Gap; oil sold off sharply, with WTI crude falling 3.9% to $80.17 and Brent losing 3.7% to $86.66 on demand-side concerns; gold rose 3.2% to $3,651; and the 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.92% as the week’s safety rotation unwound into risk assets.

Monday’s market story is a governance resolution priced into a session. The selloff began when Huang resigned. It did not fully begin, the market now says, because of AI demand concerns — it began because of uncertainty about who would lead the company that makes the chips that run AI. When that uncertainty resolved Monday morning, the market re-rated AI stocks within hours.

The Anchor · The Speed Test — What It Means That the AI Market Recovered More Than Half Last Week’s Worst Selloff Since 2020 in a Single Session, Triggered by a Single CEO Reinstatement

The AI market lost 10% last week. It recovered more than half of that in one session Monday. The catalyst was one board vote. That speed says something precise. The Nasdaq’s 6.51% gain Monday — recovering more than half of last week’s 10.02% weekly loss in a single session — is a market verdict about what last week’s selloff actually was. The speed of Monday’s recovery, triggered by a single corporate governance event, says the selloff was primarily a risk premium on uncertainty about Nvidia’s leadership, not a revaluation of AI demand. A fundamental selloff — one driven by evidence that AI demand was overstated, that revenue guidance was misleading, that the SEC probe had found actionable violations — does not reverse in hours because a CEO returns. A governance selloff does. The market had priced a 33% discount on Nvidia from peak while Huang was absent. When he was reinstated, that governance discount unwound. The question the market spent last week unable to answer — who runs Nvidia — was answered Monday morning. The market’s response time measured in hours rather than days or weeks is the most direct evidence that this was the question the selloff was actually pricing. What remains open: the SEC revenue-reporting probe into Nvidia’s export control compliance has not been resolved by the CEO reinstatement. The structural AI capex thesis validation question has not been answered. And the December FOMC meeting remains a macro catalyst on the horizon. Monday resolved the governance question. The others are still pending.

Nvidia surged 31% — adding approximately $1 trillion in market value in the biggest single-day stock gain ever recorded for a company of its capitalization — after the board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO Monday morning, following US government confirmation that the reinstatement posed no national security risk; two analysts upgraded Nvidia immediately following the announcement, per CNBC; the Nasdaq rose 6.51% to 24,089.56 — its best day since April 2025 — and the S&P 500 gained 4.73% to 7,237.17, with the Dow rising 3.01% to 49,595.31; Yahoo Finance reported the session as the market’s strongest single day since April 2025 — recovering more than half last week’s selloff — with the Motley Fool describing it as a “historic AI rebound.”

Monday’s 6.51% Nasdaq gain says the selloff was priced on a governance question, not a demand question. When the governance question resolved — Huang reinstated, national security cleared — the governance discount unwound. What did not resolve: the SEC revenue-reporting probe into Nvidia’s export control accounting is still open, the December FOMC is still a macro event, and the AI capex thesis still needs validation from revenue. Monday answered the CEO question. The other questions remain open for the market to price in the weeks ahead.

Anthropic’s public debut on Monday is a market event that the week’s selloff and rebound both contextualize. The company priced its IPO during a period when AI stocks had just suffered their worst week since March 2020. Its debut session happened to coincide with one of the strongest single-day AI recoveries in years. The IPO pricing versus the debut session price spread — whenever it is disclosed — will be the market’s first real-time measure of how investors valued Anthropic against last week’s AI selloff context.

WTI Crude fell 3.9% to $80.17 and Brent lost 3.7% to $86.66 on Monday as the risk-on AI rally drew capital into equities rather than energy, with demand concerns from the week’s macro uncertainty outweighing the geopolitical supply premium from the Ukraine conflict — JournalArta reported Friday futures had surged on the tech rout while oil slid in the same session, confirming the inverse relationship between AI risk-on and oil geopolitical premium on this cycle; gold rose 3.2% to $3,651 — gaining on the same day equities rose strongly — a divergence from the typical risk-on pattern where gold falls as equities gain, possibly reflecting the dual support of climate risk premia and geopolitical uncertainty persisting alongside the equity bounce; and the 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.92% from Friday’s 4.72% close as the safety rotation that drove last week’s bond rally partially unwound into equities, with 20 basis points of yield increase reflecting money moving from bonds back into stocks.

The simultaneous rise in gold and equities is Monday’s most unusual market signal. Standard risk-on behavior sends money out of gold and into stocks. Monday saw both rise, possibly because the news driving the equity rally — a CEO reinstatement — did not resolve the structural uncertainty about climate, geopolitics, and macro that was supporting gold’s safe-haven demand. The equity market repriced on Monday. The underlying conditions that made gold attractive last week are still present.
