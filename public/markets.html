The Anchor · In-Line CPI Versus $107 Brent — Two Different Market Reactions to the Same Friday Morning

The equity market and the oil market both behaved rationally on Friday, and they produced opposite outcomes. Equities broke a four-day losing streak because the CPI print at 3.4% annual confirmed that inflation is not re-accelerating above what the Fed has already priced — the 65% rate-hike probability holds, but there is no 75bp scenario and no panic repricing. In an equity market that had priced in a high-25bp-hike probability across four losing sessions, "in-line" is genuinely relief: you sold down to price a 25bp hike, and the data confirmed it is 25bp, so you don't need to sell further. The oil market had a completely different Friday morning. Brent's 4.87% single-session surge — from $102.30 to $107.40 — is driven by the Houthi seizure of Mokha, which changes the strategic geometry of the Iran war's supply-disruption picture. Since the conflict began, the oil threat had two components: Iranian exclusion of the Strait of Hormuz and Houthi interdiction of the Bab el-Mandab Strait. Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandab are at opposite ends of the Arabian Peninsula and the Red Sea, respectively. Until Friday, the Houthi presence at the Bab el-Mandab was a naval interdiction capability — missiles and drones deployed from Houthi-controlled inland positions in Yemen. The seizure of Mokha changes this. Mokha is the closest major Yemeni port city to the Bab el-Mandab Strait — less than 50 miles away. A Houthi foothold in Mokha means shore-based firing positions for weapons, direct observation of shipping lanes, and a forward operating base from which to enforce a direct exclusion zone. The CNBC headline — "Houthis seize Yemen port, raising threat to oil and shipping" — is accurate but understates the strategic shift: this is the difference between a Houthi naval threat to the Bab el-Mandab and a Houthi territorial threat to the Bab el-Mandab. $107 Brent reflects the beginning of that repricing, not its end. The equity market ended Friday relieved about Tuesday; the oil market ended Friday alarmed about Monday. Both were right.

Brent crude surged above $107 in a single Friday session — up 4.87% from Thursday's $102.30 close — after Iran-backed Houthi forces seized Yemen's port city of Mokha, the closest major city to the Bab el-Mandab Strait, in the largest territorial gain by Iran-aligned forces since the 2022 ceasefire; Vantage Markets notes Brent "holds above $107 on Houthi attacks." WTI crossed $101 as US Central Command separately announced it had permanently disabled three Iranian oil tankers in the Persian Gulf, and the IRGC reportedly fired 20 ballistic missiles at a US airbase in Jordan, extending the conflict to Jordanian territory.

The strategic shift from Friday's Houthi advance is qualitative, not just quantitative. Since the Iran war began, the Bab el-Mandab threat has been a naval interdiction capability: missiles and drones fired from inland Yemen. Mokha changes this to a territorial foothold: shore-based weapons, observation posts, and forward positions within 50 miles of the strait. A territory-based Bab el-Mandab threat is harder to degrade by naval strike and easier to sustain under supply from Iran than a purely naval threat. Combined with the active Hormuz exclusion zone, Friday's development means both Arabian Sea chokepoints are under simultaneous elevated threat — from the Strait of Hormuz in the north and the Bab el-Mandab in the south. $107 Brent is the market's initial repricing of that dual chokepoint threat. The prior ceiling of $100-$103 reflected one confirmed chokepoint disruption. $107 reflects two converging ones.

The FOMC rate decision Tuesday, September 16 — five days from today — remains the structural resolution event for a week in which PPI Thursday and CPI Friday both confirmed that the inflationary environment is exactly as elevated as the Fed has assessed it to be. The 10-year Treasury yield ticked up to 4.89% on Friday, reflecting the CPI confirmation, with rate-hike probability remaining at approximately 65% for a 25 basis point move. Gold rose 0.59% to $4,468, a mild safe-haven bid as the Houthi Mokha escalation added to the geopolitical premium, though the dominant market signal Friday was CPI, not geopolitics.

The most important element of Tuesday's FOMC decision is not the hike itself — 25bp is the market's base case and largely priced — but the accompanying statement's signal on pause versus continuation. If the Fed hikes and signals a pause, markets will interpret September as the cycle peak and begin pricing a rate-cut timeline. If the Fed hikes and signals openness to further tightening, markets will reprice Q4 and 2027 higher across the curve. Given $107 Brent — 25% above the oil price at the Fed's last decision — and a CPI print that is sticky at 3.4%, the "pause" signal would require the Fed to argue that current conditions are transitory, which the oil market has explicitly not cooperated with. The Mokha escalation on Friday makes the "transitory" argument harder to sustain going into Tuesday.
