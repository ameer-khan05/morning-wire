Markets · Deep Dive · S&P 7,554 ▲1.0% · CoreWeave +10.7% · Veterans Day Bond Close · Hormuz Bid Oil · Wednesday, November 11, 2026

US equities rebounded sharply on Wednesday, Veterans Day, as AI and semiconductor stocks led a broad recovery from Tuesday’s CPI-driven selloff — CoreWeave surged 10.7% after reporting a smaller-than-expected quarterly loss, Nvidia gained 2.9%, and Micron and Broadcom each rose 5.6% — lifting the S&P 500 1.0% to 7,554 and the Nasdaq 1.6% to 25,532; the bond market was closed for the holiday, holding the 10-year Treasury yield at Tuesday’s closing level of 4.97% and removing the rate-driven headwind that had dominated the prior session; the Dow rose a more modest 0.2% to 52,033, with the disparity between the tech-heavy Nasdaq’s 1.6% gain and the Dow’s 0.2% gain confirming that Wednesday’s recovery was concentrated in the AI and semiconductor sectors rather than broad-based; WTI crude rose 0.7% to $86.63 and Brent rose 0.7% to $88.10 as Hormuz shipping security concerns added a fresh geopolitical risk premium following Iran’s security chief warning that any new US strikes would be met with attacks on American military bases; gold fell 0.7% to $4,048 as equities recovered and rate expectations remained elevated.

Wednesday’s markets session is a one-day reprieve from Tuesday’s CPI correction, powered by AI earnings and the structural relief of the bond market’s holiday closure. It is not a signal that the inflation problem has changed — it has not. When the 10-year reopens Thursday, the December rate-hike calculus resumes exactly where it left off at 4.97%.

The Anchor · CoreWeave Earnings — The AI Infrastructure Surprise That Reversed Tuesday

CoreWeave reported earnings on Tuesday evening that the market had not priced: a smaller quarterly loss than analysts expected, with revenue growth that confirmed the AI cloud infrastructure buildout was still accelerating despite Tuesday’s concerns about AI infrastructure loan discounting. The market opened Wednesday with a rebound led by everything AI touched. CoreWeave’s earnings beat mattered because it arrived as a direct counter to Tuesday’s AI infrastructure loan discount story — the narrative that GPU-backed data center debt was being repriced at steep discounts due to rapid hardware depreciation. CoreWeave’s numbers said the opposite: its AI cloud revenue was growing faster than its losses were widening, and the customer demand that fills its data centers was robust enough to absorb costs that equity markets had worried about. That is the data point tech investors had been waiting for since Tuesday. Nvidia gained 2.9% not because anything changed at Nvidia, but because CoreWeave’s numbers validated the demand side of the AI infrastructure thesis. Micron and Broadcom each gained 5.6% for the same reason — beneficiaries of the same AI infrastructure buildout that CoreWeave is demonstrating is profitable. The bond market’s Veterans Day closure is the structural background condition: with no active 10-year trading, the 4.97% yield from Tuesday’s close held as a static number all day, removing the real-time rate pressure that had driven Tuesday’s selloff. Equities do not get a true reprieve from inflation until the November CPI data arrives on December 10 — the last reading before the December 11 FOMC decision. Wednesday’s session is a gap in the pressure, not a resolution of it.

CoreWeave reported a smaller-than-expected quarterly loss late Tuesday, with revenue growth in its AI cloud services division exceeding analyst forecasts — a print that directly counter-signaled the AI infrastructure loan discount story that had contributed to Tuesday’s selloff and sent CoreWeave shares up 10.7% on Wednesday, triggering a broad rally in AI and semiconductor names including Nvidia (+2.9%), Micron (+5.6%), and Broadcom (+5.6%) that lifted the S&P 500 1.0% to 7,554 and the Nasdaq 1.6% to 25,532; the AI-concentrated nature of the rebound — Dow up only 0.2%, Russell 2000 up only 0.3% — confirms that Wednesday’s recovery is sector-specific rather than a broad macro reversal; CoreWeave’s numbers matter as a demand-side data point: its AI cloud revenue growing while its losses narrow means the AI infrastructure buildout has not yet reached demand saturation, countering the concern that GPU-backed data center debt was being repriced for deteriorating demand.

The divergence between the Nasdaq (+1.6%) and the Dow (+0.2%) is the most informative market fact from Wednesday: it confirms the recovery is entirely about AI earnings and entirely not about anything else. The inflation data has not changed, the Fed’s December calculus has not changed, and the geopolitical picture has gotten more complex. What changed is one company’s quarterly loss was smaller than expected, and the market used that as permission to buy everything AI.

The US bond market was closed Wednesday for Veterans Day, holding the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.97% — Tuesday’s close after jumping 11 basis points on the hot October CPI report — and removing the real-time rate-driven pressure that had dominated Tuesday’s equity session; the bond market’s closure is a structural one-day relief valve for equities: without active rate repricing, equities can trade on earnings and sentiment rather than on the yield level that Tuesday established; the 10-year at 4.97% represents the bond market’s pricing of a December 25-basis-point Fed rate hike at 87.3% odds — a level that has not changed and will resume repricing when the bond market opens Thursday; the next significant bond market catalyst is the November CPI data release on December 10, the last inflation reading before the December 11 FOMC decision.

The bond market being closed on a day when equities are rebounding on AI earnings is not coincidental to the size of the rebound — it is a contributing cause. Without the 10-year trading, there is no incremental rate signal to weigh against CoreWeave’s earnings surprise. The full pressure environment that produced Tuesday’s decline resumes Thursday. Wednesday is a gap in the data, not a resolution of the inflation concern.

WTI crude rose 0.7% to $86.63 and Brent rose 0.7% to $88.10 on Wednesday, partially reversing Tuesday’s declines as Hormuz shipping security concerns added a fresh geopolitical risk premium — Iran’s national security chief Mohsen Rezaei warned that any new US strikes on Iran would be met with direct attacks on American military bases — offsetting the modest de-escalation signal from Secretary Rubio’s assertion that large-scale combat operations against Iran were no longer necessary; oil’s reversal from Tuesday’s peace-priced decline to Wednesday’s security-threat bid reflects the inherent volatility in the Iran-related oil risk premium: the market trades a mixture of Rubio’s framing and Rezaei’s response simultaneously, and when the two signals diverge within the same 24-hour window, the resulting price is a weighted average of two contradictory views on where the conflict is heading.

Oil’s 0.7% gain on Wednesday while equities are also rising is unusual — typically, an equity-led risk-on session reduces oil’s geopolitical risk premium as confidence in economic stability rises. That oil is rising alongside equities here says the Iran threat signal is dominating the geopolitical oil calculus, independent of what equities are doing. When the Abu Dhabi talks and the Rubio/Rezaei standoff evolve further Thursday, oil’s direction will tell us which narrative the market trusts more.

Gold fell 0.7% to $4,048 on Wednesday as recovering equity markets reduced demand for safe-haven assets and the 10-year yield’s effective hold at 4.97% — even with the bond market closed — maintained the rate level that makes Treasuries more attractive than gold as a safe-haven alternative; gold has now fallen approximately 1.5% over two sessions from Monday’s pre-CPI level of $4,109 as the inflation data printed hotter than expected and the December rate-hike probability surged to 87.3%; the daily pattern — gold down when equities are down on inflation concerns, gold down when equities are up on AI earnings — describes an asset class that is currently losing to both inflation risk (which raises real rates) and equity risk appetite (which diverts safe-haven flows); gold’s structural support argument — that high and persistent inflation is ultimately gold-bullish — is being overwhelmed in the near term by the Fed’s hawkish response to that inflation, which raises the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset.

Gold down on a day when equities are up and bonds are closed completes the picture from Tuesday: gold is currently caught between two bearish forces simultaneously. Rate hike expectations make Treasuries more attractive. AI earnings-led equity recovery makes equities more attractive. Gold wins when neither Treasuries nor equities look good — and that moment has not arrived yet in this cycle.
