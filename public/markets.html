Monday delivered simultaneous record closes for the S&P 500 (7,600.50, ▲1.48%) and the Dow (53,178.41, ▲1.32%) — the strongest single-session equity rally since the Iran conflict began February 28 — as oil's sustained retreat toward $80 fed directly into lower-rates optimism that compressed the equity risk premium, Palantir's 17% pre-market surge energized the AI software sector, and the Q2 earnings beat rate continued its run above 88%; the Nasdaq gained 2.13% and the Russell 2000 added 1.73%, confirming that the rally was broad-based across both large-cap growth and rate-sensitive small-cap names. WTI closed at $80.09 (▼4.61% on the day) and Brent at $83.87, sustaining the oil-level improvement that markets began pricing on Monday morning. Tonight's SpaceX and AMD results and Friday's Jobs Report are the week's two remaining scheduled events.

The record closes are the market's verdict on the question that defined the Iran conflict's macro impact since February 28: if oil stays below $80, does the improvement in September rate-cut probability translate into durable equity re-rating? Monday answered yes — two record closes on a day when the Iran diplomatic picture remained contradictory but the oil level held. Friday's Jobs Report will determine whether that re-rating has macro confirmation or requires a correction before the next leg.

SpaceX earnings (tonight after bell): first public quarterly report. AMD earnings (tonight after bell): AI chip revenue context. Jobs Report (Friday Aug 7): primary week macro catalyst. The record closes are doing something more specific than "markets go up." The S&P at 7,600 and Dow at 53,178 are the market's expression of a specific repricing: the equity risk premium that was elevated during 34 days of Iran conflict — compressed in the earnings beat rate and in the oil-sensitive sectors — is coming down as the probability of Hormuz reopening and its rate-cut channel consequence rises. The records are not exuberance; they are a calibration. The calibration can be wrong if the Jobs Report Friday is strong (labor market is too hot for September cut), if Iran reverses (oil returns above $85), or if SpaceX's earnings tonight reveal something disqualifying about the AI-and-space capital allocation thesis. Until one of those scenarios resolves against the current repricing, the record closes are the market's rational expression of the improved macro probability it is observing.

The 23-basis-point UST 10Y decline from 4.70% to 4.47% in 11 sessions is the bond market's simultaneous pricing of two distinct improvements: first, the AI-capex-returns validation from last week's cloud earnings that addressed the primary driver of the bond revolt (AI spending without demonstrated returns); and second, the oil-CPI channel improvement from WTI near $80 that weakens the secondary driver of the revolt (energy inflation sustaining hawkish FOMC votes). Both conditions improving simultaneously is unusual — it requires both the earnings data and the geopolitical environment to cooperate in the same direction over the same time period. That coincidence is what produced the 23-bp bond rally. Whether it continues through Friday's Jobs Report depends on whether labor market data completes the trifecta (weak jobs + low oil + AI returns = September cut certainty) or introduces the third variable that complicates the current pricing.

Monday's record closes — S&P 500 at 7,600.50 (▲1.48%) and Dow at 53,178.41 (▲1.32%) — were produced by the same mechanism that drove Monday morning's oil-futures move: sustained WTI near $80 improving the September rate-cut probability that FOMC hike dissenters have held against for 34 days, with the improvement now entering its second consecutive session rather than reverting to the prior oil level. The UST 10Y's continued decline to 4.47% — 23 basis points below the 4.70% revolt high from the previous Wednesday — reflects the bond market's sustained pricing of the improved September cut probability, and that bond improvement directly compresses the equity risk premium that had been elevated under higher-for-longer expectations, enabling the equity multiple expansion that produced the record closes. WTI closed at $80.09 rather than retesting Monday morning's $79.54 low, which means the oil improvement sustained through a full session without reversing — a more durable signal than an intraday touch of the $80 threshold.

The Nasdaq's 2.13% outperformance of the S&P's 1.48% reflects the disproportionate sensitivity of growth and AI-adjacent stocks to interest rate expectations — long-duration growth equities benefit more from rate-cut probability improvements because their valuation is more sensitive to discount rate changes. The Russell 2000's 1.73% gain shows that the rate-cut signal reached small-cap equities as well, which typically benefit more than large-caps from lower rates due to their higher reliance on floating-rate debt. Broad sector participation across size and rate sensitivity is a marker of a genuine macro repricing rather than sector rotation — it confirms that oil-driven rate-cut probability is the driver, not a specific sector narrative.

Friday's US Jobs Report arrives in the most favorable oil-and-bond configuration for a September rate-cut probability improvement that the market has seen since the Iran conflict began February 28: WTI has held near $80 for two consecutive sessions, the UST 10Y has declined 23 basis points from its revolt high to 4.47%, and the FOMC hike dissenters' energy-CPI argument is materially weaker than it was a week ago — meaning the Jobs Report result will interact with a macro environment already priced for improved September cut probability, and a soft print will compound rather than initiate the repricing. The most bullish configuration for September cut probability is a Jobs Report that shows slowing labor market growth — not a recession signal, but below the pace that would give hawkish dissenters a second simultaneous argument alongside the energy-CPI argument they are already losing.

Gold's Monday close at $4,059 — below Monday morning's $4,111.50 high and up just 0.57% versus Friday's close — reflects the partial fading of the safe-haven premium that had coexisted with oil's decline. The divergence between gold and oil has not fully resolved — gold remains elevated on an absolute basis relative to pre-conflict levels — but the direction is consistent with the market pricing a framework deal as more likely than a deal breakdown, even as Trump's "unbelievably duplicitous" public language suggests diplomatic friction continues through the Oman back-channel.

Quick hits

  • The bond market's 23-basis-point rally from 4.70% to 4.47% in 11 sessions is worth contextualizing against what caused the revolt in the first place. The bond revolt — which pushed UST 10Y from approximately 4.35% to 4.70% over the preceding weeks — was driven by two compounding arguments: (1) AI capital expenditure at hyperscaler scale without demonstrated returns creates deficit-financed government borrowing demand that bond markets were pricing at a risk premium; and (2) oil above $85 contributes to energy-CPI in a way that keeps FOMC hike dissenters hawkish, delaying rate cuts and sustaining higher-for-longer Treasury supply pressure. Both arguments have been materially addressed in 11 sessions: AI capex returns were validated by AWS ▲37% and Azure ▲41% (addressing argument 1), and oil fell to $80 (addressing argument 2). The 23-bp bond rally is proportionate to those two simultaneous improvements. What it is not is certainty about September — it is pricing of improved probability, which a strong Jobs Report Friday can partially reverse.
  • The McDonald's and Caterpillar earnings due today alongside SpaceX and AMD provide the macro-economic texture that pure AI-sector earnings cannot: McDonald's revenue is a consumer spending indicator with global reach (it reports internationally alongside the US business); Caterpillar is the industrial economy's most direct sentiment proxy — its backlog and order commentary tell you whether manufacturing and infrastructure investment is sustaining the capital-cycle expansion or beginning to slow. In a week when the equity records have been driven by AI-and-oil macro factors, McDonald's and Caterpillar will tell you whether the underlying consumer and industrial economies that support those records are still healthy or beginning to diverge from the market's optimistic re-rating.
