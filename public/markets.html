The 10-year Treasury yield breached 5% Monday for the first time since October 2023, driven by Saudi Arabia’s East-West crude pipeline shutdown pushing Brent to $108.42 and FOMC rate-hike odds rising to 86.5% on CME FedWatch — simultaneously creating a physical oil supply shock, an elevated inflation-risk signal, and a rate environment where Treasuries compete directly with equities at current multiples. The S&P 500 fell 0.80% to 7,657, the Dow declined 1.57% to 52,573, Nasdaq lost 0.66% to 26,333, and the Russell 2000 fell 1.21% to 2,927 — the equity selloff compounded by an AI-sector move after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for a slower pace of AI development. Brent crude reached $108.42 as Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline following drone attacks on pumping stations in the Riyadh and Medina regions, putting 4 million barrels per day at risk. WTI rose to $103.20. Gold edged higher to $4,479 on haven demand. FOMC rate-hike probability stands at 86.5%, the highest reading of this cycle, making tomorrow’s 25bp hike near-certain and the statement’s pause-versus-continuation signal the only unpriced variable entering Tuesday’s decision.

Monday’s market session was defined by the convergence of three independent risk factors: oil supply shock, rate pressure, and AI uncertainty. The 10Y at 5% is the session’s most consequential single data point — it is the threshold at which the Treasury earnings-yield comparison to equities shifts from a theoretical argument to a live allocation decision for institutional capital. Tuesday’s FOMC, arriving with Brent at $108 and the 10Y at 5%, is the most consequential monetary policy announcement of this tightening cycle’s second half.

The Anchor · 10Y at 5% — What the Yield Threshold Means for Equities, Rates, and the FOMC Tomorrow

The 10-year Treasury yield crossing 5% on Monday is not just a number — it is the threshold that changes the structural argument for holding equities versus Treasuries at current valuations. At 4.89% on Friday, the 10-year was below the S&P 500’s earnings yield at current multiples by enough that equities could still be justified as offering superior risk-adjusted returns versus the risk-free rate. At 5.00%, that calculation becomes roughly neutral for many institutional models — meaning capital that was previously in equities because of a clear risk-free-rate gap now faces a genuine allocation choice it did not face last week. The 10Y at 5% therefore doesn’t just pressure equities directly through higher discount rates; it changes the marginal allocation decision for institutions that are regularly rebalancing across equities and fixed income. The Saudi pipeline attack is the supply-side event that pushed the yield through 5% rather than leaving it at 4.89%: Brent at $108.42 is a materially higher oil price than the FOMC’s existing inflation assumptions were calibrated to, and a physical supply disruption that could persist until the pipeline is repaired — the Iraqi drone attacks targeted pumping stations, not the pipeline itself, but the timeline for assessment and repair is unknown — means the oil price headwind to inflation is becoming structural rather than transient. At 86.5% hike probability, the FOMC is already expected to hike 25bp tomorrow. The question the statement answers is whether the Saudi pipeline and $108 Brent change the forward guidance from pause to continuation. The data case for continuation has just gotten stronger: the Fed’s dual mandate on inflation is harder to satisfy at $108 Brent than at $107.40 Brent, and the physical supply disruption is not a demand-side event that tighter monetary policy addresses — but the inflation read-through is the same regardless of cause. The Anthropic AI warning contribution to Monday’s decline is real but secondary: it created sector-specific pressure on AI-exposed Nasdaq names that amplified the broader decline but was not the structural driver. The structural drivers are oil and the 10Y, both of which arrived simultaneously and both of which inform tomorrow’s FOMC statement. The equity market enters Tuesday having processed the worst single-session combination of rate pressure and supply shock of this tightening cycle. Whether Tuesday’s statement adds a continuation signal to that combination, or provides a pause signal that allows the CPI-relief rally to resume, determines whether Monday was a single-session shock or the beginning of a structural repricing.

The 10-year Treasury yield crossed 5% Monday — the first breach of that level since October 2023 — driven by the Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown adding an inflation-persistence signal on top of the 86.5% FOMC rate-hike probability that already had Treasuries pricing for tighter money. The yield jumped 11 basis points from Friday’s 4.89%, reaching a level that most institutional fixed-income and equity allocation models treat as the crossover point where risk-free Treasuries are roughly equivalent to the earnings yield on the S&P 500 at current multiples — making the allocation choice between equities and fixed income genuinely competitive in a way it has not been since 2023.

The 5% threshold is meaningful not because of the round number but because of what happens to equity risk-premium models at that yield level. At 4.89%, the equity risk premium — the excess return over Treasuries that justifies equity volatility — was thin but positive for most standard valuation frameworks. At 5.00%, it approaches or crosses zero for the S&P at current multiples, meaning institutional allocators who are regularly rebalancing now face a genuine argument for rotating from equities into Treasuries. That rotation is the mechanical force that took the S&P down 0.80% on Monday. The FOMC decision tomorrow doesn’t resolve the 5% question directly: even a pause signal from the statement would leave the 10-year above 4.89%, because the Saudi pipeline’s oil price impact is supply-driven and not addressed by a Fed pause. The 10Y’s direction after Tuesday depends on whether the statement language adds a continuation signal — which would take it further above 5% — or provides a pause signal that allows some of the rate risk premium to deflate.

Saudi Arabia shut its East-West crude pipeline after drone attacks struck pumping stations in the Riyadh and Medina regions, putting 4 million barrels per day at risk and reducing Yanbu Red Sea port to an estimated 5–7 days of export supply — the pipeline had been the primary alternative for Gulf crude exports when both the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandab are threatened. Brent spiked to an intraday high of $110 before settling at $108.42, up 0.95% from Friday’s $107.40. WTI rose to $103.20, up 1.37%. The drones are believed to have been launched from Iraq; an investigation is under way but no timeline for pipeline repair has been given.

The pipeline’s strategic significance is that it was specifically designed to allow Saudi crude exports to bypass the Strait of Hormuz — connecting oilfields near the Gulf to the Red Sea at Yanbu without passing through either the Hormuz or Bab el-Mandab chokepoints. With Hormuz under Iranian naval pressure, Bab el-Mandab under Houthi pressure from the Mokha foothold, and the East-West pipeline now also shut, all three of Saudi Arabia’s major export pathways face simultaneous constraint — a scenario the oil market’s tail-risk models had treated as near-impossible. Yanbu’s 5–7 day supply buffer provides a brief window before the physical export constraint becomes acute; whether the pipeline is repaired or alternative arrangements are made within that window determines whether $108 is the new floor or the first step in a further leg up.

US equities fell Monday across all major indices as the Saudi pipeline attack drove Brent to $108.42 and the 10-year yield through 5%, combining a physical oil supply shock with the highest pre-FOMC rate-hike probability of this cycle: the S&P 500 shed 0.80% to 7,657, the Dow fell 1.57% to 52,573, Nasdaq declined 0.66% to 26,333, and the Russell 2000 fell 1.21% to 2,927. An AI-sector selloff driven by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei’s call for slower AI development amplified the Nasdaq’s decline, with communication services and technology among the worst-performing sectors. FOMC rate-hike probability on CME FedWatch rose to 86.5%, the highest of this tightening cycle, ahead of Tuesday’s decision.

Monday’s decline erases most of Friday’s CPI-relief rally: the S&P gave back the majority of its 1.07% Friday gain in a single session driven by three independent forces. The structural driver — 10Y at 5% — is the one that persists beyond tomorrow’s FOMC: even if the statement provides a pause signal, the Saudi pipeline’s oil price impact is supply-driven and not addressed by Fed language. The tactical driver — AI selloff on Amodei’s statement — is more likely to reverse once the FOMC provides a cleaner macro backdrop. Tuesday’s decision is the most consequential FOMC since Warsh’s August hiking cycle began: it arrives with the broadest set of simultaneous market risks — oil supply shock, yield threshold, legislative vote, AI uncertainty — that the Fed has had to navigate in a single statement since the 2022 rate-shock period.
