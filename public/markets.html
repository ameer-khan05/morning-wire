The Anchor · Oil and Yields Define September's Open — The Inflation Trade Is Back

September's first trading day is a rate story wearing a geopolitical mask. The surface narrative is Hormuz: a tanker struck near Oman by three projectiles, Day 63 of the crisis, Brent at $91.28. But the mechanism through which that geopolitical event damages equities and lifts yields is inflation. Oil at $91.28 Brent, up nearly $4 from Monday's close, feeds directly into inflation expectations that were already elevated from Monday's Larak Island exchange. The 10-year Treasury yield rising to 4.72% is the market translating oil prices into rate-path expectations: more oil inflation means more total inflation, more total inflation means a September hike is more certain, a more certain September hike means rate-sensitive assets — tech stocks, long-duration equities, crypto — compress. That chain is what produced Tuesday's session. The Nasdaq's 0.90% decline is the rate-sensitive end of the equity market feeling the compression most. The FOMC Blackout creates a specific problem for September: the Fed cannot communicate before September 16, which means there is no mechanism to soften rate expectations even if individual FOMC members privately believe the market is over-pricing a September hike. The only data that can alter the rate path before September 15-16 is August CPI on approximately September 10. That CPI print is five days before the CLARITY vote and six days before the rate decision, placing it at the exact intersection of the two binary events that will define September's market outcome. Hot CPI (above 3.5%) hardens the hike to near-certainty and compresses both CLARITY-premium assets and rate-sensitive equities simultaneously. Moderate CPI (below 3.2%) eases the rate headwind and gives both assets room to recover before the CLARITY vote. The tanker attack itself — three projectiles, outbound transit near Oman — is not a new type of Hormuz incident. It is the continuation of the disruption campaign that has been running since July. What is different about Day 63's tanker attack versus Day 30's is the price level from which it is striking: Brent at $91.28 is already elevated from 62 days of crisis. Each incremental disruption hits from a higher base. The cumulative effect is an oil market that has now priced substantially more supply risk than it priced on July 1. If the direct U.S.-Iran exchange from Monday leads to diplomatic de-escalation, the base could correct sharply; if it leads to sustained direct exchange, the base could move meaningfully higher still.
