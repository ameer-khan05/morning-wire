The conditionality matters for Monday's opening pricing. "Perimeters agreed" is not a signed deal, and "subject to rapidly making a DEAL" places the entire catalyst in a holding pattern. Asian and European markets will trade Sunday night and Monday morning before US markets open, incorporating whatever diplomatic developments occur between now and the US open. If Asian markets open Monday with a deal confirmed, US futures will be materially higher; if the conditional has broken down, futures will track Friday's close. The Monday opening prints will be the first real-time pricing of the Iran deal outcome, GPAI enforcement week 1, and the constructive fundamental backdrop from last week's earnings — all simultaneously.

The commodity and bond market setup entering Monday reflects 33 days of geopolitical risk premium layered onto the fundamental macro backdrop: Brent at $87.84 carries an estimated $10-$20 premium over pre-conflict price levels from the sustained Hormuz disruption; the UST 10Y at 4.58%, while easing 12 basis points from Wednesday's revolt high, remains above the pre-revolt level (approximately 4.42-4.45%) because the oil-inflation argument for elevated rates has not yet resolved; and gold at $4,043.84 holds the $4,000 structural floor established in mid-July, supported by the combination of nominal yield pressure on opportunity cost (10Y at 4.58%) and sustained geopolitical safe-haven demand (33 days of conflict with no resolution). An Iran deal changes all three simultaneously: Brent premium unwinds, bond market eases further on improved September cut probability, and gold's geopolitical safe-haven bid reduces toward its pre-conflict level.

The September rate-cut probability channel is the critical transmission mechanism. The three FOMC hike dissenters' hawkish assessment is explicitly conditioned on inflation trajectory — specifically oil's contribution to energy CPI components. Brent falling from $87.84 toward $70-$75 would reduce the energy-inflation argument materially, improving the data-driven case for a September cut before August CPI even prints. The 2-year yield is the most rate-sensitive point on the curve — it would lead any bond rally, with the 10Y following toward 4.40-4.45% if the full oil-inflation relief materializes. The back end of the curve (30Y) remains less sensitive to the near-term rate-cut probability than to long-term fiscal and inflation expectations, but even a 10-15 basis point 10Y move would be meaningful for equity valuations at current earnings multiples.

Unit 42's documentation that DeepSeek completed autonomous offensive tasks that EU-compliant models refused creates a directly relevant GPAI documentation question: what systemic autonomy risk documentation has DeepSeek's developer submitted? If Brussels treats the Unit 42 report as grounds for a GPAI compliance demand against a non-EU provider in enforcement week 1, it establishes the extraterritorial reach of GPAI enforcement in practice — the enforcement question the entire non-EU AI industry is watching. The AI sector's equity pricing will respond to which path Brussels takes.
