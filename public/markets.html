The Anchor · The PCE-ISM-Jobs Three-Session Sequence — What Q4’s Opening Three Trading Days Say About October’s Macro Backdrop and the Fed’s November Decision

Q4’s first three trading days produced three consecutive macro surprises in opposite directions, each reversing the market’s reading of the previous session. The sequence is the October macro story’s opening chapter. Wednesday: PCE at 3.4% year-over-year — decelerating, below 3.5% consensus — sent 10-year yields from 5.18% to 5.06%, rallied gold, and priced the Fed’s November meeting as a hold. The bond market read PCE’s deceleration as evidence that the rate cycle’s restrictive work was proceeding. Thursday: ISM manufacturing prices paid at 77.9 — 6.8 points above August, highest since May — reversed 18 of those 19 basis points in a single session, sending the 10-year to 5.298%. The bond market read ISM prices as the leading indicator that would put PCE’s deceleration into question in November and December. Friday: Jobs +29,000 against an 84,000 consensus — unemployment rising to 4.2% — reversed the ISM move and more, sending the 10-year to 5.09%, 21 basis points below Thursday’s close. The bond market read the labor miss as the decisive signal for whether the Fed would hike in November: a labor market cooling this sharply, on a manufacturing-inflation backdrop that is ambiguous, makes a November rate hike politically and economically difficult to justify. The three-session sequence does not resolve the inflation question — ISM prices at 77.9 and PCE at 3.4% are still simultaneously true — but it does establish the hierarchy of signals the market is using to price November: labor data outweighs manufacturing cost data when they conflict, because the Fed’s mandate is labor and price stability and a 29,000 jobs print is a price stability data point in the wrong direction for a hike. The equity market’s response — S&P up 1.0%, Nasdaq up 1.7% — is the re-rating that Thursday’s ISM spike had briefly prevented: technology stocks repriced upward on the yield retreat at the rate that Thursday’s surprise had compressed them. The Nasdaq at 27,317 represents the technology sector trading at a multiple that is consistent with 5.09% yields and AI infrastructure earnings quality combined — a baseline that Thursday’s session had temporarily pushed below.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Friday that the U.S. economy added 29,000 nonfarm payroll jobs in September — roughly 55,000 below the economist consensus of 84,000 and the weakest monthly print since early 2025 — and the unemployment rate rose from 4.1% to 4.2%, as prior-month revisions left August’s surprisingly strong headline broadly intact; traders immediately priced the miss as evidence against a Federal Reserve rate hike at the November policy meeting, interpreting a 29,000 print in the context of Thursday’s 5.298% yield environment as the data point that makes November a hold rather than a hike.

The September jobs report’s position in the PCE-ISM-jobs three-session sequence gives it disproportionate market weight: arriving the session after an ISM prices surprise that pushed the 10-year to a Q4 high, the jobs miss provided the ambiguity-resolving signal the market needed. The Fed’s November decision now weighs cooling labor against hot manufacturing input costs, and the bond market’s 21-basis-point yield drop tells you which the market treats as the dominant signal: when labor is weakening, the dual-mandate math makes a hike difficult to justify even if ISM prices are elevated. The November CPI and PCE prints will determine whether the labor miss is validated by further inflation deceleration or contradicted by the ISM prices forecast.

All four major U.S. equity indices closed positive Friday as the September jobs miss drove the yield retreat that re-rated equities across sectors: the S&P 500 surged 1.0% to 7,727, the Nasdaq led with a 1.7% gain to 27,317 as technology stocks repriced on the combination of yield retreat and persistent AI infrastructure earnings quality, the Dow gained 0.6% to 51,211 as rate-sensitive industrials recovered, and the Russell 2000 rose 1.0% to 2,807 as small caps — which bear the full weight of rate-hike expectations — responded to the November-hike probability decline most sharply among the major indices.

The Nasdaq’s 1.7% gain confirms that the technology sector’s AI infrastructure multiple — demonstrated Thursday by the Nasdaq holding positive through a 24-basis-point yield spike — also amplifies to the upside when yields retreat: AI earnings quality creates a higher floor at 5.3% yields and a higher ceiling when yields fall to 5.1%. The week’s net result — S&P from 7,671 at September’s end to 7,727 at October’s first week’s end, through a two-day yield shock and recovery — is Q4 opening net positive on the inflation ambiguity that the ISM-jobs sequence produced.

The 10-year Treasury yield fell 21 basis points to 5.09% Friday — the sharpest single-day yield decline of Q4 — as the September jobs miss prompted traders to aggressively price out Federal Reserve rate-hike expectations for the November meeting, with the yield move reversing the entirety of Thursday’s ISM-driven surge and leaving the 10-year just 3 basis points above Wednesday’s post-PCE level of 5.06%; the two-session yield round trip — from 5.298% at Thursday’s close to 5.09% at Friday’s close — represents the bond market repricing the same November meeting in opposite directions on consecutive days based on conflicting economic signals.

The 10-year at 5.09% entering the weekend reflects the bond market’s resolution of the PCE-ISM ambiguity through the jobs report: labor cooling outweighs manufacturing cost acceleration as a signal for the near-term rate path because the Fed’s November decision requires a labor and inflation case for hiking, and 29,000 jobs undercuts the labor component of that case regardless of what ISM prices said Thursday. Whether 5.09% holds next week depends on October’s CPI (due October 15) and any Fed communication; if CPI confirms the PCE deceleration trend, yields could fall further; if it validates the ISM prices forecast, Thursday’s 5.298% was a preview of Q4’s ceiling.

WTI crude rose 1.8% to $93.15 and Brent gained 1.7% to $99.60 Friday — approaching the $100 threshold again — as improved risk appetite following the jobs miss restored demand-signal confidence that Thursday’s ISM-driven yield spike had compressed; gold advanced 0.9% to $4,199 as the 10-year yield retreat from 5.298% to 5.09% reduced the opportunity cost that had weighed on the metal Thursday, with the two-session yield round trip leaving gold’s weekly close approximately flat and tracking the macro ambiguity it correctly prices.

Oil’s recovery from $91.50 to $93.15 is risk-sentiment driven rather than supply-driven: the Saudi pipeline normalization that has been reducing the supply premium since September continues unchanged, and the Hormuz stalemate that maintains the geopolitical floor — pending Iran’s November 3 nuclear deadline — also continues unchanged. Friday’s oil gain reflects the improvement in demand expectations that a more favorable Fed rate path implies: lower rates support consumer and industrial activity, which supports crude demand more than the supply side has changed. Gold’s weekly flatness across the PCE-ISM-jobs three-session sequence is its honest price for genuine inflation ambiguity: when both an inflation beat and a jobs miss arrive in the same week, gold prices neither data point as definitive and ends the week where it started.
