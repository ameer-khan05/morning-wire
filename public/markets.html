The Anchor · The Week Ahead — Fed October 28, Diesel Deal Oil Variable, and Whether the AI Repricing Has Found Its Floor

Markets are closed Sunday with two open questions that will determine the character of the week ahead: whether the AI repricing that drove the Nasdaq’s 4.5% weekly loss has found its floor, and whether Trump’s diesel deal introduces a supply variable that moderates the Hormuz premium before the Fed meeting. The AI repricing has a floor-testing dynamic that becomes visible at the start of the trading week. The week’s 4.5% Nasdaq loss was driven by a specific catalyst — three simultaneous frontier model releases crystallizing the market’s view that the AI capability moat was narrowing — and this weekend’s open-weight model releases from Meta and DeepSeek provide additional data in the same direction. If Monday’s market opens with the understanding that Muse Spark Open and V4.1-Flash-1017 have further compressed the open-weight frontier, the AI repricing may continue; if the market treats the open-weight releases as a separate segment that does not affect the proprietary API revenue model, Monday may open with a relief bounce. The diesel deal is the energy market’s weekend variable. Brent at $109.86 reflects the probability that the Hormuz interdiction campaign constrains supply; if Trump’s sanctioned Russian diesel reaches global markets as a substitute, the effective supply picture changes and the Hormuz premium partially deflates. That deflation, if it occurs, is a positive for Brent-sensitive stocks and a partial inflation relief that the Fed can incorporate into its October 28 decision calculus. The 10-year Treasury at 5.097% is roughly flat through the AI selloff week, which means the equity repricing is currently operating as a pure valuation correction rather than a combined valuation-and-rates correction. If the Fed confirms Warsh’s pause signal on October 28, the rate uncertainty component of the current risk environment resolves, and the remaining variable is the AI repricing and its relationship to the open-weight model releases that this weekend has accelerated.

The Nasdaq Composite closed Friday at 26,140 down 4.5% for the week — and carries that repricing into Sunday with the additional context of Muse Spark Open and DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017, two open-weight model releases that further compress the frontier capability gap between open and proprietary AI; the S&P 500 at 7,659 (▼1.9% week) and Dow at 52,002 (weekly gain from defensive rotation) set the table for Monday’s open, which will test whether the AI repricing has found a floor or whether the weekend’s open-weight releases provide additional downside for AI-exposed equities.

The Monday open is the week’s first price discovery moment for how the market processes the open-weight model releases alongside last week’s AI bubble selloff. The two interpretations lead to different Monday dynamics: if the market treats Muse Spark Open running on Mac Studios as a direct reduction in the proprietary API revenue opportunity, Monday extends the AI selloff further into large-cap tech names that depend on AI subscription and API revenue; if the market treats the open-weight frontier as a separate category serving different deployment needs, Monday is a relief bounce from oversold conditions. The 10-year Treasury remaining flat through the selloff — at 5.097% — means there is no rates amplification in play; the AI repricing is a pure multiples correction, which historically resolves faster than corrections combined with rate pressure.

Brent crude enters the weekend at $109.86 and WTI at $99.02 — both at Friday’s close — with two competing forces that will determine whether oil moves higher or lower when markets reopen Monday: Iran War Day 231 and the ongoing Hormuz interdiction campaign representing upside pressure, and Trump’s diesel deal to route sanctioned Russian diesel to US and global markets representing a potential supply addition that could partially offset the Hormuz premium if the diesel reaches the market.

The diesel deal’s price impact depends on execution: sanctioned Russian diesel that reaches global markets is a real supply addition that the oil market must price as a Hormuz-premium offset. But sanctions circumvention logistics are complex — shipping, insurance, and financial infrastructure that has been systematically constrained by the sanctions regime does not reconstitute overnight — and the timeline from deal announcement to diesel in the market may be weeks rather than days. If traders price the diesel deal as an immediate supply addition, Monday Brent opens lower; if they price it as a medium-term variable with uncertain execution, the Hormuz premium remains the dominant factor and Monday Brent is roughly flat. Iran’s warning to Hezbollah of an imminent American strike adds an upside tail risk: a US-Iran escalation event would be a sharp positive for oil and a negative for equities simultaneously.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average’s weekly gain against the Nasdaq’s 4.5% loss is the most pronounced growth-to-defensive rotation since September, with industrials, utilities, consumer staples, and healthcare outperforming as institutional investors reduced AI-linked technology exposure and rebalanced toward sectors less sensitive to the AI moat repricing; the Russell 2000 at 2,721 (▼1.7% Friday) reflects the small-cap sector’s dual exposure to both the AI repricing and the 5.097% 10-year yield that makes debt service more expensive for the rate-sensitive smaller companies that dominate that index.

The Dow-Nasdaq divergence serves as the week’s clearest quantitative measure of rotation intensity: a weekly gain in the Dow while the Nasdaq loses 4.5% requires sector flows large enough to move both indices in opposite directions. The rotation is self-reinforcing in the near term — quantitative strategies that maintain target sector weights automatically generate buying in defensives and selling in technology as the relative performance gap widens. The rotation reverses when either the AI repricing finds a floor — reducing the selling pressure in tech — or the defensive sectors reach overvalued levels on their own metrics, which typically takes longer than one week of inflows to achieve at the current scale of the tech-to-defensive capital movement.

The Fed meeting’s October 28 rate announcement is the event that determines whether the current equity repricing can consolidate or must extend. If Warsh delivers a confirmed rate pause — as his earlier comments set up as the expectation — the rate-uncertainty component of the risk-off move resolves, the 10-year stabilizes near 5.1%, and the equity market’s remaining adjustment is limited to the AI valuation repricing, which is a narrower question that individual sector analysts can evaluate and price more precisely than macro uncertainty can be priced. The pause confirmation also removes the gold rally suppressor: Brent at $109.86 carries inflation implications that limit gold’s appeal as a rate-cut beneficiary, but a confirmed pause with a signal of no rate increases through year-end is a mild positive for gold regardless of the oil price level.
