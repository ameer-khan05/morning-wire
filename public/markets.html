The Anchor · The Hormuz Supply Shock — What Monday’s Open Prices That Friday’s Close Did Not

Brent crude closed Friday at $95.54. When Monday’s session opens, it will price two developments that did not exist as of Friday’s close. Development one: Iran’s IRGC shot down a US MQ-1 reconnaissance drone on Sunday. Development two: sea mines sank a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude in the Strait of Hormuz. The IRGC described a total of five tankers hitting mines over 48 hours, two in the southern strait and three in the central and eastern zones. The supertanker reported by Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Navy was described as carrying more than 2 million barrels — a volume representing approximately 2% of daily global crude supply in a single vessel. What those two developments mean for Brent on Monday: the diplomatic ceiling that might have contained oil prices — an active Iran negotiating track — was formally closed by Trump on Saturday. There is no designated US negotiator, no scheduled next round, and no declared timeline for re-engagement. Without a diplomatic ceiling, Brent’s $95.54 Friday close enters Monday as a floor, not a ceiling. The $100-per-barrel level that was a risk scenario last week is now the base case if any new Hormuz or Arabian Peninsula incident occurs before the first Iran negotiating round is rescheduled. The broader market implications: oil at $100 is a CPI and PPI input that makes the December FOMC 25-basis-point hike more certain and raises the probability of additional 2027 hikes. It raises the already-elevated transportation and energy costs embedded in the PPI YoY 6.5% print from Friday. And it hits the Russell 2000’s domestic-oriented small caps — already down 2.6% on Friday — harder than the S&P 500’s large caps, which carry international revenue offsets and energy sector tailwinds.
