The Anchor · The AI Trade Gets Its Fundamental Answer — Meta’s $130-145B Capex Confirmation Closes the Repricing Debate as Oil Retreats on Diplomatic Progress

Wednesday is the session where the AI investment thesis is verified empirically rather than debated theoretically. Meta’s $130-145 billion full-year AI capex guidance is the direct answer to the question that drove two weeks of Nasdaq selling: is the largest enterprise AI infrastructure buyer moderating its commitment? It is not. It is accelerating. The AI repricing narrative required a specific scenario: Meta, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon would eventually have to reduce AI infrastructure spending because AI revenue monetization was lagging the infrastructure investment. That argument rested on the assumption that the hyperscalers were investing ahead of demand. Meta’s Q3 earnings answer the demand question directly: $63.34 billion in revenue, up 23% year-over-year, with AI advertising optimization, AI agents, and AI-driven engagement each contributing to the growth. The $130-145 billion capex guidance is not speculative — it is Meta’s management confirming that the revenue growth justifies the infrastructure investment. Muse reaching 10 million downloads in 40 days, versus ChatGPT’s 123 and Claude’s 673, provides the consumer monetization evidence that the infrastructure is producing products with measurable velocity. Oil’s 1.8% retreat amplifies the earnings signal: Witkoff’s St. Petersburg meeting introduces diplomatic movement into the week’s macro environment, reducing the energy inflation pressure that had been complicating the Fed’s October 28 pause calculus since Tuesday. Wednesday’s configuration — AI capex confirmed, oil retreating, diplomatic engagement active — is the constructive setup that the past two weeks of trading had not been able to achieve simultaneously. The remaining uncertainty is the Fed’s October 28 decision, which must now absorb Brent at $108.79 rather than $110.67. That is an improvement, not a resolution — but it is enough to close the week with the AI trade’s foundation intact rather than undermined.

The market implications of Meta’s guidance ripple outward from Meta’s own stock: Nvidia’s GPU demand, data center operators’ revenue visibility, power infrastructure companies’ project pipelines, and the hyperscaler peers who will report their own capex in the coming weeks are all recalibrated by Meta’s confirmation. The AI repricing narrative argued that hyperscaler capex would moderate once investors pressured management to justify the investment-to-revenue gap. Meta’s response is to report 23% revenue growth and increase capex guidance simultaneously — the gap it is being asked to justify is closing, not widening. Thursday will open with the AI hardware supply chain at meaningfully improved valuations from Wednesday’s close.

WTI crude fell 1.8% to $101.79 and Brent declined to $108.79 Wednesday as oil markets partially reversed Tuesday’s five-month highs after Trump envoy Witkoff held a three-hour meeting with Putin in St. Petersburg presenting updated Ukraine peace proposals and securing the release of American prisoner Hubbard as a goodwill gesture — the first significant diplomatic engagement between the US and Russia on Ukraine since the Shenzhen summit proposal — with Brent’s decline from $110.67 to $108.79 reducing the energy inflation pressure on the Federal Reserve’s October 28 pause probability, though $108.79 remains elevated and the full geopolitical risk premium has not been removed.

Oil’s 1.8% WTI decline is the market’s assessment of Witkoff’s engagement against Tuesday’s adversarial baseline: Tuesday priced Iran’s deliberate delay strategy and Iran-Russia alignment; Wednesday prices the return of US-Russia diplomatic contact. The two are not symmetrically offsetting — Iran’s strategy is more directly connected to Hormuz oil supply than US-Russia Ukraine talks — but Witkoff’s St. Petersburg meeting and the Hubbard release signal that diplomatic channels remain active, which reduces the tail risk of a further geopolitical escalation premium. Brent at $108.79 into the Fed’s October 28 decision is a better inflation environment than Brent at $110.67, and the direction of movement matters as much as the level for the Fed’s communications.

The Dow rose 1% to 52,495, the Nasdaq gained 0.6% to 26,425, and the S&P 500 advanced 0.3% to 7,713 in the market’s first broadly positive close in two weeks, with all four major indices ending higher as oil’s retreat and Meta’s post-close earnings beat simultaneously resolved the two macro headwinds that had been driving the correction; gold edged down 0.3% to $4,130 — declining in a positive session consistent with risk-on behavior — and the 10-year Treasury held at 5.12%, confirming that the rate-uncertainty component of the correction remains stable with the Fed’s October 28 decision now seven days away and operating with a slightly improved inflation outlook from oil’s retreat.

Gold’s decline on a positive day maintains the pattern of the past two weeks — gold moving with the risk environment rather than against it, which confirms that the current correction has been sector-specific and macro-driven rather than systemically threatening. A systemic-risk scenario would produce rising gold alongside falling equities; the persistent gold decline on equity-down days has been the market’s signal all week that this is an oil-and-AI-repricing correction, not a credit event or a policy panic. Wednesday’s configuration — gold down on a positive-equity day, 10-year stable — is structurally identical to the prior pattern but in the constructive direction: it confirms that the recovery is broad-based and driven by the same fundamental factors that drove the correction, now moving in reverse.
