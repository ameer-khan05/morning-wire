The Anchor · Fed Hikes to 3.75%–4.00% — The Statement Language That Moved Markets

The Federal Reserve’s unanimous 25-basis-point hike to 3.75%–4.00% was the most fully priced unannounced FOMC decision in recent history — 92% on CME FedWatch entering Wednesday’s meeting. What the market was actually waiting for was the statement language: would the Fed signal pause potential or confirm continuation? Warsh’s post-decision framing — resolute on inflation but not panicked — landed closer to the pause-possible end of the spectrum than the most hawkish pre-meeting scenarios had implied. The 10-year yield pulling back to 4.998% from Tuesday’s 5.02% cycle high is the market’s translation of that statement language: not a reversal of the tightening path, but a signal that the next hike is not yet a foregone conclusion. At 16 of 18 Fed officials projecting at least one more increase in 2026 and futures markets pricing the funds rate at approximately 4.1% by December and 4.5% by September 2027, the direction of travel is clear — but the pace is now a legitimate debate rather than a settled question. The Generac +33% move is the session’s most specific signal: Amazon receiving warrants to purchase up to $340 million in Generac shares under a data-center power supply agreement is a direct proxy on AI infrastructure spending continuing at pace despite the rate environment. If AI-driven data center buildout is robust enough to lock in multi-year generator supply agreements at $340 million warrant scale, the inference demand thesis — that AI compute spending continues even if frontier model training slows — is visible in corporate deal structure rather than just in analyst projections. Generac +33% is not a macro story; it is a microstructure signal about where real money is being committed in the AI infrastructure economy. The oil market easing — WTI down 0.32% to $102.10, Brent down 0.20% to $105.62 — reflects post-FOMC dollar strength and some de-escalation of acute supply panic, but the Yanbu refinery strike from Wednesday is not resolved, and Saudi Arabia’s air-defense challenge means the structural supply risk has not diminished even as the spot price has modestly eased.

The Federal Reserve unanimously raised the federal funds rate 25 basis points to a target range of 3.75%–4.00% Wednesday, the first rate hike since July 2023, with Chair Kevin Warsh stating that “the plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too long.” Sixteen of 18 officials project at least one additional increase in 2026, with futures markets now pricing the funds rate at approximately 4.1% by December and 4.5% by September 2027. The decision was driven by headline PCE inflation at 3.7% and core at 3.4%, persistently elevated by energy price shocks from the Middle East conflict, import tariffs, and AI-driven capital spending.

The market’s Thursday recovery — S&P up 0.59%, Dow up 0.20% — reflects Warsh’s measured statement framing rather than surprise at the decision itself: the 25bp hike was 92% priced entering Wednesday, and the relief is that the guidance was not more hawkish than expected. The key phrase the bond market was listening for was the continuation signal: “additional increases may be warranted” suggests continuation; “meeting by meeting” suggests a pause is possible. Warsh’s “resolve” framing without explicit forward guidance gave the market enough ambiguity to interpret Thursday as a pause-possible setup, explaining the 10Y easing below 5% after Tuesday’s 5.02% cycle high.

Generac surged 33% in extended trading Thursday after Amazon received warrants to purchase up to $340 million worth of Generac shares under an agreement for Generac to supply backup power generators to Amazon’s data centers — a corporate deal that functions as a direct proxy on AI-driven data center buildout continuing at pace regardless of the rate environment. The deal locks in multi-year generator supply at warrant-scale commitment, reflecting Amazon’s view that data center power security is a strategic infrastructure priority for the duration of the AI compute buildout.

The Generac deal is notable because it structures the AI infrastructure thesis in a durable form: warrants to purchase $340 million in shares are a long-duration commitment that ties Amazon’s balance sheet to Generac’s performance and creates an incentive for Amazon to ensure Generac’s operational success as a supplier. For markets trying to read the AI capex trajectory through Fed rate pressure, the deal says: the companies actually making the data center investment decisions are not slowing down on power infrastructure commitments because of a 25-basis-point rate hike. That inference — that AI capex is rate-insensitive at the current level — is what the +33% move is pricing.

The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.998% Thursday — its first close below 5.00% since Monday — as the Federal Reserve’s measured post-hike statement gave the bond market enough ambiguity to dial back from Tuesday’s 5.02% cycle high. WTI crude eased 0.32% to $102.10 and Brent fell 0.20% to $105.62 as post-FOMC dollar strength weighed on commodity prices; Gold declined 1.43% to $4,325 as haven demand eased with the equity market’s Thursday recovery.

The 10Y settling just below 5.00% — at 4.998% — is a symbolically important level: it breaks the “5% Treasury” narrative that has dominated markets since Monday’s historic breach, even if the underlying rate path has not meaningfully changed. The oil modest easing reflects dollar strength rather than supply resolution: the Yanbu refinery strike from Wednesday is not remediated, Saudi Arabia’s air-defense capacity is under stress, and the structural supply risk from the Middle East conflict remains intact. The $102-$105 range for WTI and Brent is not a resolved price; it is a waiting price. Gold’s 1.43% decline reflects the same dynamic as the 10Y easing: when equity markets recover and the acute macro panic of Tuesday dissipates, haven assets return capital to risk assets.
