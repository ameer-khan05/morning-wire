The Anchor · Monday's Chip Selloff Is Pre-Earnings Repositioning — Wednesday's Nvidia Result and PCE Data Will Determine Whether Equities Enter Jackson Hole Week Constructively or Under Dual Pressure

The chip stock selloff on Monday — Micron -5.8%, AMD -3%+, Broadcom -2%+ pulling the Nasdaq to -0.76% while the Dow held positive — is best read as pre-earnings repositioning rather than a sector thesis change. Investors who hold broad semiconductor exposure routinely reduce it ahead of a binary Nvidia earnings event to limit correlation risk from a large single-stock move in either direction. The selloff tells you the market views Wednesday's result as consequential enough to hedge, not that it expects a negative outcome. The consequence of that read is that a Nvidia beat-and-raise Wednesday recovers Monday's semiconductor losses on Thursday and sets up Jackson Hole week with the AI-infrastructure cycle validated; a miss or cautious guidance from Nvidia compounds Monday's losses and sends equities into Jackson Hole with the AI capex thesis under pressure simultaneously with the Iran sanctions backdrop. The Russell 2000's break below 3,000 — closing at 2,997.07 — is the market's most direct signal of concern about the Canada tariff inflation channel. Small-cap US companies have higher domestic revenue exposure than large caps and more direct supply-chain exposure to Canadian inputs in manufacturing, building materials, and agricultural processing. The 50% tariff on Canadian goods hits small-cap companies with less ability to diversify their supply chains than S&P 500 mega-caps with global procurement leverage. The Russell's underperformance relative to the Dow (which was ▲0.26% when the Russell was ▼0.69%) reflects exactly this: the large-cap companies that are raising prices, finding alternative suppliers, and absorbing tariff costs across global margins held their value; the small-cap companies with thinner margins and fewer supply-chain alternatives sold off. The Russell's level entering Wednesday's PCE data release matters: if July PCE comes in above consensus, the rate-cut rationale weakens precisely for the floating-rate small-cap companies that have the most to gain from September's anticipated 25bps cut. A hot PCE paired with a weaker Nvidia result would be the week's worst-case scenario for the Russell 2000 specifically, and Warsh's task at Jackson Hole Friday would be to restore confidence in the rate-cut path without contradicting the PCE data that markets have just processed.

Oil markets absorbed the Operation Economic Outcast implementation without a new price shock: WTI held near $86.40 and Brent fell slightly to $92.20 (▼0.54%), consistent with markets treating the sanctions as an implementation of the previously announced escalation rather than a new incremental supply shock. The Hormuz disruption premium — approximately $10-11 per barrel above pre-crisis levels — remains intact in the $92.20 Brent price; the open question is whether Iran's confirmed zero oil exports change the market's forward pricing of disruption risk or whether it is already fully reflected. Gold at $4,673.40 shows modest softening from Friday's $4,680.60 close, as investors balance safe-haven demand from the Iran and Canada trade situations against the 10-year yield at 4.72%, which provides competition for non-yielding gold allocations.

Wednesday is the week's data-convergence day: Q2 GDP revision and July core PCE arrive in the morning session, durable goods orders follow, and Nvidia reports fiscal Q2 earnings after the close — all before the Jackson Hole symposium opens Thursday. July core PCE is the most rate-sensitive individual release: as the Fed's preferred inflation measure, a reading at or above 2.4% year-over-year would complicate Warsh's September rate-cut rationale at Friday's keynote, while a reading at or below 2.3% clears the inflation-data condition for the September 16 cut even amid Canada tariff and Iran sanctions pressures. The sequencing is significant: Warsh speaks Friday morning with Wednesday's Nvidia result, PCE print, and GDP revision already processed by markets — making the macro environment he walks into the product of Wednesday's data outcomes.

The FOMC blackout begins Saturday August 30 immediately after Warsh's keynote, meaning Friday's address is the last Federal Reserve communication before the September 16 FOMC decision. Whatever Warsh says Friday morning is what the market has to work with for 18 days — through the CLARITY Act cloture vote on September 15 and the FOMC meeting itself on September 16. The dual mandate of Friday's address — signal the September rate path and frame the Fed's posture on digital finance for CLARITY's political conditions — makes it the most consequential single event in the remaining 21-day calendar. The optimal scenario for equities is: Nvidia beats Wednesday, PCE cools Wednesday, Warsh signals September cut and engages digital finance constructively Friday. Any deviation from that sequence creates at least partial headwinds for equity markets entering September.

Oil markets absorbed Monday's "Operation Economic Outcast" implementation without a new price shock: Brent crude fell slightly to $92.20 (▼0.54% from Friday's $92.70 close) and WTI held near $86.40, as the market read the formal sanctions implementation as an execution of the previously announced "economic D-Day" escalation rather than a new incremental supply shock. Iran's central bank governor confirming Tuesday that oil exports have fallen to zero has not yet produced the additional Brent spike that a truly unexpected supply event would generate — which tells you the current $92.20 level already reflects the market's estimate of disruption risk from zero Iranian oil exports at the operational status of Hormuz Day 56. Gold softened modestly to $4,673.40 from Friday's $4,680.60, as the 10-year Treasury yield's position at 4.72% creates competition for non-yielding gold allocations even as geopolitical safe-haven demand persists.

The 10-year yield at 4.72% reflects the dual inflationary pressure of the Canada tariff and the Iran export zero simultaneously active. Both are inflationary channels: Canada's 50% tariff on crude oil (approximately 60% of US crude imports) adds a direct cost to the energy input that flows through the US economy, while Iranian zero exports tighten global oil supply in ways that sustain Brent above $90. The question Wednesday's PCE reading will begin to answer is whether the inflation pipeline from these two sources is already showing up in July PCE data or whether it arrives in the August and September readings that the FOMC won't see until after the September 16 decision. If July PCE is clean (at or below 2.3%), the September rate-cut case survives intact. If July PCE is hotter than expected, Warsh must defend a September cut against both the July inflation data and the forward inflationary pipeline from tariffs and sanctions that will arrive in subsequent months.

Quick hits

  • Dell reports earnings this week alongside Nvidia, and the Dell result matters for the AI-infrastructure trade for a different reason than Nvidia's. Dell is a major server distributor for AI compute infrastructure that does not manufacture AI accelerators directly; a strong Dell result paired with a strong Nvidia result confirms that AI-infrastructure demand is flowing through the full hardware stack, not just concentrating in Nvidia's silicon layer. A weak Dell result in the same week Nvidia beats would suggest that Nvidia's accelerator sales are going to hyperscalers but not yet generating the downstream server, storage, and networking build-out that a broadly distributed AI-infrastructure cycle would produce. The Dell-Nvidia result pair is the most diagnostic data point of the week for distinguishing between concentrated AI-chip demand and distributed AI-infrastructure deployment demand.
  • The Brent crude market's muted response to Iran's confirmed zero oil exports (▼0.54% on the day, not up) reveals the current pricing architecture for the Hormuz crisis: the market has concluded that zero Iranian oil exports via the Hormuz route is already reflected in $92.20 Brent, and that a new incremental escalation — a vessel seizure, a coalition military response, a sanctions-related maritime incident — is what would push Brent materially above current levels. The Hormuz disruption is now priced as a sustained-disruption steady state, not an uncertain-duration event. Changing that pricing requires a new specific escalation above the zero-export status quo, not a confirmation of it. Iran's defiant vow to "neutralize the economic war" is rhetoric; an IRGC vessel seizure of a coalition-flag tanker would be the event that reprices Brent above $95.
