The Anchor · Monday’s Dip-Buy Retested — Oil Back at Five-Month Highs as Iran-Russia Deepen, Making Wednesday’s Meta Earnings the Week’s Last Constructive Macro Lever

Tuesday’s reversal of Monday’s AI dip-buy is driven by one revelation: Iran was never on a path toward a Hormuz deal. Monday priced the proposal as a genuine diplomatic opening; Tuesday prices the admission. The oil market’s Tuesday reaction is the most efficient summary of what changed: Brent went from $107.97 on Monday — after the Hormuz proposal was announced — to $110.67 on Tuesday after Iranian officials told Reuters that Tehran had been deliberately prolonging talks to weaken Trump ahead of midterms. The proposal was a tactic; the delay was the strategy. Brent at $110.67 is above Monday’s $109.86 pre-proposal high, which means Tuesday did not merely erase Monday’s relief — it added a cynicism premium. Markets now price a lower probability of Hormuz relief before the Fed’s October 28 meeting than they did before Monday’s proposal was announced. The implication for equities is that the AI dip-buy thesis — which Monday’s session was beginning to execute — now requires more work from earnings than from macro tailwinds. Wednesday’s Meta earnings are the only near-term lever capable of improving the macro environment for AI stocks: if Meta confirms aggressive AI capex, the AI-repricing narrative collapses on fundamental grounds — the most sophisticated AI buyer in public equity is doubling down, which the market cannot ignore. If Meta moderates, both the AI-repricing narrative and the oil-inflation macro headwind are simultaneously active going into October 28, and the dip-buy has no fundamental support. Gold’s 0.7% decline on Tuesday — in a session with elevated geopolitical risk and equity weakness — is the market’s internal consistency check: gold should be rising if investors are pricing systemic risk, but it is declining because the current stress is sector-specific and oil-driven rather than systemically threatening. The 10-year at 5.12% is similarly stable — which means the rate-uncertainty component of the current correction has not escalated, and Wednesday’s earnings can determine whether the AI-repricing component resolves.

The September CPI energy shock can still be characterized as transitory if Hormuz relief is genuinely approaching; it cannot be characterized as transitory if the party that controls the Strait has explicitly told Reuters it is deliberately maintaining the pressure for political reasons. That distinction does not change the Fed’s October 28 decision probability dramatically — Fed decisions are data-driven, not headline-driven — but it changes the equity market’s prior probability on the pause, and Tuesday’s 0.5% Nasdaq decline reflects that revised prior.
