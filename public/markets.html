Brent crude fell 3.9% to $96.78 Friday — its largest single-session decline since Thursday's $100.69 crossing — as the geopolitical premium that had driven oil prices to a two-month high partially unwound on reports of a 10-day ceasefire proposal circulating between mediators and both sides in the Iran-US conflict. WTI crude fell 4.21% to $88.30. The oil retreat helped the Dow recover 0.45% to 51,947.25 as blue-chip stocks rebounded from Thursday's sell-off; the S&P 500 closed essentially flat at 7,411.98. The Nasdaq slipped an additional 0.64% to 24,975.82, extending Thursday's losses on chip stock weakness. All major US indexes posted weekly losses — the Nasdaq down 2.0%, its worst five-session decline since March 2026.

The week's market structure is visible in the Friday cross-section: Dow up, Nasdaq down. The Dow's blue-chip recovery reflects the oil-retreat mechanism — Brent below $97 eases inflation pass-through expectations, reduces September hike probability, and benefits rate-sensitive industrials and financials that dominate the Dow. The Nasdaq's continued decline reflects chip-stock weakness that is not oil-driven: the tech sector's capex-FCF debate from Wednesday-Thursday earnings remains unresolved by Friday's oil move, and chip stocks — the indirect beneficiaries of AI capex — are pricing the earnings signals independently of the oil retreat. The split between Dow recovery and Nasdaq continued weakness is the market pricing two different problems with two different dynamics.

The UST 10-year yield eased 4 basis points to 4.68% Friday as Brent's retreat from $100 modestly reduced the inflation pass-through signal that had pushed September Federal Reserve rate hike odds to 80% Thursday. Gold rose 0.55% to $4,050.20, recovering slightly from Thursday's $4,028 close as the inflation-premium bid held even as some geopolitical-premium demand faded. The July 29 FOMC meeting remains near-certain to hold; the September 16 meeting is the live decision point. Powell's language at the July 29 press conference — specifically whether he incorporates $100+ Brent into the FOMC's inflation baseline or describes it as a transitory supply shock to assess — is the next major rates signal before the July CPI print due in mid-August.

The distinction between "incorporated into baseline" and "transitory supply shock to assess" is not semantic — it determines whether the market prices September as 70%+ or 40-50%. If Powell says the FOMC has updated its inflation outlook to reflect sustained energy prices at $100+, the July CPI merely confirms what the FOMC has already priced, and September hike is near-certain. If Powell says the FOMC is monitoring the oil-CPI pass-through and will wait for July data, September probability compresses to the range where a sub-consensus CPI print could push September to a hold. Gold's 0.55% recovery on Friday — holding above $4,000 even as oil retreated — suggests the inflation premium is not reversing with the oil retreat, only the geopolitical excess above it. That is the correct interpretation: $96.78 Brent is still inflationary; it just crossed back below the psychological threshold that activates the strongest inflation-pass-through signal.
