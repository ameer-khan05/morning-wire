The Treasuries rally to 4.99% is the week’s most honest signal — investors are not rotating to gold or crypto for safety; they are rotating to bonds.

The Anchor · Correction Confirmed — What the S&P 500 and Dow’s Entry Into Technical Correction Territory Tells You About the AI Trade’s Week

The S&P 500 and Dow are both down more than 10% from their record highs as of Friday’s close. That is the technical definition of a market correction. The cause is not ambiguous: the AI trade that drove those highs is now being interrogated, and the interrogation started the same day the AI trade held its record IPO. Thursday’s Anthropic debut was the AI trade’s highest-water mark in a single session. ANT opened at $111.50, the S&P and Nasdaq rose on AI enthusiasm, and the market’s message was: the AI software premium is durable. Friday’s session is the correction of that enthusiasm. ANT fell 9%. The Nasdaq fell 2.25%. The S&P and Dow confirmed correction territory. The underlying reason is not rates — the 10-year yield fell to 4.99% as investors rotated to bonds, which would normally be supportive of equity valuations. The reason is valuation interrogation. The AI trade has been pricing increasingly large multiples on increasingly uncertain future revenues. ANT’s $1.5 trillion IPO valuation made that uncertainty explicit in a single price. Daily trading is now marking that price against the question it represents: at $1.5 trillion, what does Anthropic’s revenue trajectory need to look like, and when does it need to get there? Friday’s answer — $87.90 — says the market is revising that estimate. The oil story is the correction’s counterpoint. WTI and Brent rising on geopolitical risk — Kyiv bridge strikes, Mossad’s Iran push — are the market pricing the events the Shenzhen peace declaration explicitly excluded. Thursday’s great-power coordination said: oil supply will increase. Friday’s geopolitical reality said: the conflicts that threaten that supply are unresolved.

That problem is ANT at $1.5 trillion before its revenue trajectory is proven. It will not resolve with a yield drop. It will resolve with revenue data.

The 10-year Treasury yield fell 9 basis points to 4.99% on Friday — retreating from Thursday’s 5.08% and moving back below the psychologically significant 5% level — as investors rotated out of equities and into US Treasuries in the week’s most pronounced flight-to-safety move; Advisor Perspectives reported that “Treasuries add to steep weekly gain as investors flee stocks,” and CNBC’s bond desk noted yields were “sharply lower as investors flee to safe havens”; the rotation is distinct from last week’s rate movements, which were driven by macro data (the Philly Fed miss) and Federal Reserve communication (Governor Waller’s hawkish warning) — Friday’s yield drop is equity-driven, reflecting demand for the safety of Treasuries as the AI trade sells off, rather than any softening in the Fed’s rate outlook; Chicago Fed’s Goolsbee confirmed Friday that a substantial December rate hike cannot be ruled out.

A 10-year yield below 5% driven by equity flight-to-safety is a structurally different signal than a yield below 5% driven by softer economic data. It says the bond market is responding to stock market fear rather than to changing expectations about Fed policy. That distinction matters for what comes next: if the equity selloff stabilizes and the flight-to-safety rotation reverses, yields could quickly return above 5% without any change in the fundamental rate outlook. The rate environment is not driving the correction; the correction is temporarily driving the rate environment.

The peace framework and the conflicts it excluded are now trading in opposite directions in the same commodity market.
