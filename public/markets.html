The Anchor · Monday Opens the 16-Day FOMC Blackout Trading Window

Friday's close establishes the market baseline for the FOMC blackout period: S&P at 7,715.47, Nasdaq at 26,411.07, UST 10Y at approximately 4.84%, and September rate-hike odds at 55.7%. That baseline reflects Thursday's 3.7% PCE print and Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole keynote — but was set before the weekend's new information: Qatar's PM in Tehran for Hormuz de-escalation talks, another tanker attacked in the Strait, BTC recovering mildly, and Roman's successful launch. Monday's equity and commodity open will be the first opportunity to price all of that in the FOMC blackout environment where no Fed guidance is available. The commodity pricing question for Monday is the most immediate. Brent at $87.87 prices Hormuz as a supply restriction — Iranian conditions unchanged, US not talking, mine-clearing not begun, throughput at roughly 5 vessels per day versus the pre-crisis average of 15. Qatar's PM in Tehran and the Iran-Oman managed-passage negotiations represent the first substantive diplomatic engagement since the crisis began, potentially signaling a path toward partial resolution. But another tanker was attacked during the same period — Iran's operational posture has not changed. Brent must price both signals simultaneously: a diplomatic signal that suggests some off-ramp may be emerging and an operational signal that Iran is still targeting vessels. The tension between those two pieces of information will drive Monday's commodity move. The rate path for the 16-day blackout window is determined by one data point: August CPI, due approximately September 10. If CPI is hot, September rate-hike odds approach certainty. If CPI moderates, the CLARITY narrative re-dominates without competing macro headwinds. August CPI is the only confirmed piece of information that can alter the rate path before the September 15 CLARITY vote and September 16 rate decision resolve.

The 10-year Treasury yield closed Friday at approximately 4.84%, representing an 18-basis-point move from Wednesday's 4.66% — driven by Thursday's July PCE print of 3.7% year-over-year followed by Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole keynote. CME FedWatch September rate-hike probability stands at 55.7%, up sharply from approximately 35% before Thursday's data. The FOMC Blackout Day 2 means the Federal Reserve cannot clarify, revise, or update those expectations through any public communication before September 16. The market must form its rate path view through incoming data alone — and there is only one confirmed piece of incoming data before September 15: August CPI, due approximately September 10.

August CPI is the entire rate narrative for the 16-day FOMC blackout window. If it comes in hot (above 3.5%), the September rate-hike probability approaches certainty and markets must price a simultaneous rate hike and CLARITY vote in the same week — a compounding stress scenario. If CPI moderates below 3.2%, the September rate-hike odds fall back toward 30% on the data alone, even without Fed guidance, and the CLARITY narrative re-dominates without macro headwinds. The current 55.7% odds split the difference; August CPI will resolve that split five days before the CLARITY cloture vote and six days before the rate decision.

WTI crude closed Friday at $82.82 (▼0.86%), Brent at approximately $87.87, and gold at approximately $4,595 (▼0.71%). The gold decline reflects Warsh's hawkish keynote pushing yields higher and reducing the opportunity cost argument for non-yielding assets. Brent at $87.87 incorporates Hormuz as an unresolved supply restriction at Day 61. Two new Sunday developments will reach Monday's commodity open: Qatar's Prime Minister is in Tehran for de-escalation talks — the most significant diplomatic engagement of the crisis — and another tanker was attacked in the Strait on the same day. These two signals run in opposite directions for commodity risk pricing.

Iran and Oman are also separately negotiating a managed-passage framework that would allow commercial vessels to transit the Strait safely in exchange for maritime service fees paid to a new jointly administered corporation. The framework would represent a partial operational resolution — not the full diplomatic settlement Iran demands — and is being negotiated without US involvement. If the Iran-Oman framework progresses, Brent could reprice lower; if Qatar's PM returns from Tehran without progress and attacks continue, the risk premium moves higher. Monday's first print will reveal which signal markets are weighting more heavily.
