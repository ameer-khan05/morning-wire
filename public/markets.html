Brent crude held at $88.77 (+0.28%) and WTI at $82.54 (+0.17%) — elevated but below Monday's surge, reflecting the market's processing of a situation in which the Hormuz closure is now open-ended without a ceasefire or negotiating framework to time against. The 10-year Treasury yield moved to 4.68%, and FOMC July meeting minutes arrive Wednesday as the week's primary macro calendar event.

Quick hits

  • The VIX's 3.83% rise deserves a precise note on what it is measuring. The VIX measures the 30-day implied volatility of S&P 500 options — it is the market's aggregate estimate of how much the S&P will move in the next month, expressed as an annualized standard deviation. A 3.83% rise in VIX on a day when the S&P itself falls only 0.18% means that options traders expect more volatility ahead than the current day's price move implies — they are pricing in the possibility of a larger move in either direction over the next 30 days. In the context of the Iran situation, the VIX rise reflects options market participants buying downside protection against a scenario in which the geopolitical deterioration accelerates — either a military event that spikes oil toward $95 or an escalation that produces a sharper equity selloff than Tuesday's orderly 0.18% decline. VIX at elevated levels is a leading indicator of potential volatility, not a forecast of direction.
  • The simultaneous rise of gold, Treasury yields, and Bitcoin on Tuesday is a market structure anomaly worth noting. In a standard risk-off session, gold rises (safe haven), Treasury yields fall (bond safety bid), and Bitcoin falls (risk asset). Tuesday broke that pattern: gold rose, yields rose, and Bitcoin rose — three usually uncorrelated safe-haven and risk signals all going the same direction. The explanation differs for each: gold rose on geopolitical uncertainty (the standard safe-haven bid), yields rose on inflation expectations from elevated oil (the stagflation-risk signal), and Bitcoin rose on the CLARITY policy catalyst (the regulatory signal). Three different assets, three different stories, all positive simultaneously — which is unusual enough to suggest that the standard macro correlations are breaking down in an environment where geopolitical, inflation, and regulatory policy signals are all moving independently on the same day.
