Black Friday 2026 was not supposed to be a consequential trading session — abbreviated to 1pm ET, thin volume, holiday weekend. The Anchor · The Black Friday Compression — How a Half-Day Session Became the Week’s Most Important Close, and What the Week’s 6.7% Nasdaq Gain Actually Tells Us

A 6.7% weekly gain for the Nasdaq on a holiday week with one day closed and one half-day is not a typical weekly gain. It is a compressed repricing event. The week started with the Nasdaq near 22,789 — down 3.98% on Wednesday on PCE inflation data and semiconductor tariff proposals. It ends at 24,130. That is a 1,341-point move in four and a half sessions. The catalyst for the recovery was not a change in PCE data, which remains above the Fed’s 2% target. It was not a withdrawal of the tariff proposals. It was a combination of AI-sector sentiment recovery — driven specifically by Friday’s Riemann hypothesis verification, which sent Anthropic shares back above their IPO price and signaled that frontier AI reasoning has reached a category of capability the market had not yet priced — and a holiday-week technical dynamic in which thin volume amplifies buy-side order flow into larger price moves than the same volume would produce in a normal session. The Riemann proof is not a revenue event for Anthropic. It is a capability signal. The market priced that signal as a multiple-expansion catalyst: if AI can close a 167-year mathematical problem, the addressable market for AI-assisted professional work is broader than previously modeled. That repricing happened in a half-day session on the Friday after Thanksgiving. It is now embedded in the closing levels that Monday will open from.

US equities closed sharply higher in Friday’s half-day Black Friday session, with all four major indexes posting gains: S&P 500 at 7,297 (▲1.60%), Nasdaq at 24,130 (▲2.30%), Dow at 50,775 (▲1.51%), and Russell 2000 at 2,750 (▲1.71%), with The Company Chronicle and Yahoo Finance reporting chipmakers and AI stocks leading the advance as Anthropic’s stock recovered above its $83 IPO price and the Riemann hypothesis verification provided a broad AI-capability signal; the weekly performance was exceptional by recent standards: Nasdaq up 6.7% for the week, S&P up 5.6%, Dow up 5.5% — the best weekly performance for the major indexes since April 2025, achieved in only three and a half trading sessions due to Thursday’s Thanksgiving closure and Friday’s 1pm ET early close; and the recovery represents a full reversal of the week’s losses for most major indexes, though November as a whole remains negative on the cumulative PCE-inflation and tariff-driven sell-off that began earlier in the month.

The Nasdaq’s 6.7% weekly gain on only three and a half sessions is statistically unusual. Volume-normalized, it is roughly equivalent to a 10%+ weekly move in a full five-session week — a move that would have been characterized as extreme volatility rather than a standard recovery. The compression into holiday-week thin volume amplified the gains exactly as it amplified the losses earlier in November. Volatility is symmetric: the same dynamics that made the Nasdaq’s November decline look worse also made the recovery look better than the underlying demand shift would suggest.

Brent above $96 on a day when equities are rallying is an unusual market condition that reflects two simultaneous narratives: the AI-sector recovery is equity-positive, while the energy-infrastructure campaign is commodity-positive. These two signals are not causally related, but they are compressing into the same session and creating a cross-asset read that is more complex than either signal alone. Rising equity and rising oil is typically an early-cycle expansion signal; in this case it is a supply-disruption premium atop a tech-recovery rally.

India is reportedly close to an agreement with the US Trade Representative that would reduce India’s reciprocal tariff rate from 25% to 17%, making it the first potential resolution in the Thursday Thanksgiving-day announcement that established rates on 14 trade partners — with China facing additional duties reaching 45% on some product categories — a development that, if confirmed, would signal the administration is using the tariff schedule as a negotiating instrument rather than a fixed policy outcome and that willing bilateral partners can secure lower rates through agreement; the India tariff negotiation is structurally significant because India is both a large economy and a potential beneficiary of manufacturing diversification away from China — a lower tariff rate for India would accelerate the supply-chain shift that US policy has been trying to encourage; and the 14-partner tariff framework as a whole will generate a full market-reaction event at Monday’s regular full session, the first opportunity to price the Thursday announcement without the volume constraints of Friday’s abbreviated Black Friday session.

India at 17% versus China at 45% is a 28-percentage-point differential that makes manufacturing in India substantially cheaper than manufacturing in China for US-bound goods. The administration appears to be using tariff differentiation to accelerate supply-chain decoupling from China rather than simply imposing maximum rates across the board. If the India deal is confirmed and similar deals follow with other willing partners, the Thursday tariff announcement becomes a framework for managed decoupling rather than a broad trade war.
