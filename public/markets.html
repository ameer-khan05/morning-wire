Alphabet fell 11.75% after Q3 results showed search advertising revenue growing just 12% year-over-year — a deceleration from the prior quarter’s 16% — with free cash flow running negative $9.1 billion as AI infrastructure capital expenditure exceeded operating cash generation, delivering the Nasdaq’s worst single session in a month as the index fell 1.9% to 25,973; Microsoft moved in the opposite direction, rising 2.61% after Q3 EPS of $4.89 beat the $4.73 estimate, revenue of $92.28 billion beat $90.63 billion, and Azure cloud computing grew 48% against the 43% analyst expectation, partially offsetting Alphabet’s Nasdaq drag but leaving the S&P 500 down 0.3% to 7,700 and the Dow flat at 52,398; PCE inflation data released Thursday reinforced Chairman Warsh’s December tightening thesis, keeping the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.01%, and Brent crude eased 0.5% to $114.80 as Vice President Vance’s nuclear letter to Iran via Qatar tempered the Hormuz risk premium that Iran’s three-tanker mine incident had partially elevated, while gold edged 0.2% higher to $4,044 as the equity-session weakness generated a modest safe-haven bid.

Thursday’s markets session is an AI-investment reckoning with a split verdict: Alphabet’s capex is running ahead of its search-revenue return; Microsoft’s Azure shows AI cloud revenue materializing at the pace the infrastructure investment implies. Both are true simultaneously, in the same earnings week.

The Anchor · Alphabet and Microsoft — The Same Week, Two Different AI Return Stories

Alphabet’s Q3 miss and Microsoft’s Q3 beat are not two separate stories. They are one story about where AI investment stands in the technology sector. Microsoft committed to AI infrastructure in 2023 and is now collecting: Azure +48%, beating estimates by five points, Copilot penetration across enterprise accounts. Alphabet committed to the same cycle later and is still in the cost phase: search revenue at +12%, FCF negative $9.1 billion, Gemini 4 Carbon a genuine capability landmark not yet translating to proportionate revenue. The read is not that Alphabet’s investment was wrong but that it is six to eight quarters behind Microsoft on the return curve. The 10-year at 5.01% makes that wait more expensive — if Warsh hikes in December, the cost of capital rises at exactly the moment Alphabet needs its revenue inflection to arrive.

Alphabet’s Q3 earnings report missed revenue and profit expectations by a meaningful margin: search advertising revenue grew 12% year-over-year, decelerating from the prior quarter’s 16%, and free cash flow ran negative $9.1 billion as AI infrastructure capital expenditure exceeded operating cash generation — the stock fell 11.75%, making it the Nasdaq’s dominant drag for the session and driving the index 1.9% lower to 25,973 in the tech index’s worst single-session decline in a month, as investors confronted the gap between Alphabet’s AI capability leadership — demonstrated the same day by Gemini 4 Carbon’s #1 AI benchmark ranking — and the revenue return that AI infrastructure investment is generating in the company’s core search business.

The question is not whether AI investment will return — Gemini 4 Carbon’s benchmark leadership says the capability is there. The question is how many more quarters of negative FCF occur before search revenue catches up.

Microsoft beat Q3 expectations across every major metric — EPS of $4.89 against the $4.73 estimate, revenue of $92.28 billion against the $90.63 billion estimate, and Azure cloud computing growing 48% against the 43% analyst projection — with the 5-percentage-point Azure beat attributed to AI workloads including Copilot enterprise licensing and AI inference demand from companies running models on Azure infrastructure; the stock rose 2.61% in the session where Alphabet fell 11.75%, partially offsetting the Nasdaq’s Alphabet-driven decline and providing the most direct evidence of the week that AI cloud revenue is materializing at scale on Microsoft’s platform.

A 5-point beat on the growth rate estimate at 48% scale signals the estimate was structurally too conservative — AI inference demand is arriving faster than modeled. That is the direct counterpart to Alphabet’s capability leadership without commensurate revenue.
