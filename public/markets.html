The Anchor · The Week That Turned Negative — What the Nasdaq’s Worst Week Since March 2020 Tells You About the AI Trade’s Reckoning and What Comes Next

The Nasdaq turned negative for 2026 on Friday. The S&P 500 had its worst week since April 2025. The VIX hit its highest level of the year. These are not noise. The week that produced these results began with the AI trade at its highest valuation in history — Anthropic’s $81 billion IPO, the Nasdaq at year-to-date highs, the AI hardware supercycle priced at record multiples. By Friday’s close, the same trade had produced its worst week since the pandemic-era market crash. The mechanism is not ambiguous: the week introduced two categories of risk that the AI trade had not yet priced. The first is the IPO pricing question — Anthropic at $1.5 trillion before its revenue proves that multiple. The second is the SEC’s accounting probe into Nvidia — the first formal regulatory scrutiny of whether the AI hardware revenue that underpins the entire sector’s valuation is reported as accurately as markets have assumed. The first category is a valuation question. It resolves when revenue data closes the gap. The second category is a reporting question. It resolves when the SEC determines what it found. The market repriced both simultaneously, in a single week, in the direction of maximum uncertainty. The 10-year yield’s fall to 4.72% — bonds rallying 27 basis points on the week — confirms that the correction is not about rates. Rates have been falling as equities sell off. The correction is about the evidence base for the AI valuation thesis. That evidence base has not yet been produced. Until it is, the market will continue to test the gap between what the AI trade is priced at and what it has demonstrated.

The Nasdaq turning negative for 2026 is the week’s most precise market verdict: the index that most directly represents the AI trade began 2026 with one of the most powerful bull cases in its history and ended the week with that case under simultaneous interrogation from IPO price discovery and SEC accounting scrutiny. The two forces are not independent — Anthropic’s IPO pricing the application layer at $1.5 trillion and the SEC probing Nvidia’s hardware revenue reporting are both measuring the same underlying gap: between the AI sector’s priced revenue expectations and its demonstrated revenue reality.

Nvidia fell 14% on Friday after Reuters reported an SEC accounting probe into how the company categorizes its data-center revenue by geography and business unit — a development that Crypto Briefing called “the worst week in 18 years” for the AI chip giant — and the VIX closed at its highest level of 2026, with 247 Wall St. reporting that the fear index’s spike “historically” suggests the S&P 500 could rally 10.5% over the following three weeks; Jim Cramer separately said on Friday that the market had “sliced right through” his S&P 500 price target — a capitulation signal that market technicians sometimes read as a contrarian positive; and the Washington Post’s market analysis was headlined “the sudden reversal in the stock market, and what comes next,” reflecting a broad recognition that the AI bull case that defined 2026 has been fundamentally challenged in a single trading week.

Nvidia’s 14% drop is the week’s single most consequential market event because it added a new risk category — regulatory scrutiny of AI revenue reporting — to an AI trade that was already re-pricing under IPO valuation interrogation and demand uncertainty. The VIX spike’s historical pattern of preceding short-term rebounds is a real statistical tendency, but it doesn’t resolve the underlying uncertainty that drove the spike. The rebound, if it comes, will be a relief rally from oversold conditions. It will not be a resolution of the SEC probe or a proof of the AI sector’s revenue thesis. Those are longer-term questions that the week opened without answering.

The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.72% by Friday’s close — down approximately 27 basis points from Thursday’s 4.99% — as investors rotated aggressively out of equities and into US Treasuries in the week’s most pronounced flight-to-safety move, with the bond market’s weekly gain described by multiple analysts as the clearest evidence that the selloff is about equity valuation uncertainty, not rate fear; gold fell to $3,537 on Saturday morning spot pricing — down approximately 2.6% from Thursday’s $3,630 — as investors continued to prefer Treasuries over gold as the safety asset of choice, maintaining the pattern that has characterized every significant selloff since the spring; WTI Crude was at $83.41 (▲ 0.2%) and Brent at $91.80 (▼ 1.0%) on the Friday close — with markets closed Saturday — as risk-off demand-destruction concerns put modest pressure on Brent while WTI held near flat on Iran’s record drone attacks on commercial shipping providing a partial geopolitical offset.

The 10-year at 4.72% on a week when the Nasdaq lost 10% is the bond market’s definitive statement about the correction’s cause: yields fall when investors buy bonds for safety, and they are buying bonds, not gold, not Bitcoin, specifically Treasuries. That means the safety rotation is about equity-specific fear, not broad macro uncertainty — if it were broader macro uncertainty, gold would have rallied. Gold fell 2.6%. Treasuries rallied 27 basis points. The market is rotating out of AI equity risk into risk-free government paper, the safest possible safe-haven expression, and it is doing so at scale.
