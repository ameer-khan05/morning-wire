Markets are closed Sunday. The week begins with two independent variables that will determine direction in the first 72 hours: Iran's oil-price signal (Monday commodity open) and August CPI (Tuesday). Equity context entering the week: S&P 500 at a record 7,757.64 (Friday close), Nasdaq at 26,690.62 (+5.19% for the week, best weekly gain since April), Dow at 54,036.93 (+2.96% weekly), Russell 2000 at 3,034.49 (+1.10% Friday, outperforming on rate-sensitivity). The driver of Friday's record: July nonfarm payrolls came in at −23,000 versus the +80,000 consensus, and September rate-hike probability fell from 58% to 42%, triggering the risk-on rotation that is the market's standard response to a shift from "hike majority" to "hold or cut majority." Gold at $4,401.30 (+2.37%) and UST 10Y at 4.64% (▼7bp) confirmed the dovish rate read from the bond and commodity markets simultaneously with the equity record. Monday oil variable: Iran's national security chief on Saturday formally listed US concessions required before Hormuz reopens — lift the naval blockade, compensate for war damages, release frozen assets. The IRGC separately stated the Strait would reopen only after the US "fully accepts" conditions. WTI closed at $77.08 Friday — if the Monday open prices these escalating demands as a setback to Hormuz progress, a move to $79-81 would partially rebuild the conflict premium that had been declining on deal-progress pricing. If the demands are read as negotiating posture, WTI stays near $77. The market's interpretation of this distinction on Monday morning will be the first signal about which macro scenario the week is running toward. Aug CPI Tuesday: the headline number consensus is approximately 2.7% year-over-year (matching the prior print), with core CPI expected similarly benign. A print at or below 2.7%: hike probability falls from 42% toward 20-25%, the rate-cut thesis becomes the majority view for September, and equities have room to extend the record. A print at 3.0%+: hike probability returns above 50%, the majority scenario flips back to "hike or hold," and Friday's rally reverses substantially because the discount-rate assumption that justified the record re-rating would need to be revised upward again. A print in the 2.7-3.0% range: ambiguous, 42% hike probability persists without a clear directional move, and the record may consolidate rather than extend or reverse. The record S&P close entering the week is not a guarantee of continuation — it is an expression of a specific set of expectations about the September Fed meeting that Tuesday's CPI will either validate or complicate. The three-factor configuration that drove Friday's rally (labor soft, energy below conflict peak, AI earnings beating) needs inflation to cooperate as the fourth factor for the September rate-cut scenario to become the majority view. Oil on Monday morning and CPI on Tuesday morning are the two data points that will tell us whether this four-factor configuration is assembling or if one leg is weaker than the others.

Individual stock context entering the week: SpaceX (SPCX) at $133.11 following last week's 15.83% Friday gain (+19% weekly) and Atlassian's 35% earnings-day move have established the two highest-profile individual stock narratives entering the week. No major earnings are expected this week for large-cap tech names; the calendar focuses on macro data rather than earnings catalysts. The primary individual-stock variable is SpaceX's continued lockup absorption dynamics — the second tranche's schedule and whether Friday's confirmation that the first tranche cleared without a cliff extends to the second remains the stock-specific question.

August CPI on Tuesday is the most consequential single data point for equity markets in the two weeks between now and the September 16 FOMC — not because the labor market data is irrelevant (Friday's −23,000 payrolls were definitively dovish on the employment leg of the Fed's dual mandate) but because the inflation leg of the dual mandate is what will determine whether the Fed can translate a weak labor market into an actual cut rather than a hold; the rate-easing trade that produced Friday's record is pricing a hold-or-cut scenario at 58% probability, and adding a benign CPI would push that probability significantly above 60% while a hot CPI reverses it below 50%, changing the dominant narrative from "rate cut incoming" back to "higher for longer uncertainty." The consensus expectation is approximately 2.7% year-over-year for headline CPI and a similar figure for core — matching July's print and confirming the disinflation trajectory that has been accumulating since the Iran conflict's peak energy-price impact in early July. A 2.7% or lower print with a downside surprise in core components would be the maximum-bullish scenario for September equities.

Wednesday's PPI (producer prices) provides a leading indicator for future CPI because production costs feed into consumer prices with a 1-2 month lag; if PPI is also benign, the two-data-point confirmation of disinflation entering September would give the Fed the clearest possible case for a cut at the September 16 meeting. Thursday's jobless claims update the labor picture following the July shock — initial claims near the 230,000-250,000 range would suggest the July payroll print was a one-month anomaly rather than the start of a trend, while elevated claims would confirm deteriorating labor conditions. The eclipse on Tuesday (same day as CPI) creates an unusual trading context; US markets will open normally despite the eclipse, but thin morning participation from European trading desks could amplify any CPI-driven move in the first hour of US trading.

Quick hits

  • The relationship between Iran's escalating demands and WTI pricing deserves a precise mechanism map for readers tracking the oil-equity connection. WTI at $77.08 on Friday reflected a market that had priced significant deal progress (the bilateral Iran-Oman framework coordinates were set) while maintaining a $3-4 premium above the fully-reopened scenario (~$73-74). The premium reflects the probability that the blockade impasse persists and Hormuz stays closed beyond the expected resolution timeline. Iran's Day 40 demand list — blockade lifted, war compensation, frozen assets — raises the effective concession requirement for reopening above what was implied by the bilateral framework negotiations; the IRGC's "fully accept" language suggests Iran is not yet in the mode of accepting a phased or partial resolution. If Monday's oil market reads those signals as the preconditions for reopening having moved further away, the conflict premium expands from $3-4 toward $6-8, which would put WTI in the $79-82 range. The UNSC vote on August 17 (Monday of next week) then becomes the next hard deadline: if the vote produces a binding resolution that creates political cover for US blockade withdrawal, the conflict premium collapses back toward $3-4. This is the standard high-geopolitical-risk oil volatility pattern — big swings around hard diplomatic deadlines.
  • August 12 is simultaneously eclipse day and CPI release day — an unusual overlap that creates a specific trading context. CPI at 8:30am ET will be the first major market event of the day, likely producing significant Treasury and equity futures moves before US equity markets open at 9:30am. Totality over Iceland begins around 5:27am ET (09:27 UTC), before US markets open, so the eclipse itself does not directly interfere with market hours. The eclipse-day effect on trading volume is more indirect: European trading desks — which participate in currency and bond markets during US morning hours — may have thinner staffing than typical on eclipse morning as European traders make use of the mid-day GMT eclipse viewing. Thinner European participation in the pre-US-open period could amplify the CPI reaction in Treasury futures before US bond markets open fully. It is a small effect, but unusual enough to note: the single most consequential data release of the week coincides with the largest astronomical event over Western Europe in a decade.
