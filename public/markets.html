Wednesday’s market story is two positive catalysts simultaneously: election relief and AMD’s data center result. The Anchor · Dow +2.5%, Nasdaq Correction Reversed — AMD and the Election on the Same Morning

The Nasdaq entered correction on Tuesday. It reversed the correction on Wednesday. Both moves are driven by different things, and both are correct. The Tuesday correction was driven by Apple’s earnings miss — a demand signal for consumer devices that carry AI-spending thesis implications. The Wednesday reversal is driven by AMD’s data center result — a supply-side confirmation that the enterprise AI demand that the Menlo Ventures report simultaneously quantified at $177 billion. Apple and AMD are not in direct competition, but they are both inputs to the same market thesis: the AI-spending buildout requires consumer devices to carry the AI features that justify the data center spend that AMD supplies. Apple’s miss raised the question of whether the consumer device layer will generate the demand that connects the infrastructure spend to the revenue line. AMD’s beat answers from the other direction: the data center spending is happening regardless of Apple’s consumer miss, because enterprise AI is the independent variable and consumer AI is the dependent one. Cloud hyperscalers, enterprise software companies, and AI-first startups are spending on AMD data-center GPUs because the enterprise AI application market is growing at 5.4x — not because consumer iPhone sales met guidance. The oil story is the election result translated directly into commodity pricing: WTI below $100 for the first time in months is the energy market’s assessment that the Democratic sweep raises the probability of a U.S.–Iran peace deal and reduces the near-term Hormuz disruption risk premium that had been embedded in crude prices since February. That repricing is not guaranteed to hold — Iran’s Supreme Leader’s caution about reading too much into the election, and the possibility that Trump continues defensive operations, keeps $95 oil as an optimistic scenario rather than a settled new range. Gold rising to a record $4,047 on the same day oil falls 6.4% is the market saying: the geopolitical risk is lower today than yesterday, but it is not zero.

AMD reported Q3 2026 results after Tuesday’s close with total revenue of $13.4 billion and non-GAAP EPS of $2.09 — beating Wall Street estimates of $13.08 billion and $1.94 respectively — driven by a data center segment that generated $8.5 billion in revenue, up 97% year-over-year and ahead of consensus estimates of $8.19 billion; the data center segment now accounts for 64% of AMD’s total revenue, up from 47% a year ago, as hyperscaler and enterprise demand for AMD’s MI300X GPU accelerators and successor chips has grown faster than the broader market; CEO Lisa Su guided Q4 revenue to approximately $17 billion plus or minus 3%, implying continued double-digit sequential growth and confirming that the AI infrastructure buildout has not peaked in AMD’s customer base through year-end.

AMD’s 97% data center revenue growth — against a consensus of 90%-plus — is the number that demolishes the AI-spending-skepticism narrative from one direction. Apple’s miss raised the question of whether AI demand was real. AMD’s answer is that it is real enough to generate $8.5 billion in a single quarter from a single segment, growing at twice the prior year’s rate, in a company that is not Nvidia. The two data points together — Apple weak on consumer devices, AMD record on enterprise data centers — are consistent: the AI-spending thesis is an enterprise story, not a consumer story.
