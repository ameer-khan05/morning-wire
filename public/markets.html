The Anchor · The Monday Setup — Three New Inputs That Friday’s Close Did Not Price

Friday’s $93 Brent and $4,048 gold priced a world where de-escalation was the more probable scenario in Iran and geopolitical risk was receding. Sunday delivered Russia’s largest Kyiv missile salvo of the month. These two facts describe Monday’s opening trade. Three separate new inputs arrived Sunday that Friday’s close did not reflect: Russia’s 36-missile attack on Kyiv energy infrastructure, which argues for a geopolitical risk premium that oil and gold did not carry into the weekend; COP31 opening in Antalya Monday, where climate commitments from major fossil fuel producers could add supply-side constraint signals; and Democrats’ vow to reverse Trump’s tariff architecture, which introduces a trade-policy variable that Friday’s pricing did not account for — tariff reversal is generally positive for equities through the import-cost and consumer-spending channel but introduces uncertainty about the timeline and mechanism that is itself a temporary headwind. The 10-year at 4.87% and the fourth consecutive weekly S&P decline are the structural backdrop. Monday morning is the first price discovery session for all three new inputs simultaneously.
