The Anchor · Brent at $85.91 Gives Warsh the Macro Room He Needs — The Oil Drop Is the Most Consequential Pre-Jackson Hole Development for the Rate-Cut Narrative

The Iran-Oman temporary Hormuz deal is, for markets, primarily an oil story — and the oil story is primarily a Fed story. Brent crude at $85.91 versus $92.20 is a $6.29 per barrel reduction in the energy-input price that flows through US gasoline costs, transportation expenses, agricultural fuel costs, and manufacturing energy inputs. That reduction, if sustained, reduces the inflationary pipeline that has been the central complicating factor in the September rate-cut rationale: a Fed that cuts rates into a supply-driven oil price shock is defending a difficult policy position; a Fed that cuts rates when oil has fallen back from crisis highs to more moderate levels has a much cleaner macro narrative. Warsh's task at Jackson Hole Friday is to signal a September cut without contradicting the inflation data, and the Iran-Oman deal has just materially improved the inflation data's trajectory before he speaks. The question is whether the deal is durable — if the Hormuz corridor is actually implemented and mine-clearing begins, the oil market will price a sustained reduction in the disruption premium; if the conditions attached (US fulfilling June peace deal commitments) prove contentious and the deal collapses, Brent could retrace $5-$8 of Wednesday's decline within days. The Tuesday equity rebound requires its own careful read. Chip stocks led the recovery — Nasdaq ▲0.66%, Micron/AMD/Broadcom reversing most of Monday's 3-6% losses — but the catalyst for Tuesday's rebound was not any news event; it was pre-position rebuilding ahead of tonight's Nvidia result. The market is positioned for a Nvidia beat-and-raise, which means the equity session's Tuesday strength is predicated on tonight's result confirming the thesis. A Nvidia miss tonight would undo Tuesday's rebound and begin Wednesday's trading in a different frame than the one Wednesday's morning data has established. The sequencing matters: PCE arrives this morning before markets open, Nvidia reports after they close, and Warsh speaks Friday morning into whatever Wednesday's close has produced. The optimal sequence for equity markets is clean PCE this morning + Nvidia beat tonight = Wednesday closes constructively and Warsh speaks Friday into a market that has processed a clean week of confirming data. The risk sequence is hot PCE this morning + Nvidia miss tonight = Wednesday is a bad session and Warsh speaks into a market that is simultaneously processing two negative data surprises in the week before the FOMC blackout.

The Russell 2000's Tuesday recovery — ▲0.50% to 3,012.07, back above 3,000 after Monday's break below — reflects the Iran-Oman deal's downstream benefit for small-cap companies. The 50% tariff on Canada had been the primary headwind for small-cap companies with Canadian supply-chain exposure; Wednesday's oil price drop adds a secondary tailwind for small-cap energy consumers who have been facing higher energy-input costs from the Hormuz disruption premium. UST 10-year yields easing to 4.66% from 4.72% provides a floating-rate financing benefit for the small-cap companies most exposed to short-term borrowing costs. If the September rate cut confirms, the Russell's recovery from below 3,000 to 3,012 may prove to be the beginning of a sustained re-rating of the small-cap index's rate sensitivity — from a liability (higher financing costs for floating-rate debt) to an asset (September cut removes the single biggest overhang on small-cap earnings capacity).
