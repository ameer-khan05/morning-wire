The Anchor · Nasdaq Enters Correction, Oil Reprices Iran — Two Opposite Moves on the Same Day

The Nasdaq is in correction. The Dow is up. Oil is back above $100. All three are simultaneously true and internally consistent on Tuesday. Apple’s disappointing Q4 earnings are not just a single-company miss — they are the third consecutive major tech earnings report to disappoint guidance expectations after October’s hyperscaler cycle, and the Nasdaq’s entry into correction territory at more than 10% below its all-time high is the market’s verdict that the AI-spending thesis needs revenue evidence that the Q3 reporting season has not provided. Apple is the world’s largest consumer technology company. When Apple’s earnings disappoint, it is not a guidance problem — it is a demand signal for the devices and services that carry Apple Intelligence and that justify the AI-spending infrastructure the entire tech sector has been building toward. The Dow gaining 0.4% on the same day reflects that the market’s AI anxiety is specifically a growth-company, high-multiple, tech-sector anxiety: the Dow is less exposed to the AI-spending thesis than the Nasdaq, so it does not reprice on the same data. WTI above $100 on the Iran strikes is the partial re-inflation of Monday’s geopolitical premium deflation: the U.S. striking Iranian SAM sites in the Strait of Hormuz is not a de-escalation event. It is a military action that raises near-term Hormuz disruption risk even if the longer-term diplomatic track continues. Oil priced both correctly in two days — falling below $100 on diplomatic direction Monday, rising back above $100 on military action Tuesday.

Wednesday’s open prices all three simultaneously.

Monday and Tuesday together tell a coherent oil story: oil fell below $100 when Iran’s diplomatic direction was the dominant signal, then recovered above $100 when a U.S. military strike became the dominant signal. Both are correct pricing on their respective days. The question for Wednesday is which signal — diplomatic or military — dominates the post-midterm week in which Trump decides which track to continue.

AMD’s Q3 report is the last major earnings event of the week that matters to the Nasdaq correction thesis: if AMD’s data-center GPU revenue meets or exceeds consensus, it provides a single counter-data-point to the AI-spending-disappointment narrative that Apple and the hyperscalers have established this cycle.
