The Anchor · The Fourth Weekly Decline — What Friday’s Rebound Did and Did Not Fix

Friday’s 1.9% Nasdaq rebound was the right reaction to 165,000 October payrolls. It was also insufficient to prevent the S&P 500 from posting its fourth consecutive weekly decline. These two facts are compatible: the jobs report provided a floor and a dip-buying catalyst, but it did not resolve the regulatory uncertainty that produced Thursday’s selloff. Four consecutive weekly declines for the S&P describe a market that has been unable to build a recovery foundation since the Democratic congressional sweep introduced AI regulation uncertainty alongside macroeconomic strength. The 10-year yield at 4.87% is the bond market’s read on that same strength: the economy is too robust for easing, and December now carries a live rate-hike scenario. The setup for Monday is complicated by Saturday’s Iran developments: the US blockade expansion to land commerce and the IRGC missile ceiling removal are the signals oil markets priced out on Friday by sending Brent to $93, and they may need to be priced back in on Monday if the news is absorbed as escalation rather than posturing.

The simultaneous messages from Friday — jobs strong enough for equities to rebound and yields to jump — describe the late-cycle position precisely: the economy is too good for a Fed pivot and generating equity support at the same time, which is the configuration that squeezes equity risk premiums from both directions.
