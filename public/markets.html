Friday futures are up: Nasdaq ▲1.11%, S&P ▲0.47%, Dow ▲0.53%, absorbing Amazon's blowout against Apple's headwind.

Thursday's session answered a specific market question with two simultaneous data points: does AI infrastructure investment generate commercial returns at hyperscaler scale? Azure at 41% annual revenue growth said yes Wednesday; AWS at 37% quarterly growth said yes again Thursday. Meta's advertising-yield path is confirmed as the outlier — a business model where AI capex monetizes indirectly, on a lag, through advertising yield improvements rather than directly through compute-consumption pricing. Thursday's 2.80% Nasdaq gain is the market's pricing of that two-hyperscaler confirmation. Apple's 7% decline prices the uncertainty that the AI-in-product model adds: hardware AI demand is working (iPhone ▲22%), but services conversion is not accelerating at the expected rate. The market is picking AI winners — and the clearest winners are the ones charging enterprise customers for AI compute.

Quick hits

  • Amazon's $220 billion 2026 capex plan is now the largest among the hyperscalers — exceeding Meta's raised $130–145 billion guide — yet Amazon is simultaneously posting 43% operating income growth while Meta's FCF collapsed 91%. The structural explanation is timing-of-revenue-recognition: Amazon's AWS deploys infrastructure and immediately begins charging enterprise customers for compute capacity on usage-based models, with revenue recognition contemporaneous with infrastructure deployment. The capex and the operating income it generates arrive in the same financial reporting period. Meta's infrastructure supports advertising models that generate revenue on a lag: deploy AI, wait for the model to improve advertising yield, wait for advertisers to raise CPM bids on improved yield, then recognize the revenue. Amazon is spending $220 billion in 2026 while posting 43% operating income growth because its AI infrastructure monetization is direct, usage-based, and contemporaneous. The comparison with Meta's 91% FCF collapse is the clearest single illustration of how business model determines AI capex economics more than capex scale does.
  • Apple's Q2 services miss alongside iPhone's 22% growth creates a specific analytical tension for the services-as-AI-monetization thesis. The thesis assumes: AI features (Apple Intelligence, Siri on-device) drive iPhone upgrades (confirmed: ▲22%) → new iPhone users engage more with Apple's services ecosystem → services revenue grows faster than the installed base would otherwise predict. The missing step is the services engagement lift. iPhone's 22% growth is either pulling forward upgrades that would have happened anyway (replacement cycle acceleration without incremental services spend) or genuinely adding incremental users who engage with Apple's services at below-average rates. The services miss indicates that the incremental iPhone buyers from AI features are not services-heavy users at the rate the market expected. Tim Cook's successor will face this question on his or her first earnings call: did Apple Intelligence buy hardware volume without services conversion, or is the conversion coming with a one-to-two quarter lag?
