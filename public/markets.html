Intel fell 29% — its worst single-session performance since 1974 — after Q3 revenue of $16.5 billion beat estimates and data center revenue grew 78%, but Q4 guidance of $15–$16 billion came in well below expectations and the company disclosed a manufacturing accident at its Kiryat Gat fab in Israel; Amazon’s simultaneous miss — EPS $1.89 against the $1.97 estimate, with a $42 billion twelve-month cash burn from AI infrastructure — compounded the damage, and together the two reports drove the Nasdaq 1.4% lower to 26,185, the index’s largest single-day decline in three weeks, as October’s AI-earnings season closed with a second straight week of big-tech disappointment following Alphabet’s Thursday miss; the S&P 500 fell 0.3% to 7,720 and the Dow declined 0.3% to 52,343; in commodities, WTI crude rose 2.3% to $103.68 and Brent advanced 2.4% to $113.44 as President Trump’s in-person briefing from Admiral Cooper, Defense Secretary Hegseth, and Chairman Caine on Iran military strike options for after the November 3 midterms tightened the Hormuz risk premium; the 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.91% from 5.01% on a flight-to-safety bid, and gold fell 2.6% to $3,917 in a broad liquidation session.

Friday’s session closes October with a precise verdict on AI investment across the hyperscalers: Microsoft is collecting on its cycle, Amazon’s AWS is near collection, and Intel and Alphabet are still in the cost phase — three of the five major AI-infrastructure spenders on the wrong side of the return curve at the same moment.

The Anchor · Intel’s 29% Crash — October’s AI-Earnings Reckoning Closes With Its Worst Session

Four of the five major AI hyperscalers have now reported October earnings. Three have disappointed on cash flow, guidance, or both. Friday’s Intel crash is the month’s final and sharpest statement on the AI investment cycle. Intel’s 78% data center revenue growth in Q3 is real. It did not save the stock because Q4 guidance tells a different story: production constrained by a fab accident, PC cycle delayed, guidance range $15–$16 billion below where analysts needed it. The pattern across October’s earnings season is that AI infrastructure spend is accruing costs faster than it is generating revenue for the companies building it. Microsoft is the exception: Azure +48% showed last week that the revenue side can materialize. Amazon’s AWS +39% is also materializing, just not fast enough to cover the $42B cash burn. Intel and Alphabet are still in the investment phase. November’s calendar — including the midterms on Nov. 3, which Trump has flagged as a potential Iran action trigger — will determine whether the oil rally on top of the tech selloff becomes the month’s defining second act.

Intel fell 29% on Friday, its worst single-session performance since 1974, after Q3 results that beat revenue ($16.5B, +21%) and showed data center and AI revenue growing 78% to $7.3 billion were immediately eclipsed by Q4 guidance of $15.0–$16.0 billion and a non-GAAP EPS forecast of just $0.25 — both well below analyst estimates — compounded by disclosure that a manufacturing accident at Intel’s Kiryat Gat fab in Israel had disrupted production capacity at a critical moment; the stock’s 29% decline made it the Nasdaq’s dominant drag for the session and the month, erasing substantial ground as October closed on AI-earnings season’s harshest verdict.

Intel’s data center growth was the number AI-infrastructure bulls needed to see. The Q4 guidance and fab accident are the numbers the market actually priced. A factory in Israel is not a recoverable headwind by December — the disruption compound into the quarter the guidance already missed.

Nasdaq down 1.4%, Russell down 0.5% — the divergence is the tell. October’s AI-earnings reckoning hit the hyperscalers specifically, not the broader economy. Microsoft remains the outlier that shows the revenue side is achievable; everyone else this week has shown it is not yet arrived.

WTI crude rose 2.3% to $103.68 and Brent advanced 2.4% to $113.44 as news emerged that President Trump received an in-person briefing from CENTCOM Admiral Brad Cooper, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and Joint Chiefs Chairman General Dan Caine on options for renewed strikes on Iran — with reports indicating the administration is weighing whether to resume major combat operations after the November 3 midterms, a date Trump has previously flagged as a potential action trigger; the military-options briefing reversed some of the diplomatic premium built into oil prices after Vice President Vance’s nuclear letter to Tehran via Qatar on Thursday, as the administration’s dual diplomatic-military posture sent the clearest signal yet that the Iran timeline is post-midterms rather than post-deal.

Oil rising 2.3% on a day when equities fell 1.4% is the precise opposite of normal risk-off behavior: the Iran military briefing created a geopolitical premium that disconnected crude from the equity selloff. Brent back above $113 after briefly retreating from Thursday’s $114.80 reinstates the Hormuz-tension floor.

The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.91% from Thursday’s 5.01% as the Intel crash, Binance breach, and Friday’s broad risk-off session generated a flight-to-safety bid for government bonds, easing the yield by 10 basis points even with Warsh’s December rate-hike thesis still priced in; gold fell 2.6% to $3,917 — a counterintuitive move in a risk-off session — as the combination of Intel’s 29% collapse, leveraged position unwinds, and the Binance-triggered liquidity stress in crypto markets generated forced selling across asset classes, with gold serving as a source of liquid collateral for margin calls rather than a safe haven.

Gold falling 2.6% while bonds rally 10 bps captures the session’s character precisely: this is a forced-liquidation event, not a pure risk-off rotation. When gold and equities fall together, margin calls are the driver. The bond rally is the institutional safety trade; the gold drop is the leveraged desk covering.
