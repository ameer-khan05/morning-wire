The Anchor · Friday's Close Is the Baseline; Monday's Open Prices the Weekend

Friday's close establishes the market baseline for the FOMC blackout period: S&P at 7,715.47, Nasdaq at 26,411.07, UST 10Y at approximately 4.84%, and September rate-hike odds at 55.7%. That baseline reflects Thursday's 3.7% PCE print and Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole keynote — but was set before two material weekend developments: Nvidia's reported $12.9 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, and the UAE's accusation that Iran attacked two ADNOC-linked commercial vessels. Neither was priced at Friday's close. Monday's equity and commodity open will be the first opportunity to price both simultaneously in a FOMC blackout environment with no Fed guidance available. The UAE-ADNOC vessel allegation is the more immediately consequential commodity risk for Monday. Brent at $87.87 priced Hormuz as an unresolved supply restriction — Iran's four conditions for reopening the Strait, US non-engagement, mine-clearing not begun — but not as an active kinetic conflict against Gulf state commercial shipping. If the attacks are confirmed, the risk premium embedded in Brent shifts from "supply restriction uncertainty" to "active maritime conflict against GCC commercial interests," a categorically higher risk level. The Nvidia-Hugging Face deal is a positive catalyst for the Nasdaq: the deal establishes Nvidia as a vertically integrated AI infrastructure company with recurring software revenue, supporting the valuation premium of the AI infrastructure thesis. Friday's 0.50% Nasdaq decline came despite Thursday's strong Nvidia-led gain; Monday's open will test whether the Hugging Face deal adds enough positive catalyst to absorb the macro headwinds.

The 10-year Treasury yield closed Friday at approximately 4.84%, representing an 18-basis-point move from Wednesday's 4.66% driven by two sequential shocks: Thursday's July PCE print of 3.7% year-over-year — 170 basis points above the Fed's 2% target — followed by Friday's hawkish keynote from Chair Warsh at Jackson Hole, in which he signaled that inflation remains too high and further tightening may be required. CME FedWatch September rate-hike probability stands at 55.7%, up sharply from approximately 35% before Thursday's PCE data. The FOMC blackout that began Saturday means the Fed cannot revise those expectations through forward guidance, press conferences, or speeches until after the rate decision itself — leaving markets to form rate path expectations through the lens of incoming data alone.

The rate path context for the next 17 days is this: if August CPI (due approximately September 10, before the September 15 CLARITY vote) comes in above 3.5%, the September rate-hike probability approaches certainty and markets will need to price a simultaneous rate hike and legislative vote in the same week. If CPI moderates below 3.2%, September rate-hike odds would likely fall back toward 30% even under the blackout, as the market interprets the data independently. The 17-day blackout window makes August CPI — not Fed guidance — the dominant variable for rate path formation. The CLARITY countdown runs in parallel: if the cloture vote on September 15 succeeds, crypto markets reprice rapidly in a direction that also affects risk-on/risk-off equity positioning, creating a multi-variable resolution event on September 15-16 that the current FOMC blackout makes it impossible for the Fed to pre-explain.

WTI crude closed Friday at $82.82 (▼0.86%), Brent at approximately $87.87, and gold at approximately $4,595 (▼0.71%). The gold decline reflects upward yield pressure from Warsh's hawkish keynote — higher real yields reduce the opportunity cost of non-yielding assets like gold. Brent at $87.87 incorporates the Hormuz Day 60 supply restriction: Iranian conditions for reopening the Strait unchanged, US not negotiating, mine-clearing not begun, commodity vessel throughput at roughly one-third of pre-crisis levels. A new risk arrived Saturday: the UAE accused Iran of attacking two ADNOC-linked commercial vessels, an allegation not yet confirmed and not priced in Friday's Brent close. If confirmed Monday, Brent faces upward pressure from a qualitative shift in the Hormuz conflict — from supply restriction to active targeting of GCC state commercial assets.

Supply restriction and active vessel targeting are distinct risk categories. Brent at $87.87 prices the former: a Strait throughput reduction that is real, persistent, and priced as a supply constraint with a possible diplomatic resolution. Active Iranian targeting of UAE-linked commercial vessels prices the latter: an escalation that involves direct conflict with GCC commercial sovereignty and creates new pressure on the UAE's parallel diplomatic architecture with Tehran. The UAE has the most commercially active relationship with Iran of any GCC state; confirmed attacks on UAE-linked vessels would create political pressure on the UAE to close those channels and align more explicitly with the US position, narrowing the available mediation pathways for a diplomatic resolution. The Brent price that reflects all of this is not $87.87. Monday's commodity open will be the first opportunity to find out what it is.
