The Anchor · Q4 Week 1 Close — What the PCE-ISM-Jobs Three-Session Sequence Established as the Starting Baseline for October

Q4’s first week produced the most informative possible opening sequence: three consecutive sessions with conflicting macro signals, and a bond market that revealed its signal hierarchy by pricing all three in sequence. The result — the 10-year at 5.09% and the S&P at 7,727 entering the first weekend — is October’s baseline. Wednesday: PCE at 3.4% year-over-year decelerating below consensus sent yields from 5.18% to 5.06%, pricing November as a Fed hold. Thursday: ISM manufacturing prices paid at 77.9 — 6.8 points above August — reversed 18 of those 19 basis points in a single session, sending the 10-year to 5.298%. The bond market read ISM prices as evidence that PCE’s deceleration would not persist into November and December readings. Friday: Jobs +29,000 against an 84,000 consensus, unemployment rising to 4.2%, reversed the ISM move and more, sending the 10-year to 5.09% — 21 basis points below Thursday’s close and 3 basis points above Wednesday’s PCE-day level. The three-session sequence established four things. First, the bond market’s Q4 signal hierarchy: labor data outweighs manufacturing cost data as the dominant near-term Fed signal, because a 29,000 jobs print undercuts the labor component of the dual-mandate case for hiking regardless of what ISM prices say. Second, the technology sector’s AI infrastructure multiple is structurally higher than pre-AI baselines: the Nasdaq held positive through Thursday’s 24-basis-point yield spike and amplified Friday’s yield retreat to 1.7% gains, indicating a floor at 5.3% yields and a ceiling that extends further on the upside when yields fall. Third, the inflation picture is genuinely ambiguous — not temporarily confused — because the ISM and jobs data are describing two real things simultaneously: manufacturing input costs that are rising and a labor market that is cooling, a combination the Fed cannot resolve with one November decision. Fourth, Brent’s proximity to $100 and WTI at $93 are not transient oil volatility but a persistent risk premium from the Iran situation that will not resolve before the November 3 nuclear deadline. October 15 CPI is the data event that will do most to determine whether the ISM prices forecast or the jobs-miss narrative is the correct Q4 inflation read. Until then, the baseline is set.
