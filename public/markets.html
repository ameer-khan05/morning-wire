Markets delivered a split verdict Thursday: Nvidia's $96.22 billion Q2 FY27 earnings beat drove Nasdaq up 1.5% to 26,543.57 and the S&P 500 up 0.8% to 7,738.70, with NVDA +9%, Salesforce +11.2%, and CrowdStrike +9% leading the tech sector's strongest single-session rally of the year — while July PCE landed at 3.7% year-over-year on the headline (core at 3.3%), well above the 2.3% consensus, pushing the 10-year Treasury yield from 4.66% to 4.77% and raising serious questions about whether September's anticipated 25 basis-point rate cut can be defended at 3.7% inflation. Brent crude rebounded from Wednesday's deal-driven $85.91 to $87.65 as Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed the US is "standing in the way" of the Hormuz corridor implementation, and gold fell approximately 0.58% to $4,628 under the dual pressure of rising yields and higher rate-hike probability. The equity market chose Nvidia over PCE on Thursday; the bond market chose PCE over Nvidia.

Warsh enters Friday's Jackson Hole keynote in a materially more difficult communication position than Wednesday's edition described. On Wednesday, the narrative was: lower oil, clean PCE, rebounding equities — the optimal setup for a September cut signal. On Thursday, the actual data produced the opposite: 3.7% PCE headline, Brent rebounding to $87.65 on Hormuz complications, and a bond market already pricing reduced September cut probability at 4.77% UST 10-year. Warsh must address Friday whether 3.7% PCE changes the September calculus, and his answer will determine whether equity markets retain Thursday's gains through the weekend or give them back as bond yields lead.

The Anchor · The Bond Market Sees 3.7% PCE — the Equity Market Sees $96 Billion Nvidia — and Warsh Must Reconcile Both Friday at Jackson Hole

Thursday's market session was two separate markets operating from two separate data inputs, producing two separate verdicts that will be reconciled — or not — by what Warsh says Friday morning at 10am EDT. The equity market's verdict: Nvidia's $96.22 billion quarter (+106% year-over-year) is the most definitive validation of the AI infrastructure investment cycle in the history of that cycle, and the revenue acceleration from $65 billion to $89 billion in the data center segment in a single quarter means the hyperscaler AI capex commitments are not just intact — they are accelerating. That verdict drove the Nasdaq up 1.5%, with NVDA +9%, Salesforce +11.2%, and CrowdStrike +9% all confirming that the AI trade has broadened from hardware to the enterprise software layer that Salesforce and CrowdStrike represent. The bond market's verdict: 3.7% PCE on the headline — the 65th consecutive month above the Fed's 2% target, 140 basis points above the consensus that the market had built its September cut expectation on — means that cutting rates in September would require either a new data narrative or an explicit acknowledgment that the Hormuz energy-inflation spike is transitory enough to look through. At 4.77% UST 10-year, the bond market is pricing the scenario that Warsh cannot credibly make that argument on Friday. The Brent crude move from $85.91 (Wednesday's post-deal close) to $87.65 (Thursday's close) is a direct response to the Hormuz deal's complications. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said Thursday that the US is "standing in the way" of implementation; a senior Iranian source said the deal is "not yet finalized." The mine-clearing operations that were the structural differentiator of Wednesday's announcement — the observable, physically verifiable component — have not begun. Maritime intelligence services tracking vessel movements in the Hormuz approaches report no mine-clearing vessel deployment as of Thursday. If the mine-clearing vessels are not deployed within the next 7-10 days, the credibility of the deal as a sustained de-escalation mechanism deteriorates substantially, and Brent's move from $87.65 toward $90 becomes the base case rather than the risk case. Warsh enters Friday needing to address whether 3.7% PCE, a partially-unwinding Iran-Oman deal, and a 10-year yield at 4.77% constitute the environment for a September cut — and whether the AI infrastructure cycle's validation by Nvidia's $96 billion supports a pro-growth rate cut rationale that the inflation data alone would not support. This is the most complex rate-communication environment since the mid-2022 rate-hiking cycle accelerated, and the Jackson Hole symposium is the only remaining opportunity before the September 16 decision for Warsh to shape expectations.

The Russell 2000's 0.5% gain Thursday — to 3,027.13, maintaining the above-3,000 recovery from Monday's break below — reflects a nuanced read: small-cap companies benefited from Thursday's risk-on sentiment (Nvidia AI capex validation = growth outlook positive) while facing the headwind of 4.77% UST 10-year yields (which increase floating-rate borrowing costs for small-cap companies). The net result, +0.5%, suggests the growth signal slightly outweighed the rate signal for small-cap investors on Thursday — but Warsh's Friday keynote could change that calculus: a "September hold" signal would push yields higher still and pressure the Russell's rate-sensitivity exposure, while a "September cut despite 3.7% PCE" signal would be a significant small-cap catalyst as the rate sensitivity turns from headwind to tailwind.

July PCE landed at 3.7% year-over-year on the headline and 3.3% on core — on the 65th consecutive month of above-target inflation — 140 basis points above the 2.3% consensus, triggering a 10-year Treasury yield move from 4.66% to 4.77% as the bond market priced reduced probability of September's anticipated 25 basis-point rate cut. Forbes headlined "Rate Hikes Now More Likely"; Al Jazeera noted the reading as another month of US inflation "holding at 3.7%, above Fed target." The reading's significance for the Jackson Hole setup is direct: before Thursday, Warsh could signal a September cut by arguing clean PCE plus improving energy trajectory; after Thursday, he must either argue that 3.7% is lookthrough-able on the forward trajectory, or signal a hold and accept the equity market consequence of the rate-path reversal.

The forward PCE trajectory argument — that August data will look better because the Iran-Oman deal will have reduced oil prices — is the most structurally available argument for a September cut given Thursday's reading. July PCE captures data through July 27, before Wednesday's Iran-Oman announcement. The deal, even if contested, has reduced oil prices from crisis highs. If the deal's oil-price effect holds into August, the August PCE reading (released in late September, after the September 16 FOMC decision) could show meaningful energy disinflation. Warsh could argue: "The July reading reflects conditions that have materially improved since the quarter-end. Cutting in September is appropriate given the trajectory, not the level." This is a defensible argument, but it requires Warsh to explicitly acknowledge that he is prioritizing the forward trajectory over the current 3.7% reading — an unconventional communication that the bond market will scrutinize carefully. The 11-basis-point move in 10-year yields Thursday tells you how much uncertainty the bond market is now pricing into what was, 48 hours ago, a near-certain September cut.

Brent crude rebounded from Wednesday's deal-driven $85.91 to $87.65 Thursday as Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed the US is "standing in the way" of implementing the Hormuz corridor agreement and mine-clearing operations had not begun, with Qatar's Prime Minister flying to Tehran for emergency mediation and the UAE announcing suspension of all trade and financial transactions with Iran. WTI followed to $82.90, up 3.88%. The Eastern Herald headlined Thursday's oil dynamics as "Brent at $87.65 as PCE Data Fails to Break the Floor" — a reference to the fact that even a hot PCE reading (which typically creates growth-slowdown expectations that pressure oil) did not drive Brent below $87.65, because the Hormuz uncertainty created an offsetting supply-risk floor. Gold fell approximately 0.58% to $4,628 as rising yields (4.77% UST 10-year) and higher rate-hike probability from the PCE reading applied the standard inverse pressure on the non-yielding metal.

The gap between Wednesday's $85.91 and Thursday's $87.65 — roughly $1.74 per barrel in a single session — is the oil market's initial pricing of the probability that the Hormuz deal faces substantial implementation obstacles. At $85.91 Wednesday, the market was pricing a meaningful probability of mine-clearing beginning within 10-14 days and the temporary corridor becoming operational. At $87.65 Thursday, the IRGC's "US standing in the way" claim and the absence of mine-clearing vessel deployment are being priced as a material reduction in that probability. The market is now pricing a scenario in which the deal exists as a formal diplomatic announcement but takes weeks to months to implement (or collapses), rather than the scenario Wednesday's session was pricing (credible near-term implementation). Gold at $4,628 — still roughly $750 above pre-crisis levels — reflects that the base geopolitical premium from the Hormuz crisis remains elevated even as the PCE and rate-hike probability create a modest downward counterpressure.
