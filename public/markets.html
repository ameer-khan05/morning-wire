U.S. equity markets snapped a four-week losing streak Monday as the S&P 500 surged 2.2% to 7,537 and all 11 sectors closed higher, adding approximately $1 trillion in market capitalization, as Brent crude collapsed 5.0% to $88.35 and WTI fell to $88.23 on a simultaneous de-escalation signal from two directions: Trump’s team confirming Ukraine-Russia peace talks in Abu Dhabi for November 10–11 — the fourth round of formal US-brokered negotiations since January — and COP31 opening in Antalya, Turkey with 190+ nations whose commitments to fossil fuel production phase-down add supply-side constraint pressure on the same session that geopolitical risk premiums on oil are deflating; AMD surged 11% on strong forward guidance, Nvidia fell 6.4% on new China probe headlines, the Dow gained 2.0% to 52,295, the Russell 2000 added 2.8% to 2,830, and the CBOE Volatility Index fell 20% to 20.51; the 10-year Treasury yield closed at 4.86%, essentially flat, with December Fed rate-hike odds still live after October’s 165,000 payrolls.

Monday’s markets session is a confluence of de-escalation signals hitting simultaneously: Iran peace talks, Ukraine-Russia negotiations, COP31 climate commitments — each one, separately, is oil-negative and risk-asset positive. Together in a single session they produced a 5% oil crash and a 2% equity surge that ended four consecutive losing weeks.

The Anchor · The Oil Crash — Iran De-Escalation, Abu Dhabi Talks, and COP31 All Price in the Same Session

Three separate de-escalation signals hit oil in the same Monday session: Trump announcing peace talks in Abu Dhabi for November 10–11 reduced the geopolitical risk premium on oil that Russia-Ukraine and Iran were maintaining; COP31 opening in Antalya with major fossil fuel producers present added supply-side commitment pressure; and Iran de-escalation signals reduced the Strait of Hormuz disruption risk that had been supporting Brent above $90. Brent crashing 5% to $88.35 in a single session is the market’s simultaneous pricing of all three inputs. Each one, by itself, would likely produce a 1-2% oil decline. Together — because they arrived in the same session, reinforcing each other — they produced a 5% move. The equity market treated the same events as a 2% buying signal: oil falling signals lower energy costs, lower inflation pressure, and reduced geopolitical risk premium on equities. The result is the highest single-session equity gain since the AI selloff and the end of the four-week S&P losing streak. The question the Abu Dhabi talks will answer over the next 48 hours is whether Monday’s pricing was correct. If the November 10–11 negotiations produce a ceasefire framework, the de-escalation trade is confirmed and oil stays below $90. If they produce nothing, Brent is repriced back above $90 by Wednesday, and the equity session partly unwinds.

Brent below $90 for the first time since February is not a normal daily oil move — it is a regime signal. The market is pricing the probability of geopolitical de-escalation high enough to remove the risk premium that has held oil above $90 since the Iran conflict began. Abu Dhabi on November 10–11 will confirm or deny the thesis in real time.

AMD’s 11% gain versus Nvidia’s 6.4% decline in the same session is not a one-day anomaly — it is the AI chip market resolving a structural question: which semiconductor company is best positioned to serve AI demand without China-related export risk? Monday’s answer, confirmed by forward guidance, is AMD. That may change. For now, it is the market’s clearest bet.
