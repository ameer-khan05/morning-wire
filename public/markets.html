The Anchor · The Week’s Market Picture — What Friday’s Close Means for Monday’s Open

The week that ended Friday produced four distinct market signals that Monday’s session will begin to process. Signal one: the 10-year yield at 5.14% — not just at 5%, but above it, driven by PPI YoY at 6.5%, the highest since November 2022. The 5.14% level broke Bitcoin’s 200-day moving average, pushed gold to a 13-month low, and caused Russell 2000 to fall 2.6% on a day large caps rose 0.8%. It established the rate environment for Monday’s open as structurally unchanged: the December 11 FOMC hike is priced at near-certain odds, and no weekend development challenges that pricing. Signal two: Trump’s “we don’t need a deal with Iran” statement, combined with Vance’s removal from the negotiating role. For oil markets, this is unambiguously bullish: the active diplomatic track that could have produced a de-escalation was removed on Saturday. Brent at $95.54 enters Monday without a near-term ceiling from diplomacy — the $100-per-barrel level that was theoretical last week is now the base case if the conflict maintains its current pace. Signal three: Kushner’s announcement that Europe joins Ukraine talks. For equity markets, this is marginally constructive — a broadened diplomatic process reduces the binary risk of a sudden US withdrawal from the negotiations — though Russia’s evasiveness about the commitment reduces the near-term impact. Signal four: the week’s corporate developments — Nvidia’s 12.75% weekly decline, Anthropic’s IPO 6x oversubscribed, OpenAI’s $10 billion Q3, Tesla-SpaceX merger committees. These are stock-specific catalysts, not macro signals. Monday’s session will begin the process of pricing all four.

The December 11 FOMC date is six weeks away. In those six weeks, the data that could change the near-certain hike expectation would need to show a sharp and unexpected deceleration in both CPI and PPI that the last five months of data do not suggest is coming. The base case for Monday’s market is a Fed that hikes in December with high probability, and the 10-year that reflects that reality at 5.14%.
