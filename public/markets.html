The Anchor · Monday’s Test — Why the First Full Session After Thanksgiving Is the Tariff Market’s Real Reckoning

Friday’s Black Friday session was abbreviated, thin, and tilted by AI-sector sentiment. Monday is the first full session. The Thursday Thanksgiving-day tariff announcement set rates on 14 trade partners: China at additional duties as high as 45% on some product categories, India reportedly negotiating toward 17%, and 12 other economies awaiting bilateral engagement or accepting listed rates. Friday’s half-day session absorbed this framework — weakly. Volume was constrained, the session closed at 1pm ET, and the market’s reading of the tariff risk was subordinate to the Riemann hypothesis AI-sector catalyst that sent Anthropic shares above their IPO price. Monday brings full volume, full institutional participation, and the first post-holiday opportunity for multi-billion-dollar rebalancing desks to reprice the tariff overlay. The India deal — if confirmed before Monday’s open — is a positive signal: a 28-percentage-point differential between India (17%) and China (45%) accelerates supply-chain diversification toward a willing partner and signals the administration is using the tariff schedule as a negotiating instrument rather than a fixed outcome. The China rate — if Monday’s desk-level analysis prices 45% duties on China-linked categories at current import volumes — has not been fully absorbed. These two reads can coexist: a Monday market that rallies on India’s tariff resolution while repricing China-linked equities downward is not a contradiction. What is unusual is that the week’s most consequential market event — the tariff announcement — was repriced in a half-day session that was dominated by a mathematics proof. Monday normalizes the signal-to-noise ratio.
