Science · AHA Sessions 2026 · ZEUS Fails · Lp(a)HORIZON Fails · CAC Score · Leucine · Friday, November 6, 2026

The American Heart Association’s Scientific Sessions 2026 delivered two of the most anticipated negative trial results in recent cardiology memory on Friday: the ZEUS trial of ziltivekimab, Novo Nordisk’s IL-6 ligand inhibitor anti-inflammatory cardiovascular drug, missed its primary MACE endpoint despite achieving statistically significant high-sensitivity C-reactive protein reduction — the third major failure for anti-inflammatory cardiovascular therapy after CANTOS and canakinumab; and the Lp(a)HORIZON trial of pelacarsen — the first large-scale outcome trial for Lp(a) lowering — also missed its primary endpoint despite achieving more than 60% reduction in Lp(a), raising deep questions about whether lipoprotein(a) lowering translates to cardiovascular event reduction even when the biomarker target is aggressively hit; separately, a new large-scale CAC score statin study found that heart scans lead to fewer statin prescriptions with identical cardiovascular outcomes, challenging current aggressive statin-initiation guidelines; and University of Cologne researchers published findings that the amino acid leucine stabilizes mitochondrial proteins and boosts cellular ATP production, identifying a potential dietary aging-research target.

Two landmark negative trials at the same AHA sessions is a reckoning for the cardiovascular biomarker paradigm: lowering hs-CRP and lowering Lp(a) both failed to translate to fewer heart attacks, forcing a question about whether inflammation and lipoprotein(a) are causes of cardiovascular events or markers of something upstream that neither drug addresses.

The Anchor · AHA Sessions 2026 — The Biomarker Paradigm’s Worst Day

Lowering hs-CRP failed to prevent heart attacks in ZEUS. Lowering Lp(a) by more than 60% failed to prevent heart attacks in HORIZON. These are not two independent bad news stories — they are two simultaneous failures in the same underlying scientific theory: that attacking known cardiovascular biomarkers will produce downstream cardiovascular event reduction. The biomarker-to-outcome translation is the assumption that justifies the entire next generation of targeted cardiovascular drugs in development. CANTOS failed with canakinumab on the inflammation pathway. ZEUS failed with ziltivekimab on the same inflammation pathway. HORIZON failed with pelacarsen on the Lp(a) pathway. Three major trials. Three failures. The lipid-and-inflammation paradigm that has dominated cardiovascular drug development for 15 years just had its worst AHA Sessions in that period. The question the field will now ask is whether the biomarkers are causal or correlative — and the answer to that question restructures the entire cardiovascular drug development pipeline.

The ZEUS trial of ziltivekimab — Novo Nordisk’s IL-6 ligand inhibitor designed to reduce cardiovascular risk through anti-inflammatory action — missed its primary MACE endpoint at AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 despite achieving statistically significant high-sensitivity C-reactive protein reduction, confirming that ziltivekimab successfully reduces the inflammatory biomarker it targets while failing to translate that reduction into fewer major adverse cardiovascular events including heart attacks, strokes, and cardiovascular deaths; ZEUS was built on the scientific logic of CANTOS — the landmark canakinumab trial that showed reduced MACE but insufficient magnitude relative to side effect profile — with the expectation that a more selective IL-6 pathway inhibitor would achieve a better therapeutic index; the ZEUS failure is the second major trial failure for anti-inflammatory cardiovascular therapy after CANTOS, and raises the same question that CANTOS raised: whether inflammation is a cause of cardiovascular events or a correlated marker of disease processes that anti-inflammatory therapy cannot interrupt.

Novo Nordisk’s pipeline and stock implications are secondary to the science: ziltivekimab’s failure, combined with CANTOS, makes a strong empirical case that hs-CRP reduction is not a causal intervention for MACE — it treats the downstream marker without touching whatever upstream process drives both the inflammation and the events.

Lp(a) HORIZON Trial Pelacarsen Misses Primary MACE Endpoint 60 Percent Lp(a) Reduction Cardiovascular Events Not Reduced Olpasiran Muvalaplin Pipeline Implications First Large Scale Lp(a) Outcome Trial AHA 2026 Lp(a)HORIZON · Pelacarsen · MACE Miss HORIZON (Fri Nov 6 2026): pelacarsen → antisense → Lp(a) → 60%+ → lowering → MACE → primary → endpoint → missed → olpasiran → muvalaplin → pipeline → implications → causal → question → raised → AHA 2026

The Lp(a)HORIZON trial of pelacarsen — the first large-scale cardiovascular outcome trial for a lipoprotein(a)-lowering drug — missed its primary MACE endpoint at AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 despite achieving more than 60% reduction in Lp(a) levels, raising the most consequential question in cardiovascular genetics of the year: whether lipoprotein(a) elevation is a causal risk factor for cardiovascular disease or a marker correlated with underlying genetic risk that cannot be modified by lowering the lipoprotein itself; pelacarsen is an antisense oligonucleotide that substantially reduces Lp(a) in blood, and the magnitude of its biomarker reduction was not in question — the question was whether that reduction would translate to fewer heart attacks and strokes; it did not in HORIZON; the implications extend beyond pelacarsen to the broader Lp(a)-lowering pipeline, including olpasiran (Amgen) and muvalaplin (Eli Lilly), which are designed around the same causal hypothesis that HORIZON just challenged.

HORIZON does not prove Lp(a) is non-causal — trial design issues including population selection, baseline Lp(a) levels, and event rates could explain the miss — but it substantially increases the evidentiary burden on the remaining Lp(a) pipeline. Olpasiran and muvalaplin will be read very differently in the wake of this result.

CAC Score Statin Study Heart Scan Fewer Statin Prescriptions Same Cardiovascular Outcomes Challenges Current Aggressive Statin Initiation Guidelines Individualized Risk Assessment Evidence AHA 2026 Fri Nov 6 2026 CAC Score · Fewer Statins · Same Outcomes CAC (Fri Nov 6 2026): heart scan → CAC → score → guided → decisions → fewer → statin → prescriptions → identical → cardiovascular → outcomes → guideline → challenge → risk → stratification → individualized

A large-scale study presented at AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 found that using coronary artery calcium (CAC) scores from non-invasive heart scans to guide statin prescription decisions resulted in significantly fewer statin prescriptions than current risk-score-based guidelines — with identical cardiovascular outcomes over a median 7-year follow-up period — challenging the current approach of using 10-year pooled-cohort equation scores to initiate statin therapy in a substantial portion of adults 40–75; the study compared CAC-score-guided decisions against standard risk-score-initiated therapy in a large population across multiple health systems, finding that CAC scores effectively stratified patients who could safely defer statins from those where statin initiation was clinically necessary, without producing higher rates of myocardial infarction or stroke in the CAC-deferred group; the finding is actionable: a CAC score of zero in a low-to-intermediate-risk patient identifies a population where statin therapy adds minimal marginal benefit against a background of guideline pressure to prescribe.

The CAC score statin study lands in a context where the ZEUS and HORIZON results have already raised questions about cardiovascular biomarker causality. If inflammatory biomarker and Lp(a) lowering don’t reduce events, and CAC-guided decisions show we’re overprescribing statins to populations who don’t benefit, the aggregate message from Friday’s AHA sessions is: cardiovascular medicine needs a paradigm recalibration.

Leucine Amino Acid Mitochondrial Protein Stabilization Cellular ATP Production Boost University Cologne Researchers Aging Research Target Mitochondrial Function Caloric Restriction Analogy Fri Nov 6 2026 Leucine · Mitochondria · ATP · Aging Target Leucine (Fri Nov 6 2026): leucine → amino acid → mitochondrial → proteins → stabilizes → ATP → production → boosts → University → Cologne → aging → research → target → mTORC1 → cellular → energy → metabolism

Researchers at the University of Cologne published findings in Nature Aging that the branched-chain amino acid leucine stabilizes key mitochondrial proteins and significantly boosts cellular ATP production, identifying a dietary and supplementation pathway with potential relevance to aging research and age-related metabolic decline; the team found that leucine activates mTORC1 signaling in a way that specifically protects mitochondrial protein complexes from age-related degradation — a mechanism analogous to some effects of caloric restriction — and demonstrated the finding across both cell culture and mouse models, with aged mice supplemented with leucine showing measurably higher cellular energy output on metabolic assays; leucine is already a focus of exercise physiology and muscle-protein-synthesis research, but its direct role in mitochondrial protein stabilization represents a new research angle that could connect dietary protein composition to mitochondrial aging biology without requiring caloric restriction.

The leucine finding arrives in a week where cardiovascular biomarker research had a rough day — it is a reminder that the most promising aging targets are sometimes the ones that reframe familiar nutrition science rather than develop entirely novel pharmaceutical mechanisms. Leucine is already in every protein shake; whether its mitochondrial effects are therapeutically meaningful in humans requires the next phase of work.
