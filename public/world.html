The Anchor · The Energy War’s Second Day — What It Means That Both the Houthi Campaign and the Hormuz Mine Campaign Are Continuing 24 Hours After Wednesday’s Dual-Front Opening

Wednesday’s dual energy shock — the Houthi strike on Ras Tanura and Iran’s Hormuz mine campaign — was an opening, not a conclusion. Thursday’s events confirm both fronts are continuing. The Saudi coalition’s interception of two Houthi ballistic missiles targeting Riyadh on Thursday shows that Wednesday’s Ras Tanura strike did not exhaust the Houthi order-of-battle — the campaign has the resources to sustain daily attacks. The IRGC navy’s report of two tankers striking mines in the Strait of Hormuz confirms the mine campaign is not a one-day event: the mines are still in place and still being encountered. Two simultaneous Iranian-aligned campaigns — Houthi conventional strikes on Saudi infrastructure from Yemen, IRGC mine-laying in the Strait — running on consecutive days suggests a coordinated escalation cycle rather than independent opportunistic attacks. The oil market is pricing this correctly: Brent above $91 on a confirmed second day of dual-front activity. The escalation cycle’s next variable is Saudi and US response. Wednesday’s intercepts and Thursday’s intercepts demonstrate that Saudi air defenses are functioning but not stopping the campaign. At what point the United States decides to intervene directly — rather than providing defensive support to Saudi Arabia — is the threshold that the market is watching without being able to price it precisely yet.

A Saudi-led coalition air defense network intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles launched toward Riyadh on Thursday, the second consecutive day of Houthi attacks on Saudi targets following Wednesday’s drone and missile strike on the Ras Tanura oil refinery and Riyadh’s financial district, with the interceptions confirming that Saudi air defenses are actively engaged but that the Houthi campaign has not exhausted its strike capacity; the IRGC navy separately reported Thursday that two oil tankers caught fire and took on water after striking naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz while traveling an “unauthorized route” — the second day of confirmed mine-related incidents in the strait since Iran’s mine campaign was disclosed Wednesday; and the dual-front campaign — Houthi conventional attacks on Saudi infrastructure, IRGC mines blocking the strait — sustained Brent crude above $91 and WTI above $82 into Thursday’s closed US market session, with energy commodity markets pricing a second consecutive day of confirmed dual-front supply-chain disruption.

Two intercepted Houthi ballistic missiles targeting Riyadh is a different category of attack than Wednesday’s drone-and-cruise-missile combination at Ras Tanura. Ballistic missiles require more sophisticated interception capabilities than drones — they travel faster and at higher altitude. The Saudi coalition’s successful interceptions on two consecutive days demonstrate functioning advanced air defense, but successful interceptions do not eliminate the threat: each intercept is a data point about Saudi defense capacity, not a deterrent to future launches.

A Thanksgiving meeting between the US president and the NATO Secretary General is not routine. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Thursday that Ukraine is waiting for the United States to determine a date for a new round of peace talks with Russia, confirming that the diplomatic framework for negotiations exists but that Washington — not Kyiv — holds the authority to set the timeline; Zelenskyy’s statement, delivered as Trump and NATO Secretary General Rutte met at Mar-a-Lago, positions Ukraine as ready to engage while placing the scheduling responsibility with its primary backer — a posture that protects Kyiv from accusations of blocking negotiations while maintaining leverage over the terms; and the confirmation that a framework exists represents meaningful progress from earlier in November, when the conditions for any talks were still contested, with both the United States and Ukraine insisting on sovereignty and territorial integrity guarantees that Russia has publicly rejected.

Zelenskyy saying Ukraine is waiting for Washington to set the date is a carefully calibrated diplomatic statement. It signals readiness without concession: Ukraine is not resisting talks, the United States is in control of the schedule, and the framework — whatever its terms — is sufficiently acceptable to Kyiv that Zelenskyy is publicly deferring to Washington’s timing. What the framework actually says about territory and security guarantees is the detail that will determine whether negotiations can begin rather than merely be announced.

Taiwan’s rapid adoption of Meta’s Threads platform has made the network a primary vector for Chinese state-linked influence operations ahead of Taiwan’s local elections, with disinformation researchers documenting coordinated account networks amplifying pro-Beijing narratives — including content questioning Taiwan’s defense capabilities, amplifying economic anxieties, and promoting candidates sympathetic to cross-strait reconciliation — at scale across the Threads ecosystem; the Threads-Taiwan dynamic represents a shift from previous Chinese information operations on the island, which primarily used Facebook and LINE, toward newer platforms where content moderation systems are still developing and enforcement capacity is lower; and Taiwan’s government information security agencies said they have identified multiple coordinated inauthentic behavior networks operating on Threads and have requested Meta take enforcement action, with no confirmed response timeline from Meta at the time of reporting.

Threads is a newer platform in Taiwan’s information ecosystem, which is precisely why it is attractive for influence operations: the platform’s content moderation infrastructure is less mature than Facebook’s, coordination detection algorithms have less training data on Taiwanese influence-operation patterns, and Taiwan-specific researchers have had less time to map the network’s actor landscape. The timing — local elections approaching — is consistent with China’s documented practice of intensifying information operations in the months before Taiwan’s electoral events.
