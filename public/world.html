The Anchor · Trump’s Iran Decision — Three Paths, One Choice, No Timeline

Trump has three Iran options, all three are actively on the table, and his advisers are arguing for all three simultaneously. The strikes camp — led by hawkish voices in the NSC — argues the IRGC drone downing constitutes a direct provocation that requires a kinetic response to maintain deterrence; the strangulation camp argues secondary sanctions and naval pressure can achieve the same strategic outcome at a fraction of the escalation risk; the deal camp argues Iran’s 7-demand proposal via Qatar, however maximalist, signals a willingness to negotiate that a kinetic response would permanently foreclose. What Trump will decide is not legible from the outside. What is legible is that oil markets on Friday are pricing “deal more likely than strikes” — Brent fell 3.1% to $93 — and that assessment is a bet on the deal camp winning an argument that is visibly not yet won.

By the Numbers
7
Formal preconditions submitted by Iran to the Trump administration via Qatar’s diplomatic channel, in response to US overtures toward an interim nuclear deal. The 7 demands include: (1) comprehensive sanctions relief, (2) release of frozen Iranian assets estimated at $65–80 billion, (3) end of the US naval blockade of Iranian oil exports, (4) full US military withdrawal from the Persian Gulf region, (5) withdrawal of all DOJ prosecutions of Iranian nationals and Iranian-linked entities, (6) guaranteed right to continue uranium enrichment at current capacities, and (7) legally binding no-first-strike commitments. All seven are non-negotiable in Iran’s stated position, though analysts note that maximalist opening positions are standard for Iranian negotiations — the signal is not the specific demands but the existence of a formal demand list.
